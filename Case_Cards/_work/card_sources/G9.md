## Indications
- **Every glaucoma case, both eyes:** open or closed angle, early narrow angles, extent of closure. In open-angle glaucoma, look for angle recession, pseudoexfoliation, pigment and new vessels.
- **Yearly in open-angle glaucoma:** the lens thickens with age, so angle closure may develop.
- **Also:** iridotomy patency; new vessels in central retinal vein occlusion (undilated); tumour, foreign body, blood, Kayser–Fleischer ring.

**Keywords the examiner listens for:** total internal reflection · Goldmann lens · Zeiss four-mirror · indentation · scleral spur · Schwalbe's line · Shaffer grade · occludable angle · peripheral anterior synechiae · angle recession

## Instrument check
@widths 30 36 34
| Lens | Contact and coupling | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Goldmann** single-, two- or three-mirror (indirect; angle mirror smallest, dome-shaped) | 12 mm; steeper than the cornea, so it needs **viscous coupling fluid** | Grading without pressure; steadies the globe |
| **Zeiss four-mirror** (also Posner, Sussman) | 9 mm; **no coupling fluid** (tears bridge the gap) | Whole angle quickly; **indentation** |
| **Koeppe** (direct) | Supine; hand-held microscope | Infants under general anaesthesia |

Also ready: anaesthetic drops, disinfectant, anhydrous glycerin for corneal oedema.

## Steps
@widths 48 52
| Step | Why — the viva answer |
|---|---|
| 1. **Order:** Van Herick, then **applanation tonometry**, then gonioscopy, before dilatation. Defer if the cornea is oedematous | Gonioscopy lowers the pressure, so later tonometry reads falsely low. Dilating a narrow angle can cause closure. Oedema hides the angle |
| 2. **Introduce yourself, explain, take consent:** "A numbing drop, then a lens on your eye. It does not hurt." | Examiners tick it. A calm patient keeps still |
| 3. **Disinfect the lens** before and after each patient | It touches every patient's cornea |
| 4. **Topical anaesthetic drop** | Lens contact is painless |
| 5. **Instruct:** chin on the rest, forehead on the band. "Keep both eyes open; look where I ask." | For a narrow angle, the patient looks **towards the mirror** in use |
| 6. **Semi-dark room;** slit beam 2 mm wide, at a right angle to the mirror | Keeps the examiner's eyes partly dark-adapted |
| 7. Coupling fluid in the Goldmann cup; place the lens gently | The lens curve is steeper than the cornea |
| 8. **Grade before any pressure** | Pressure distorts the angle and opens an appositional closure |
| 9. **Rotate through 360°.** The mirror at 12 o'clock shows the **inferior** angle | Each mirror shows a mirror image of the **opposite** angle |
| 10. From the iris root forwards: ciliary body band, **scleral spur**, trabecular meshwork, **Schwalbe's line** | The deepest structure seen grades the angle |
| 11. **Indentation** with the Zeiss lens | Aqueous pushes the iris back: **apposition opens, synechiae stay closed** |
| 12. Note iris contour, pigment, synechiae, new vessels, angle recession, pseudoexfoliation material | Secondary causes change the diagnosis |

## How to record the result
One cross each for the right eye (RE) and left eye (LE): in each quadrant, the **deepest structure seen**. Use scleral spur (SS), ciliary body band (CBB), trabecular meshwork (TM), Schwalbe's line (SL) or "Closed". Mark a closed quadrant "appositional" (opens on indentation) or "peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS)".

:::gonio
RE | S=SS | T=SS | I=SS | N=SS
LE | S=TM | T=SS | I=SS | N=SS
caption: Deepest structure seen — right eye open all round; left eye narrower superiorly (pigmented TM only); neither occludable
:::

Written: "RE: scleral spur in all quadrants, open angles. LE: pigmented TM superiorly (Shaffer grade 2), scleral spur elsewhere. No PAS or new vessels."

Your department writes a grade in each quadrant "by modified Shaffer's grading" (III in all four on its primary open-angle glaucoma sheet). Confirm with your seniors which structure each grade stands for, and write the structure too.

## Normal values and interpretation
@widths 24 76
| Structure | How to recognise it |
|---|---|
| Ciliary body band | Pink, dull brown or slate-grey band behind the spur; wider in myopes, narrower in hypermetropes |
| Scleral spur | Narrow **white** line; the longitudinal ciliary muscle and iris processes attach here |
| Trabecular meshwork | The posterior part is **pigmented**: the main outflow site. Schlemm's canal may show as a darker line. Blood fills it under lens pressure, raised episcleral venous pressure or hypotony |
| Schwalbe's line | Most anterior; whitish or pigmented; the end of Descemet's membrane |

@widths 28 12 36 24
| Shaffer | Width | Structures seen | Closure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4** wide open | 35–40° | Up to ciliary body | Impossible |
| **3** open | 25–35° | Up to ciliary body | Impossible |
| **2** moderately narrow | 20° | Pigmented TM | Possible, unlikely |
| **1** extremely narrow | 10° | Only SL ± non-pigmented TM | Possible |
| **S** slit | < 10° | None; no obvious contact | Parts look closed |
| **0** closed | — | Iridocorneal contact | Complete |

@widths 14 86
| System | Grades |
|---|---|
| Scheie | **Wide open** all seen · narrow **1** iris root hard to see · **2** CBB obscured · **3** posterior TM obscured · **4** only SL |
| Spaeth | **Four** parameters: **insertion** A anterior to TM, B at TM, C at SS, D deep to SS, E into ciliary body · **width** 10°/20°/30°/40° · **contour** S steep, R regular, Q queer (deeply concave) · **TM pigment** 0–4 |

**Van Herick** (peripheral depth ÷ corneal thickness): **4** ≥ 1 · **3** ¼–½ · **2** ¼ · **1** < ¼. Grade 1 may be occludable.

## Common errors
@widths 45 55
| Error | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pressure while grading | Apposition opens: the angle looks **wider** |
| Charting the mirror's own quadrant | **Wrong quadrant** |
| Pigmented SL or Sampaolesi's line taken for TM | Grade 1 called 2: looks **wider** |
| Synechial closure called without indentation | **PAS over-called** |
| Iris processes taken for PAS | **PAS over-called** |
| Not comparing the fellow eye | **Recession missed** |

## Viva questions
Q: What is gonioscopy, and why do you need a lens?
A: **Gonioscopy** examines the anterior chamber angle. Light from the angle undergoes **total internal reflection** at the tear–air interface. The lens replaces that interface with one of higher refractive index.
Q: Why grade with a Goldmann lens and indent with a Zeiss?
A: The **Goldmann lens** avoids accidental pressure that distorts the angle. **Indentation** with the **Zeiss four-mirror** shows the extent of PAS. In plateau iris it shows a double hump: the iris drapes over the ciliary processes.
Q: Is this angle occludable? How do you stage angle closure?
A: An **occludable angle** shows no pigmented meshwork (**Shaffer grade** 1 or 0) without indentation in at least 3 quadrants. By the ISGEO classification, iridotrabecular contact in 3 or more quadrants (almost 270°), with normal IOP and no PAS, is a primary angle-closure suspect. Raised IOP and/or PAS make it primary angle closure; optic neuropathy makes it primary angle-closure glaucoma.
Q: Direct versus indirect gonioscopy?
A: Direct: erect view, sees over a convex iris; slow, supine. Indirect: quick, slit lamp, allows compression; orientation confusing.
Q: Iris processes versus peripheral anterior synechiae?
A: **Iris processes** are lacy and fenestrated, with structures visible between. **Peripheral anterior synechiae** are solid and hide the angle.
Q: Causes of PAS?
A: Primary angle closure, anterior uveitis, iridocorneal endothelial syndrome, after intraocular surgery, trauma.
Q: Normal versus new vessels in the angle?
A: **Normal vessels** are broad and stop short of the spur. New vessels are fine, cross the **scleral spur** and arborise on the meshwork.
Q: What is Sampaolesi's line?
A: **Sampaolesi's line** is a dark, scalloped, granular, discontinuous pigment band on or in front of **Schwalbe's line**; meshwork pigment is fine and continuous. Seen in pseudoexfoliation, pigment dispersion and chronic inflammation.
Q: Causes of meshwork pigmentation?
A: Pigment dispersion, pseudoexfoliation, trauma, laser iridotomy, acute angle closure, anterior uveitis.
Q: Gonioscopy after blunt trauma?
A: **Angle recession**: a wide ciliary body band and a prominent spur; compare the other eye. Also cyclodialysis (ciliary body off the spur, hypotony), foreign body, blood.
Q: Gonioscopy versus imaging?
A: Gonioscopy is the gold standard: it tells PAS from apposition. Optical coherence tomography and ultrasound biomicroscopy complement it.
