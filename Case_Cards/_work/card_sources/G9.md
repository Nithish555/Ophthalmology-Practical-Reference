## Indications
- **Every glaucoma case, both eyes:** open or closed angle; early narrow angles; extent of closure; secondary causes in open-angle glaucoma (angle recession, pseudoexfoliation, pigment, new vessels).
- **Every year in open-angle glaucoma:** the lens thickens with age, so an angle-closure component may appear.
- **After treatment:** iridotomy patency, trabeculectomy ostium, cyclodialysis.
- **Other:** new vessels (undilated, in vein occlusion), tumour, cyst, foreign body, blood, Kayser–Fleischer ring.
- **Therapeutic:** indentation can break an acute attack.
- Optical gonioscopy is the **gold standard**; anterior segment optical coherence tomography and ultrasound biomicroscopy only complement it.

**Keywords the examiner listens for:** total internal reflection · Goldmann lens · Zeiss four-mirror · indentation gonioscopy · scleral spur · Schwalbe's line · Shaffer grade · occludable angle · peripheral anterior synechiae · angle recession

## Instrument check
@widths 30 36 34
| Lens | Contact and coupling | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Goldmann** two- or three-mirror (indirect; angle mirror smallest, dome-shaped) | 12 mm; steeper than the cornea, so needs a **viscous coupling fluid** | Grading without pressure; steadies the globe; laser trabeculoplasty |
| **Zeiss four-mirror** (Posner on a rod; Sussman hand-held) | 9 mm; **no coupling fluid** (tears bridge the gap) | Whole angle quickly; **indentation**. Does not steady the eye |
| **Koeppe** (direct) | Patient supine; hand-held microscope | Infants under general anaesthesia; erect view |

Also: slit lamp, topical anaesthetic, coupling fluid, disinfectant; anhydrous glycerin if the cornea is oedematous.

## Steps
@widths 48 52
| Step | Why — the viva answer |
|---|---|
| 1. **Order:** Van Herick, then **IOP by applanation**, then gonioscopy, before any dilatation | Van Herick is the first guide. Gonioscopy lowers IOP, so tonometry after it reads falsely low. Dilating a narrow angle can bring on closure |
| 2. **Defer** if the cornea is oedematous (acute attack), or clear it first with glycerin | Oedema hides the angle |
| 3. **Introduce yourself, explain, take consent:** "A numbing drop, then a lens rests on your eye for a few minutes. It does not hurt." | Examiners tick it. A calm patient keeps still |
| 4. **Disinfect the lens** before use | It touches the cornea of every patient |
| 5. **Topical anaesthetic drop** in both eyes | Lens contact is painless |
| 6. **Instruct:** chin on the rest, forehead on the band. "Keep both eyes open and look straight ahead, or where I ask." | To see into a narrow angle, the patient looks **towards the mirror** in use |
| 7. **Semi-dark room.** Slit beam 2 mm wide, at a right angle to the mirror | Dark adaptation helps the examiner see |
| 8. Fill the Goldmann cup with coupling fluid; place the lens gently on the cornea | The lens is steeper than the cornea; the fluid fills the gap |
| 9. **Grade before any pressure** | Pressure distorts the angle and opens an appositional closure |
| 10. **Rotate through 360°.** Each mirror shows the **opposite angle**: at 12 o'clock it shows the inferior angle | The image is a mirror image of the opposite angle, so chart the opposite quadrant |
| 11. Name structures from the iris root forwards: ciliary body band, **scleral spur**, trabecular meshwork, **Schwalbe's line** | The deepest structure seen grades the angle |
| 12. **Indentation** with the Zeiss lens: press gently | Aqueous is forced into the angle and pushes the iris back. **Apposition opens; synechiae stay closed** |
| 13. Note iris contour, pigment, synechiae, new vessels, recession, exfoliation material, iridotomy | Secondary causes change the diagnosis |
| 14. Remove the lens and **disinfect it again** | Protects the next patient |

## How to record the result
One cross per eye. In each quadrant write the **deepest structure seen**: scleral spur (SS), ciliary body band (CBB), trabecular meshwork (TM), Schwalbe's line (SL) or "Closed".

:::gonio
RE | S=SS | T=SS | I=SS | N=SS
LE | S=TM | T=SS | I=SS | N=SS
caption: Deepest structure seen in each quadrant — right eye open in all quadrants; left eye open, superior quadrant narrower (pigmented TM only); neither eye occludable
:::

Written: "Gonioscopy (Goldmann lens): right eye (RE) scleral spur seen in all four quadrants, open angles. Left eye (LE): pigmented TM only superiorly, scleral spur elsewhere. No peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS) or new vessels." A closed quadrant is "appositional" or "PAS".

Your department writes a grade in each quadrant "by modified Shaffer's grading" (III in all four on its POAG sheet); confirm with your seniors which structure each grade stands for, and write the structure too.

## Normal values and interpretation
@widths 24 76
| Structure | How to recognise it |
|---|---|
| Ciliary body band | Pink, dull brown or slate-grey band behind the spur. Narrower in hypermetropes, wider in myopes |
| Scleral spur | Narrow **white** line; attachment of the longitudinal ciliary muscle. Iris processes insert here |
| Trabecular meshwork | Band about 600 µm wide. The posterior part is **pigmented** and is the main outflow site. Schlemm's canal may show as a darker line, sometimes with blood |
| Schwalbe's line | Most anterior; whitish or pigmented; the end of Descemet's membrane |

@widths 28 12 36 24
| Shaffer | Width | Structures seen | Closure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4** wide open | 35–40° | Up to ciliary body | Impossible |
| **3** open | 25–35° | Up to ciliary body | Impossible |
| **2** moderately narrow | 20° | Pigmented TM | Possible, unlikely |
| **1** extremely narrow | 10° | Only SL ± non-pigmented TM | Possible |
| **S** slit | < 10° | None; no obvious contact | Parts look closed |
| **0** closed | — | Iridocorneal contact | Complete |

**Occludable angle:** pigmented TM not seen (Shaffer 1 or 0) without indentation in **at least three quadrants**.

@widths 14 86
| System | Grades |
|---|---|
| Scheie | **Wide open** all seen · narrow **1** iris root hard to see · **2** CBB obscured · **3** posterior TM obscured · **4** only SL |
| Spaeth | **Four** parameters: **insertion** A anterior to TM, B at TM, C at SS, D deep to SS, E into ciliary body · **width** 10°/20°/30°/40° · **contour** S steep, R regular, Q queer (deeply concave) · **TM pigment** 0–4 |

**Van Herick** (peripheral depth ÷ corneal thickness): **4** ≥ 1 · **3** ¼–½ · **2** ¼ · **1** < ¼, may be occludable · **0** contact.

## Common errors
@widths 45 55
| Error | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pressure while grading | Apposition opens: a closing angle looks **open** |
| Charting the mirror's own quadrant | Findings put in the **wrong quadrant** |
| Pigmented SL or Sampaolesi's line taken for pigmented TM | Grade 1 called grade 2: angle looks **wider** |
| Closed angle called synechial without indentation | **PAS over-called** |
| Iris processes taken for PAS | **PAS over-called** |
| Gonioscopy before tonometry | IOP reads falsely **low** |
| Not comparing with the fellow eye | **Angle recession missed** |

## Viva questions
Q: Why can't you see the angle without a lens?
A: Light from the angle undergoes **total internal reflection** at the tear–air interface. A goniolens replaces it with an interface of higher refractive index; an indirect mirror sends rays out below the critical angle.
Q: Direct versus indirect gonioscopy?
A: Direct: erect view, simple orientation, sees over a convex iris; slow, supine, special equipment. Indirect: quick, at the slit lamp, allows compression; orientation confusing, harder in narrow angles.
Q: Why start with a **Goldmann lens** and indent with a Zeiss?
A: The Goldmann lens avoids accidental pressure on the cornea, so the angle is not distorted. The small **Zeiss four-mirror** lens indents easily.
Q: What else does **indentation gonioscopy** show?
A: The extent of PAS, and the double-hump sign of plateau iris. Indenting the central cornea can also open the angle in an acute attack.
Q: Iris processes versus peripheral anterior synechiae?
A: Processes are lacy and fenestrated; structures show between them. **Peripheral anterior synechiae** are solid and hide the angle.
Q: Causes of PAS?
A: Primary angle-closure glaucoma, anterior uveitis, iridocorneal endothelial syndrome, after intraocular surgery, and trauma.
Q: Normal versus new vessels in the angle?
A: Normal vessels are broad and stop at the spur. New vessels are fine, cross the **scleral spur** and arborise on the meshwork.
Q: What is Sampaolesi's line?
A: A dark, dense, scalloped pigment band on or in front of **Schwalbe's line**: salt-and-pepper, granular, discontinuous. Seen in pseudoexfoliation, pigment dispersion and chronic inflammation.
Q: Causes of meshwork pigmentation?
A: Pigment dispersion, pseudoexfoliation, trauma, after laser iridotomy, after acute angle closure, anterior uveitis. Pigmentary glaucoma: wide-open angle, dense dark-brown pigment all round.
Q: Gonioscopy after blunt trauma?
A: **Angle recession** (wide ciliary body band, iris set back; compare the other eye), trabecular dialysis, cyclodialysis (ciliary body off the spur, causing hypotony), foreign body, blood.
Q: Why are miotics ineffective in angle-recession glaucoma?
A: The meshwork is scarred. Prostaglandin analogues are the drug of choice.
Q: Causes of blood in Schlemm's canal?
A: Raised episcleral venous pressure (carotid-cavernous fistula, Sturge–Weber syndrome, superior vena cava obstruction), hypotony, after trabeculectomy, or simply after gonioscopy.
Q: Gonioscopy in angle closure?
A: Acute or subacute: an **occludable angle**. Chronic: PAS appear late. The **Shaffer grade** documents it.
Q: Gonioscopy versus imaging?
A: Gonioscopy is the gold standard: it shows PAS and tells them from apposition. Optical coherence tomography and ultrasound biomicroscopy complement it.
Q: *(extra)* Gonioscopy in congenital glaucoma?
A: Direct lens: compacted meshwork, high iris insertion, iris-root vessel loops (Loch Ness monster), fluffy peripheral iris tissue (Lister's morning mist).

**Mnemonic:** angle structures: "I CAN SEE TILL SCHWALBE'S LINE" · Goldmann three-mirror: "The smallest the lens, the more peripheral the view".
