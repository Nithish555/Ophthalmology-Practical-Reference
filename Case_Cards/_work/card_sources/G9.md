## Indications
- **Every glaucoma case, both eyes**: open or closed angle; **early narrow angles**; extent of closure; **iridotomy patency**.
- **Secondary glaucomas**: **angle recession**, **new vessels**, **pseudoexfoliation**, pigment. Also tumour, foreign body, blood, **Kayser–Fleischer ring**.
- Therapeutic: indentation can **break an acute angle-closure attack**.

## Instrument check
- **Indirect** (slit lamp): mirror shows the **opposite** angle.
  - **Goldmann** three-mirror: angle mirror **smallest, dome-shaped**; contact **12 mm**, steeper than cornea → **viscous coupling agent**; steadies the globe.
  - **Four-mirror** (**Zeiss**, **Posner**, **Sussman**): contact **9 mm**, **no coupling agent**; whole angle at once; **indentation**.
- **Direct** (Koeppe): supine, usually under general anaesthesia (infantile glaucoma).

## Steps
1. **First**: Van Herick, then **intraocular pressure**. Corneal oedema (acute attack): **defer**.
2. **Consent**: introduce yourself, explain, take consent.
3. **Instruction**: "Numbing drops, then a lens on your eye; keep still, look where I ask."
4. **Topical anaesthetic drops**.
5. **Disinfect** the lens.
6. **Dim the room**. Slit beam **2 mm** wide, perpendicular to the mirror.
7. Place the lens gently; **grade before any pressure**.
8. Rotate **360°**. Mirror at 12 o'clock: **inferior** angle. Narrow angle: patient **looks towards the mirror**.
9. **Iris root forwards**: **ciliary body band (CBB)** → **scleral spur (SS)**, white → **trabecular meshwork (TM)**, posterior **pigmented** (main outflow), anterior non-pigmented → **Schwalbe's line (SL)**.
10. **Indentation** (four-mirror): press gently. **Appositional** closure opens; **peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS)** stay closed.
11. **Note**: iris contour, TM pigment, PAS extent, new vessels, angle recession, pseudoexfoliation, blood.

## How to record the result
One cross per eye, right eye (RE) and left eye (LE); each quadrant: **deepest structure seen** (SS, CBB, TM, SL or "Closed").

:::gonio
RE | S=SS | T=SS | I=SS | N=SS
LE | S=TM | T=SS | I=SS | N=SS
caption: Deepest structure seen in each quadrant — RE open angles in all quadrants; LE open, superior quadrant narrower (pigmented TM only); neither eye occludable
:::

Written: "RE SS all round: open angles. LE superior pigmented TM only, rest SS. No PAS or new vessels." Closed quadrant: "appositional" or "PAS".

Your department writes a grade in each quadrant "by modified Shaffer's grading" (III in all four on its primary open-angle glaucoma sheet); confirm with your seniors which structure each grade stands for, and write the structure too.

## Common errors
- **Pressure** while grading opens **appositional** closure.
- Charting the mirror's quadrant, not the **opposite** one.
- Pigmented SL or **Sampaolesi's line** taken for pigmented TM (grade 1 called 2).
- Closed angle called synechial without **indentation**.
- Iris processes taken for PAS.

## Viva questions
Q: Why a goniolens, and its principle?
A: **Total internal reflection** at the tear–air interface hides the angle; the lens replaces that interface with one of **higher refractive index**. An indirect mirror sends rays out **below the critical angle**.
Q: Direct versus indirect?
A: Direct: erect view, simple orientation, sees over a convex iris; supine, slow. Indirect: quick, allows compression; orientation confusing.
Q: Indentation (dynamic) gonioscopy?
A: Pressing a four-mirror lens: **appositional** closure opens, **synechial** stays; shows PAS extent.
Q: What is the scleral spur?
A: Posterior lip of the scleral sulcus; attachment of the **longitudinal ciliary muscle**.
Q: Iris processes versus PAS?
A: Processes **lacy, fenestrated**, angle seen between; PAS **solid**, hide it.
Q: New versus normal angle vessels?
A: New: **fine**, **cross the SS**, **arborise** on TM. Normal: broad, do neither.
Q: Sampaolesi's line versus pigmented TM?
A: **Salt-and-pepper**, dark, granular, discontinuous versus **"brown sugar"**, fine, continuous.
Q: Gonioscopy after trauma?
A: **Angle recession**, trabecular dialysis, cyclodialysis, foreign body, blood.

**Mnemonic:** angle structures: "I CAN SEE TILL SCHWALBE'S LINE" · Goldmann three-mirror: "The smallest the lens, the more peripheral the view".
