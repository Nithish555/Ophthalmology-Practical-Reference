## The instruction you may get
- "Examine the fundus of this glaucoma patient and draw it."

## Spot
- **Flame-shaped and dot-blot haemorrhages** radiating from the disc, with **dilated, tortuous veins**: four quadrants (**"blood and thunder"**) in central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO).
- **Hemiretinal vein occlusion** *(Kanski — not in your practical books)*: regarded by some as a CRVO variant, ischaemic or non-ischaemic, occluding the **superior or inferior branch** of the central retinal vein: **hemispheric** (a major branch at or near the disc) or **hemicentral** (one trunk of a dual-trunked central retinal vein). Branch-occlusion picture in that **hemisphere**. Neovascularisation of the iris: commoner than in branch, rarer than in central occlusion. Extensive ischaemia → manage as **ischaemic CRVO**.
- **Glaucomatous cupping**, same or fellow eye.
- Old eye: **optociliary shunts**, **sheathed veins**, **laser scars**, **epiretinal membrane**.

## Focused examination
1. **Undilated, both eyes: relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD)**: unreliable with **glaucomatous discs** or miotic pupils.
2. **Pupillary margin: neovascularisation of the iris (NVI)**, in clock hours.
3. **Intraocular pressure (IOP)**: over **22 mm Hg** needs a cause: open-angle glaucoma, neovascular glaucoma (NVG), angle closure.
4. **Undilated gonioscopy: neovascularisation of the angle (NVA)**, without NVI in **12%**; occludable angle.
5. **Lens**: nuclear sclerosis grade.
6. **Dilate. Distant direct ophthalmoscopy**: red glow; **media**.
7. **Disc**: size, shape, colour, margins, **cup–disc ratio**, rim; **glaucomatous cupping**; swelling or pallor; shunts or **new vessels on the disc (NVD)**.
8. **Vessels**: dilated, tortuous veins, all quadrants or one hemisphere; sheathing.
9. **Background**: each lesion by colour, shape, size in disc diameters, margins, number, location → "suggestive of…": flame (superficial) and dot-blot (deep) haemorrhages, cotton-wool spots.
10. **Macula**: foveal reflex, oedema, epiretinal membrane.
11. **Periphery, indirect ophthalmoscope**: **new vessels elsewhere (NVE)**.
12. **"Confirmed with a 90 D (dioptre) lens"**: tells shunts from NVD.
13. **Draw** both fundi: red haemorrhages, blue veins, yellow sclerosed vessels, black laser scars, green epiretinal membrane.

## Say-it description
:::say
Mr X, a 65-year-old hypertensive man with primary open-angle glaucoma on drops, has sudden painless defective vision in the right eye for two months.

Vision: right eye 6/36, no better with pinhole; left eye 6/9. Non-contact tonometry: 18 and 16 millimetres of mercury.

Both eyes: conjunctiva, cornea, anterior chamber and iris normal, no new vessels; no relative afferent pupillary defect; grade 2 nuclear sclerosis; undilated gonioscopy: open angles, no new vessels.

Right fundus: good red glow, clear media. Disc vertically oval, pink, well-defined margins; cup–disc ratio 0.7, inferior rim thinning, nasalisation, suggestive of glaucomatous cupping. Superior veins dilated, tortuous. Multiple red flame-shaped lesions, a quarter to half a disc diameter, ill-defined margins, with red dots and blots, radiating from the disc into the superior hemisphere only, suggestive of superficial and deep retinal haemorrhages; no cotton-wool spots. Foveal reflex absent, retina thickened, suggestive of macular oedema. Indirect ophthalmoscopy: no peripheral new vessels. Confirmed with a 90 dioptre lens.

Left fundus: cup–disc ratio 0.8, inferior rim thinning, suggestive of glaucomatous cupping.

Right eye clinically **non-ischaemic hemi-central retinal vein occlusion** involving the superior hemisphere, with macular oedema, in a known case of primary open-angle glaucoma in both eyes, intraocular pressure under control on medical treatment; both eyes grade 2 nuclear sclerosis.
:::

## Differentials
@widths 28 72
| Condition | Distinguishing point |
|---|---|
| Branch retinal vein occlusion | Collaterals cross the **horizontal raphe** (central: **at the disc**); more **retinal** than iris new vessels |
| Ocular ischaemic syndrome | Veins **not tortuous**; **mid-peripheral** haemorrhages; normal disc; carotid **stenosis** |
| Diabetic retinopathy | **Always bilateral**; **beaded** veins; **hard exudates** |

## Must know
- **Types (Central Vein Occlusion Study, CVOS)**: ischaemic; non-ischaemic, about **75%** (**34%** turn ischaemic by **3 years**); **indeterminate** (haemorrhage hides perfusion).

@widths 30 32 38
| Feature | Non-ischaemic | Ischaemic |
|---|---|---|
| Vision | Moderate to severe loss | **Counting fingers or worse** |
| RAPD | Absent or mild | **> 0.7 log unit** |
| Fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) non-perfusion | **< 10 disc areas** | **> 10 disc areas** |
| Electroretinogram (ERG) | Normal or mildly depressed | **b-wave < 60% of normal; b-to-a ratio < 1** |

- **Grid laser**: helps branch occlusion, not central (diffuse leakage involving the fovea).
- **Glaucoma both ways**: open-angle glaucoma heads the ocular associations. CRVO raises IOP by NVG, **steroid-induced glaucoma** or angle closure (cause or effect).
- **"100-day glaucoma"**: NVI usually at **2–4 months**; ischaemic: **NVI 49%, NVG 29%** within **6 months**; **one-third** with NVI get NVG. Significant: **NVI over 2 clock hours**, or **any NVA**.
- **Investigations**: FFA once haemorrhages clear (about **3 months**); optical coherence tomography (OCT); blood pressure, fasting blood glucose, lipid profile; hypercoagulable screen if under 60.
- **Macular oedema**: intravitreal **anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF)** first (**ranibizumab**, **aflibercept**, **bevacizumab**); steroids (**triamcinolone**, **dexamethasone implant**) cause cataract and **raise IOP**.
- **New vessels**: **prompt panretinal photocoagulation (PRP)**; **not prophylactic** unless ischaemic with follow-up impossible; anti-VEGF only temporises.
- **IOP and follow-up**: antiglaucoma drugs, then trabeculectomy or drainage valve. Review **monthly for 6 months**, then tapered (CVOS), with undilated gonioscopy each visit. See G1 and G5.

## Viva questions
Q: Why does glaucoma predispose to CRVO?
A: Retinal venous pressure must exceed IOP, so raised IOP causes stasis (Virchow's triad); the backward-bowed lamina cribrosa and nasalisation compress the vein.
Q: What will you look for at every follow-up visit?
A: Vision, RAPD (conversion to ischaemic), IOP, undilated NVI and NVA, NVD, NVE, macular oedema (OCT), fellow eye.
Q: Why is RAPD unreliable here?
A: Needs normal discs, pupils and fellow eye; ERG does not (b-wave: **80%** sensitivity and specificity).
Q: Why call it non-ischaemic?
A: Vision better than counting fingers, no RAPD, no cotton-wool spots; FFA confirms (under 10 disc areas).
Q: Why not prophylactic PRP?
A: CVOS: no prevention of NVI or NVA; later PRP regressed them better in untreated eyes (**56%** versus **22%**).
Q: Why is NVI commoner than NVD?
A: Retina too ischaemic to form new vessels; anterior segment capillaries stay viable.
Q: Shunt or NVD?
A: Shunts drain retinal blood to the choroid (good compensation, old occlusion); NVD are finer, forming a network.
Q: Ischaemic index?
A: Non-perfused ÷ total retinal area; over 50% (about 10 disc areas): significant neovascular risk.
Q: Commonest cause of visual loss?
A: Macular oedema.
Q: Commonest field defect?
A: Central scotoma.
Q: Why gonioscopy in the fellow eye?
A: An occludable angle, as the occlusion may follow angle closure.
