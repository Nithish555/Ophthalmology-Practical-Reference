## At a glance
- **Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG)** with a **glaucoma drainage device (GDD)**: a tube in the anterior chamber (AC) drains aqueous to a plate at the equator, usually after a **failed trabeculectomy**.
- On the department checklist: stable patient, visible signs, long surgical viva.
- Examiners test the **tube**, **plate quadrant**, **motility**, intraocular pressure (IOP) control, device types, surgery, complications.

## Case format — history
Mr X, a 62-year-old man, has come for regular follow-up; his right eye (RE) had a drainage device 3 years ago, after a failed trabeculectomy.

### Special tests
**Slit-lamp examination of the tube**
1. Diffuse light: entry site and clock hour.
2. Slit beam: length, bevel, course, gap from endothelium and iris, any touch.
3. Tip: open, or blocked by blood, fibrin, iris or vitreous.
Record: as in the table.

**Plate quadrant and patch graft**
1. Patient looks down and away from the plate; lift the upper lid.
2. Bleb over the plate in the house bleb order (height, clarity, extent, location, vascularity, microcysts, fibrosis, sutures); patch graft intact or melted; any exposure.
Record: as in the table.

**Gonioscopy with a tube**
1. IOP first: gonioscopy lowers the next GAT reading.
2. One cross for the RE and one for the left eye (LE), deepest structure per quadrant; note the tube tip, PAS, NVA.

## What you must know
### Definition
- **Glaucoma drainage device (aqueous shunt)**: an **alloplastic tube** leading to a **large equatorial reservoir** that drains aqueous humour from the AC.
- **Parts**: **tube** (AC or pars plana); **plate (explant)** at the equator, with a **ridge** where the tube inserts (keeps the capsule off the tube opening); **valve** if valved; after surgery, a **capsule (bleb)** and a **scleral patch graft**.
- All devices: materials fibroblasts cannot firmly adhere to; equatorial plate; similar tube diameters. For POAG itself, see card G1.

### Classification
**By flow**: **seton** (non-lumened; flow along its surface) · **shunt** (hollow; two-way flow) · **valve** (one-way; needs an activating pressure).

**Named devices: valved vs non-valved**
@widths 24 18 32 26
| Device | Valve | Plate | Tube |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Molteno** (the first) | Non-valved | Circular, polypropylene; single, double or triple | Outer **0.63 mm**, inner **0.3 mm** |
| **Baerveldt** | Non-valved | Silicone | Outer **0.64 mm**, inner **0.3 mm** |
| **Ahmed glaucoma valve** | **Valved** | Oval polypropylene, **184 mm²** (double **364 mm²**) | Silicone; opens above **8–10 mm Hg** |

- Ahmed: two silicone elastomer membranes, **Venturi effect** (Bernoulli's principle): fully open at high pressure, narrowing as pressure falls.
- Non-valved sizing: **larger plates for younger, healthier eyes**; smaller for older, sicker eyes.

### Pathogenesis
- Aqueous runs down the tube onto the plate; a **fibrous capsule** forms around the plate and becomes the bleb.
- **Drainage capacity ∝ capsule surface area; resistance ∝ capsule thickness.**
- Valved or flow-restricted devices work at once; a non-valved tube waits for the capsule.

### Investigations
@widths 30 40 30
| Test | Why | Expected finding |
|---|---|---|
| GAT: time, drops | Low = hypotony; high = blocked tube, thick capsule, hypertensive phase | At target |
| Gonioscopy | Tube tip; PAS (avoid for a tube); NVA (treat first) | Tip free; open angle |
| +90 dioptre (D) lens; indirect ophthalmoscopy | Disc; folds, choroidal effusion, detachment | No hypotony signs |

Corneal oedema: take the IOP as higher than read; repeat GAT once it clears.

### Management
**This case (device working)**: drops to target as for POAG (card G1), but **no miotics or alpha agonists**: they increase vascularisation of the bleb capsule. Every visit: tube, cornea, plate cover, motility, IOP.

**Indications**: refractory glaucomas (uveitic, neovascular, congenital, refractory juvenile, after PKP, aniridia, post-traumatic); **multiple failed trabeculectomies** or failed filtering surgery with antimetabolites; **extensive conjunctival scarring**; failed primary glaucomas.

**Before surgery (contraindications and checks)**
- **Potential for useful vision is a must**; without it, cyclodestruction.
- Check lid position, scleral exposure, tear film, blepharitis, peripheral cornea, vitreous in the AC (may block the tip), cataract (combine), PAS (avoid for the tube), NVA (treat first).
- Pseudophakia, shallow AC, post-PKP, poor endothelium → **pars plana tube**. Stop **pilocarpine or echothiophate** and **warfarin**.

**Key steps**
1. Limbus-based or fornix-based conjunctival flap.
2. **Superotemporal** quadrant (next, inferonasal); avoid superonasal.
3. Plate at the **equator** between two recti, anterior edge **8–10 mm** behind the limbus, sutured to **episclera** (superior rectus insertion if staphyloma or quiescent scleritis).
4. **Non-valved: tie the tube for 2–3 weeks** (**rip cord** of 4-0 chromic catgut; **8-0 polyglactin or 10-0 prolene** released by **argon laser suturolysis**; 5-0 nylon with a spacer). **Valved: one stage.**
5. **AC tube**: **23-gauge needle tract** at the posterior edge of the blue limbus; **bevel up**; **1.5–2 mm** inside, **parallel to the iris**.
6. Or **pars plana tube**: complete vitrectomy; **3–4 mm** behind the limbus; **bevel down**.
7. Full-thickness **scleral patch graft** over the **3–5 mm** of tube exposed to lid contact.
8. Reform the AC with **hyaluronic acid (Healon)**: less hypotony and flat AC.

**After surgery**: a **hypotensive phase** and a **hypertensive phase** (IOP poorly controlled although the device works properly).

**Valved vs non-valved**: valved is one-step; safety and efficacy appear equal, except in Sturge–Weber syndrome, where the **Ahmed valve** gives better long-term control than the Molteno.

**Device vs trabeculectomy**
@widths 26 37 37
| Feature | Drainage device | Trabeculectomy |
|---|---|---|
| Bleb | **Posterior**; organised capsule; **no microcysts** | At the limbus; microcysts |
| IOP fall | **Smaller percentage**; comparable results in neovascular glaucoma | Larger |
| Place in POAG | After failed filtering surgery or scarred conjunctiva | First operation after failed medical treatment |

**Still uncontrolled, or poor visual potential**: **cyclodestruction** (transscleral diode or endolaser cyclophotocoagulation) reduces aqueous production; risks hypotony, phthisis bulbi, retinal detachment; may need repeating.

### Complications
**Intraoperative**: hyphaema; lens or endothelial damage; **scleral perforation** while suturing the plate (gush of vitreous → **cryotherapy** under indirect ophthalmoscopy); hypotony.

@widths 30 38 32
| Complication | Cause | Prevention / action |
|---|---|---|
| Early: hypotony, flat AC, choroidal effusion, suprachoroidal haemorrhage | Excess run-off; **leak around the tube**; high pre-operative IOP | Tight tract; tie; Healon |
| Early: IOP rise before the tie absorbs | Tied tube | Danger to an advanced disc |
| Tube occlusion | Blood, fibrin, iris, vitreous | Full vitrectomy for a pars plana tube |
| Tube migration: forward onto cornea, lens or iris; or extrusion | Hypotony; poor plate fixation | Correct length, parallel to iris; secure plate |
| Graft melt, **erosion** → **endophthalmitis** | Poor lid closure, dry eye; exposure | Patch graft; check lids |
| Raised IOP, open tube | **Thick fibrous capsule**; hypertensive phase | No miotics or alpha agonists |
| Diplopia, strabismus | Bleb displacing globe; fibrosis; superonasal plate (**pseudo-Brown's syndrome**) | Superotemporal plate |

Also: corneal oedema and graft failure (with or without touch); dellen; aqueous misdirection; hypotony maculopathy, chorioretinal folds; cataract; chronic iritis; retinal detachment; early endophthalmitis (contamination at surgery).

### Prognosis and counselling
- IOP falls less than after trabeculectomy; drops may still be needed.
- Report at once: pain, redness, discharge, falling vision (infection through an exposed tube) or double vision.

### Recent advances
- Neovascular glaucoma with vitreous haemorrhage: intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) **2–3 days** before vitrectomy, panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) and **valve implantation** gives a high success rate.

## Viva questions
### Why did you ask / examine…?
Q: Why did you ask about previous trabeculectomy?
A: Multiple failed trabeculectomies and scarred conjunctiva are the main indications.
Q: Why did you ask about double vision?
A: Early, transient: orbital and rectus oedema. Late: bleb displacing the globe, muscle fibrosis.
Q: Why did you ask which drops he uses?
A: Miotics and alpha agonists are contraindicated: they increase vascularisation of the bleb capsule.
Q: Why did you lift the upper lid?
A: To see the bleb, patch graft and any exposure; tube exposure is linked to endophthalmitis.
Q: Why did you look at the gap between tube, cornea and iris?
A: Tube–corneal touch causes corneal oedema and graft failure; in hypotony the tube moves forward.

### Diagnosis
Q: Define a glaucoma drainage device.
A: An alloplastic tube leading to a large equatorial reservoir that drains aqueous humour from the AC.
Q: What are setons, shunts and valves?
A: Seton: non-lumened, surface flow. Shunt: hollow, two-way flow. Valve: one-way, needs an activating pressure.
Q: How does a device bleb differ from a trabeculectomy bleb?
A: More posterior; organised capsule separable from Tenon's capsule; no conjunctival microcysts.

### Investigations
Q: What do you look for on gonioscopy in a device eye?
A: Tube tip (free or blocked); PAS (keep a tube away); NVA (treat before surgery).

### Management and surgery
Q: How does the Ahmed valve work?
A: Two silicone elastomer membranes act as a one-way valve (Venturi effect), opening only above 8–10 mm Hg.
Q: Where do you place the plate, and why not superonasally?
A: Superotemporally, at the equator between two recti, 8–10 mm behind the limbus. Superonasal: pseudo-Brown's syndrome, optic nerve impact.
Q: Why and how is a non-valved tube tied?
A: For 2–3 weeks while the capsule forms: rip cord, a laser-released suture, or nylon with a spacer.
Q: When do you choose a pars plana tube?
A: Aphakia or pseudophakia, after PKP, shallow AC, poor endothelium.
Q: Why a scleral patch graft, and its complications?
A: It covers the 3–5 mm of tube exposed to lid contact, preventing tube erosion, hypotony, tube–corneal touch and scleral perforation; complications: dellen, scleral melt.
Q: Which drugs do you stop before surgery?
A: Pilocarpine or echothiophate (less postoperative inflammation) and warfarin.

### Complications
Q: What causes hypotony after one-stage and two-stage surgery?
A: Leak around the tube where it passes through the eye wall; a needle tract gives a tight fit.
Q: How do you recognise and treat scleral perforation?
A: A gush of vitreous; cryotherapy to the area under indirect ophthalmoscopy.
Q: What are the phases after device surgery?
A: Hypotensive and hypertensive; in the hypertensive phase IOP control is inadequate although the device works.
Q: What if the pressure stays high despite surgery?
A: Cyclodestruction (diode or endolaser cyclophotocoagulation); risks hypotony, phthisis bulbi, retinal detachment.

### Recent advances
Q: How do you improve valve surgery in neovascular glaucoma?
A: Intravitreal anti-VEGF 2–3 days before vitrectomy with PRP and valve implantation.

## Examiner traps
:::trap
- Calling the bleb over the plate a trabeculectomy bleb, or expecting microcysts.
- "Tube in the AC" without clock hour, length, bevel, course, gaps and tip.
- Not lifting the upper lid to see the plate, graft and exposure.
- Skipping EOM and diplopia (on the department sheet).
- Advising pilocarpine or an alpha agonist after a device.
:::

## Say-it script
:::say
Mr X, a sixty-two-year-old man with primary open-angle glaucoma in both eyes for ten years, has come for regular follow-up. His right eye had a failed trabeculectomy and then a glaucoma drainage device three years ago. He has no double vision or redness.

In the right eye, a tube enters the anterior chamber at ten o'clock: two millimetres long, bevel up, parallel to the iris, clear of the cornea and iris, with a patent tip. A bleb lies over the plate superotemporally; the patch graft is intact, with no exposure. The chamber is quiet and of normal depth, and the lens shows grade two nuclear sclerosis. Eye movements are full, without diplopia. Intraocular pressure by Goldmann applanation tonometry is fourteen millimetres of mercury on the right and sixteen on the left at ten in the morning. Gonioscopy shows open angles with the tube tip free. The cup–disc ratio is zero point eight on the right and zero point six on the left.

My diagnosis is: right eye, primary open-angle glaucoma, severe stage, status post trabeculectomy with failed bleb and glaucoma drainage device implantation, with a well-positioned patent tube, intraocular pressure under control on medical management, and grade two nuclear sclerosis; left eye, primary open-angle glaucoma, moderate stage, on medical management.

I would continue his drops, avoiding miotics and alpha agonists, review the tube, cornea, plate and pressure at every visit, and repeat the fields.
:::

## Quick recall
:::recall
- Drainage ∝ capsule area; resistance ∝ capsule thickness.
- **Ahmed**: valved, **184 mm²**, opens above **8–10 mm Hg**; **Molteno**, **Baerveldt**: non-valved, **tied 2–3 weeks**.
- Plate **superotemporal**, **8–10 mm** behind the limbus; avoid superonasal (pseudo-Brown's).
- AC tube **bevel up**, **1.5–2 mm**, parallel to iris; pars plana **bevel down**, **3–4 mm** behind limbus.
- Device bleb: posterior, **no microcysts**; smaller IOP fall than trabeculectomy.
- After a device: **no miotics, no alpha agonists**.
- Hypotony after either technique: **leak around the tube**.
**Mnemonic:** none in your mnemonics file for this case.
:::
