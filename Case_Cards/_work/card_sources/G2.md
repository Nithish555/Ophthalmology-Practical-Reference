## At a glance
- **Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG)** after **trabeculectomy**; the bleb has **failed** and the intraocular pressure (IOP) is above target.
- Checklist case: lasting signs (bleb, surgical iridectomy, cupped disc), long surgical viva.
- Tested: **bleb in house order**, Seidel's test, iridectomy patency, **internal ostium**, antimetabolites, the shallow chamber.

## Case format — history
**Chief complaint:** "Mr X, a 56-year-old man, came with complaints of defective vision in the right eye (RE) for the past 1 year and the left eye (LE) for the past 6 months; the right eye was operated for raised pressure 6 months ago."

### Special tests
**Bleb — in slide order**
1. Patient looks down: **height**, **clarity**, **horizontal extent** (clock hours), **location over the limbus**.
2. High magnification: **vascularity**, **microcysts**, **subconjunctival fibrosis**, **sutures**; then Seidel's test.

Record: in this order, ending "suggestive of a good functioning / failing / failed bleb".

@widths 24 36 40
| Feature | Good functioning bleb | Failing or failed bleb |
|---|---|---|
| Height | Medium: low and diffuse, or circumscribed and elevated | **Flat** (reduced) |
| Clarity, wall | **Translucent**, thin | **Opaque**; thick wall |
| Vascularity | **Avascular** | **Highly vascular, corkscrew** vessels |
| Microcysts | **Numerous** | **Absent** |
| Subconjunctival fibrosis | None | **Present** |
| IOP | Controlled | **Raised** |

**Seidel's test (bleb leak)**
1. Fluorescein over the bleb: **0.25%** shows many leaks; **2%** shows subtle ones.
2. Cobalt blue light: aqueous leaking through the dye = **positive**.

Record: "Seidel's test negative."

**Retro-illumination (PI patency)**
1. Light reflex clearly seen through the iridectomy = patent.
2. Margin **sharp, well defined = surgical**; **ragged, irregular**, with pigment dispersion = **laser**.

Record: "Retro-illumination positive: patent surgical PI at 12 o'clock."

**Gonioscopy of the operated eye** (technique: card G9)
1. After GAT, before dilatation: deepest structure seen in each quadrant.
2. Superiorly: internal ostium, PI, PAS.

## What you must know
### Definition
- **Trabeculectomy**: a **partial-thickness filtering operation**. Aqueous passes through a limbal fistula, around the edges of a scleral flap, into the subconjunctival space; it leaves by transconjunctival filtration or through lymphatics and vessels.
- **Failing bleb**: signs of failure appearing, IOP rising. **Failed bleb**: no longer filters (types 3 and 4), so IOP is elevated.
- For primary open-angle glaucoma itself, see card G1.

### Classification
**Filtering operations**: full thickness, no flap (sclerectomy, trephination, Scheie's thermal sclerostomy, laser sclerostomy, iridencleisis, goniopuncture); partial thickness (**trabeculectomy**); non-penetrating (deep sclerectomy, viscocanalostomy, canaloplasty).

**Bleb types (four)**

@widths 10 70 20
| Type | Appearance | Filters? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Thin, **polycystic** | Yes |
| 2 | **Flat, thin, diffuse**, relatively avascular; microcysts at high magnification | Yes |
| 3 | **Flat**, no microcysts, **engorged surface vessels** | No: IOP up |
| 4 | **Encapsulated (Tenon's cyst)**: localised, highly elevated dome of hypertrophied Tenon's capsule; engorged vessels | No: IOP up |

### Pathogenesis
- Fibroblasts proliferate → **subconjunctival fibrosis**, the **commonest cause of bleb failure**; blood under the flap adds scarring.
- Long-term drops: more conjunctival fibroblasts and inflammatory cells, fewer goblet cells.
- Aqueous damps fibroblasts; older eyes do better (atrophic Tenon's capsule).

| Test | Why | Expected finding |
|---|---|---|
| Seidel's test | Bleb leak | Negative |
| Ultrasound biomicroscopy | PI patency; function of the trabeculectomy | Patent PI and ostium |

### Management
**Indications (POAG)**: failed maximal medical therapy with progressive damage; IOP above target on 3 drops; poor compliance or side effects; baseline IOP over 40 mm Hg; one eye lost to glaucoma; difficult follow-up.

**Relative contraindications**: likely to respond to less invasive treatment; **previously failed trabeculectomy**; scarred conjunctiva; active neovascularisation; uveitic glaucoma.

**Key steps**
1. Bridle suture; limbus- or fornix-based conjunctival flap.
2. Scleral flap based at the limbus, **3 mm radially × 4 mm circumferentially**, cut through 2/3 of scleral thickness.
3. MMC now, if used (table).
4. Dissect the flap into clear cornea; paracentesis.
5. Enter the AC behind the flap hinge; Kelly Descemet's membrane punch: **internal ostium 1.5 × 2 mm**.
6. **Peripheral iridectomy**, basal, wider than the ostium.
7. Flap: interrupted **10-0 nylon**; reform the AC with balanced salt solution (BSS).
8. Conjunctiva: **8-0 vicryl**, watertight.
9. **Atropine 1%**; subconjunctival betamethasone and gentamicin.

**Antimetabolites** go in the sub-Tenon's space: fibrosis is the commonest cause of failure.

@widths 16 42 42
| Feature | Mitomycin C | 5-fluorouracil |
|---|---|---|
| Action | Alkylating agent; all phases of the cell cycle; much more potent | Pyrimidine analogue; **S phase** only |
| Dose | **0.2–0.5 mg/ml for 1–5 min** | **50 mg/ml for 5 min**; subconjunctival **5 mg** |
| Method | Sponge **4.5 × 4.5 mm** under conjunctiva–Tenon's, flap edges pulled over it; rinse with BSS; tighter flap | 30-gauge needle, **90°–180°** from the site; twice daily week 1, daily week 2: **21 injections (105 mg)** |
| Toxicity | More hypotony, bleb leak, bleb infection; **endothelial**; cystic overfiltering bleb (commonest) | **Epithelial**; punctal occlusion |
| Used in | Almost all; essential after failed trabeculectomy, conjunctival or cataract surgery, secondary glaucoma, the young or dark-skinned, drops over 3 years | MMC complications before; risk of hypotony maculopathy |

**Other modulation**: steroids before and after surgery; non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs; collagen inhibitors (β-aminopropionitrile, penicillamine). Prevent toxicity: thorough irrigation, watertight closure.

**Failing or failed filter**
- **Blocked fistula** (early, deep AC): low-energy argon laser around the block; internal bleb revision; intracameral tissue plasminogen activator **6–12.5 µg** for fibrin or clot.
- **Failing bleb**: hourly steroids; **digital pressure** (IOP above **12 mm Hg** or target missed); argon laser suture lysis (**50 µm, 0.02–0.1 s, 250–1000 mW**) or releasable sutures.
- **Failed bleb** (late, fibrosis): restart drops; repeat filtering surgery with antimetabolite at a fresh site, or a drainage implant.
- **Encapsulated bleb**: settles in **2–4 months**; drops meanwhile.

**This eye**
1. Restart drops, **no beta-blocker** (asthma).
2. Above target: repeat trabeculectomy with **MMC** at a fresh superotemporal or superonasal site, or a **glaucoma drainage device** (card G3).
3. Cataract: temporal clear cornea spares the conjunctiva; combined surgery if IOP is uncontrolled.

**Follow-up**: bleb, leak, IOP, AC depth, disc and macula; fields as on card G1.

### Complications
**Early**, besides the table: low IOP, formed AC → **hypotony maculopathy**; high IOP, deep AC → blocked fistula, failing bleb; also uveitis, hyphaema, dellen, loss of central vision.

**Late**: bleb infection and endophthalmitis, cataract, leaking bleb, Tenon's cyst, failure, malignant glaucoma, lid retraction, sympathetic ophthalmia. **Snuff-out**: sudden loss of vision after surgery in advanced glaucoma.

**Shallow AC**: commonest problem after surgery; causes PAS, endothelial damage, cataract.

@widths 34 16 14 36
| Condition | IOP | Seidel | Fundus / other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wound (conjunctival) leak | Low, soft eye | **Positive** | Poor bleb |
| Overfiltration | Very low | Negative | **Good bleb**; choroidal detachment possible |
| Serous choroidal detachment | Low | — | Drain if very deep |
| Pupillary block (incomplete PI) | High | — | PI not patent |
| Malignant (ciliary block) glaucoma | **High** (hard eye) or normal | Negative | **No bleb**; aqueous forced into the vitreous |
| Delayed suprachoroidal haemorrhage | High | — | Aphakic, highly myopic eyes at risk |

— = not stated in the books.

- **Wound or bleb leak**: pressure dressing, Simmons shell, bandage contact lens, cyanoacrylate glue, aqueous suppressants; large defect → resuture or graft.
- **Overfiltration**: firm patching, shell, aqueous suppressants, atropine; then reform the AC.
- **Malignant glaucoma**: strong mydriatics → intravenous mannitol → neodymium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet (Nd:YAG) laser to the anterior hyaloid face through the PI → anterior vitrectomy via the pars plana.
- **Hypotony maculopathy**: prevent with a partial-thickness flap, tighter after MMC, and releasable sutures; 5-FU if at risk.
- **Bleb infection (blebitis)**: earliest sign **mild hyperaemia around the bleb**; delay → **bleb-related endophthalmitis**. Frequent topical antibiotics; steroids after **12–24 hours**.

### Prognosis and counselling
- Worse: age under 40, previous failed filter, aphakia or pseudophakia, neovascular glaucoma, active uveitis, previous conjunctival surgery.
- Plain trabeculectomy lowers IOP by about **41%**.
- Counsel: surgery protects the remaining vision; lost vision does not return. Come at once if the eye turns red.

### Recent advances
- **Photodynamic therapy**: blue light with a photosensitising agent kills fibroblasts.
- **Non-penetrating surgery**: AC not entered, so less overfiltration and hypotony.

## Viva questions
### Why did you ask / examine…?
Q: Why did you ask when the operation was done?
A: Early complications (shallow chamber, blocked fistula, failing bleb) differ from late ones (leak, infection, Tenon's cyst, cataract).

Q: Why did you ask whether an antimetabolite was used?
A: More hypotony, bleb leak and bleb infection, especially with mitomycin C.

Q: Why did you ask about redness?
A: Mild hyperaemia around the bleb is the earliest sign of bleb infection; delay risks endophthalmitis.

Q: Why did you ask about sudden loss of vision?
A: Acute IOP rise with corneal oedema, central retinal vein occlusion, or snuff-out in advanced glaucoma.

Q: Why did you retro-illuminate the iridectomy?
A: To prove patency: an incomplete iridectomy causes pupillary block, with high IOP and a flat chamber.

Q: Why did you do gonioscopy in an operated eye?
A: To see the internal ostium and iridectomy, and any block: iris, ciliary body, lens, vitreous or clot.

### Diagnosis
Q: What are the features of a good filtering bleb?
A: Avascular; numerous microcysts; low and diffuse, or circumscribed and elevated.

Q: What are the signs of a failing bleb?
A: Reduced height, thicker wall, vascularisation, loss of microcysts, raised IOP.

Q: What are the causes of failure of filtration?
A: Intraocular: blocked fistula, bleb failure, poor technique. Extraocular: subconjunctival fibrosis (commonest), racial and genetic factors.

### Investigations
Q: How do you detect a leaking bleb?
A: Fluorescein: 0.25% shows many leaks, 2% subtle ones (Seidel positive).

### Management and surgery
Q: Is "trabeculectomy" a misnomer?
A: Yes. The block need not contain trabecular meshwork; it is more a peripheral posterior keratectomy.

Q: Why a peripheral iridectomy, and how big?
A: To stop the iris blocking the ostium and to prevent pupillary block; basal and wider than the ostium.

Q: Why avoid 12 o'clock?
A: The anterior ciliary vessel enters the ciliary body 3–4 mm from the limbus and bleeds; scarring there also blocks a repeat operation.

Q: Fornix-based or limbus-based conjunctival flap?
A: Equal success. Fornix: easier, better exposure, early limbal leak. Limbus: suture lysis, 5-FU, digital pressure safer early; lower late IOP.

Q: When do you suture the scleral flap tighter?
A: Angle-closure glaucoma or past malignant glaucoma; aphakic or highly myopic eyes; nanophthalmos; raised episcleral venous pressure.

Q: When do you advise digital pressure, and what are its risks?
A: When IOP rises above 12 mm Hg or misses target. Risks: subconjunctival haemorrhage, hyphaema, wound rupture.

Q: Why use releasable sutures or laser suture lysis?
A: The flap can be closed tight against hypotony, then IOP titrated after surgery.

### Complications
Q: What is the commonest problem after trabeculectomy?
A: A shallow anterior chamber.

Q: How do you tell wound leak, overfiltration and malignant glaucoma apart?
A: Leak: soft eye, poor bleb, Seidel positive. Overfiltration: good bleb, Seidel negative. Malignant glaucoma: no bleb, Seidel negative, IOP high (hard eye) or normal.

Q: Which organisms cause bleb-related endophthalmitis?
A: Streptococcus species (most common), Haemophilus influenzae, Staphylococcus.

Q: What is the snuff-out phenomenon?
A: Sudden loss of vision after trabeculectomy in advanced glaucoma.

### Recent advances
Q: What is new in wound modulation?
A: Photodynamic therapy: blue light with a photosensitising agent kills fibroblasts.

## Examiner traps
:::trap
- A highly elevated bleb called "good": an **encapsulated bleb** does not filter.
- "No leak" without **Seidel's test**: leak and overfiltration both give a low IOP.
- Not retro-illuminating the **iridectomy**: an incomplete one causes pupillary block.
- Gonioscopy without naming the **internal ostium**.
- A **beta-blocker** for this asthmatic.
- "Status post trabeculectomy" without **bleb status, IOP and lens**.
:::

## Say-it script
:::say
My patient, a fifty-six-year-old asthmatic man, has gradual defective vision in the right eye for one year and the left eye for six months. He was not compliant with drops, and the right eye was operated six months ago.

Vision is 6/60 in the right eye and 6/24 in the left, improving to 6/12 and 6/9 with pinhole. The right eye has a flat, opaque bleb from eleven to two o'clock superiorly, with dilated tortuous vessels, no microcysts and subconjunctival fibrosis. Seidel's test is negative. A surgical iridectomy at twelve o'clock is patent on retro-illumination. The anterior chambers are of normal depth. The right eye has a grade three relative afferent pupillary defect; both lenses have grade two nuclear sclerosis. Intraocular pressure by Goldmann applanation is twenty-six millimetres of mercury on the right and twenty-four on the left. Gonioscopy shows open angles, with a patent internal ostium on the right. The cup–disc ratio is 0.9 on the right and 0.75 on the left.

My diagnosis is right eye primary open-angle glaucoma status post trabeculectomy with failed bleb and grade two nuclear sclerosis; left eye primary open-angle glaucoma and grade two nuclear sclerosis.

I would document the fields, restart drops avoiding beta-blockers, and if the pressure stays above target, plan repeat trabeculectomy with mitomycin C at a fresh site or a glaucoma drainage device.
:::

## Quick recall
:::recall
- **Good bleb**: avascular, numerous microcysts. **Failing**: lower, thicker-walled, vascular, microcysts lost.
- Commonest cause of failure: **subconjunctival fibrosis**; commonest problem after surgery: **shallow AC**.
- **Leak**: Seidel positive. **Overfiltration**: good bleb. **Malignant glaucoma**: no bleb; IOP high or normal.
- **MMC 0.2–0.5 mg/ml for 1–5 min**; **5-FU 50 mg/ml for 5 min**, subconjunctival **5 mg**.
- Flap **3 × 4 mm**; ostium **1.5 × 2 mm**.
- Digital pressure if IOP above **12 mm Hg**; Tenon's cyst settles in **2–4 months**.
- Earliest sign of bleb infection: **hyperaemia around the bleb**.

**Mnemonic:** none in your mnemonics file for this case.
:::
