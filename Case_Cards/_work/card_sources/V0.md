## What this session is
A **live mock long-case examination** held on Zoom during the pandemic, when practical exams were moving to virtual case presentations.
- **Seven postgraduates** presented a long case each. Each presentation was meant to be about 5 minutes; one took 8.
- Each presentation was followed by about **20 minutes of viva** and a few minutes of feedback on **technique**.
- The organisers said plainly that the aim was to test the **technique of answering**, not knowledge.
- **Examiners:**
  - Prof **M. Vanathi** — professor of cornea and refractive surgery, Dr R.P. Centre, AIIMS New Delhi; co-author of a postgraduate ophthalmology textbook with Dr Zia Chaudhuri.
  - Prof **Nagaraju** — senior professor, Regional Institute of Ophthalmology (Minto), Bangalore; more than 30 years of teaching.
  - Dr Gopal (retina, the organiser) joined for the retina cases.
- **Host:** Dr Sai Kumar.
- **Closing talk:** medical management of glaucoma, by Dr Thomas Arun Varghese, at the start of World Glaucoma Week.
- Names are as heard in the recording.

## How to read these cards
- **One card per case (V2–V8)**:
  - the case as presented;
  - a table of **examiner corrections** — what the candidate said and what the examiner wanted;
  - **think like this** — the reasoning the examiner walked the candidate through;
  - the **viva** questions with the answers that were accepted or given, each with its time;
  - a corrected **say-it** summary;
  - the **images** shown, each with its points.
- **"(added)"** marks an answer or fact the session did not state in full; it was completed from standard texts so that you have a model answer.
- **V10** is the glaucoma talk. **VV** collects every viva question by topic. **VS** lists every slide in time order. **VQ** is a topic finder: "what did they say about …?".
- Patient names on the slides have been left out and patient photographs are cropped to the eyes.

## Summary of the whole session
Across seven cases the examiners kept asking the same thing: **show how you reached the diagnosis**.
- **History:** they wanted it **in the patient's own words**, told **prospectively** (not rearranged to fit the final diagnosis), with **optical history** under its own heading. Every negative point had to name the differential it excludes.
- **Examination:** they wanted it described in a fixed order — adnexa before the cornea; disc → macula → vessels → background in the fundus — with exact measurements, not "two-thirds of the cornea".
- **Diagnosis:** ordered as **primary, then secondary, then tertiary**, using precise terms ("senile cataract", "complicated cataract", "neurogenic ptosis", "secondary angle-closure glaucoma").
- **Management:**
  - start with **your** patient, not hypothetical scenarios;
  - drugs by **generic name with the correct dose**;
  - recent investigations relevant to the case (OCT, anterior segment OCT, angiography) are fair game.
- **Mistakes the examiners went for first:**
  - an ignored BP of 220/110;
  - ptosis listed separately in a third nerve palsy;
  - "pseudoproptosis" with equal axial lengths;
  - a 49-year-old's cataract called senile;
  - digital tonometry on a perforated eye.

## Summary by hour
**First hour (0:00–1:00).**
- **Case 1, PACG with cataract:**
  - building differentials from the history;
  - why each negative history is asked;
  - Van Herick grading;
  - planning surgery for PACG with cataract (medical control first, then cataract surgery alone or sequential surgery);
  - target pressure in the low-to-mid teens;
  - the need for a field report.
- **Case 2, CRVO with neovascular glaucoma:**
  - categorising risk factors;
  - signs of ischaemia;
  - why NVI rather than NVD;
  - indentation gonioscopy for synechial closure.

**Second hour (1:00–2:00).**
- **Case 2, continued:**
  - collaterals versus new vessels;
  - haemorrhage types;
  - IOP control without prostaglandins or miotics;
  - anti-VEGF with the correct dose;
  - OCT thickness;
  - steroids;
  - timing of PRP.
- **Case 3, proptosis:**
  - signs of TED;
  - when to abandon TED;
  - CT versus MRI;
  - exophthalmometers;
  - red desaturation;
  - pseudoproptosis.
- **Feedback break.**
- **Case 4, third nerve palsy (start):** the presentation.

**Third hour (2:00–3:00).**
- **Case 4, viva:**
  - the BP and ptosis mistakes;
  - localising the lesion from the orbit to the nucleus;
  - the midbrain syndromes;
  - pupil sparing;
  - when a palsy is an emergency.
- **Case 5, perforated corneal ulcer:**
  - bacterial versus fungal;
  - corneal diagrams;
  - scraping;
  - Mooren's versus PUK;
  - glue versus therapeutic keratoplasty;
  - anterior segment OCT.
- **Case 6 begins.**

**Fourth hour (3:00–4:05).**
- **Case 6, high myopia with uveitis:**
  - ordering the diagnosis;
  - proving pseudoproptosis and earlier LASIK.
- **Case 7, retinal detachment:**
  - trauma history and scars;
  - Shafer's sign;
  - Lincoff's rules;
  - degenerations that need treatment;
  - buckle versus vitrectomy;
  - pathological myopia;
  - refractive lens exchange;
  - Gonin.
- **Residency-preparation advice.**
- **Glaucoma talk:** newer drugs and their delivery.
