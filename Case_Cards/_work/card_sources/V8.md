## Think like this
1. **Count the risk factors for detachment in this eye:**
   - a vitrectomised eye (after IOFB removal);
   - pseudophakia;
   - YAG capsulotomy;
   - (here) old trauma with an atrophic scar near the laser marks.
2. **Classify every RD**: rhegmatogenous, tractional, exudative, or combined. This one is **rhegmatogenous**, so there must be a break — keep looking.
3. **Use Lincoff's rules** to predict where the break lies from the shape of the detachment, then indent that area.
4. **Remember the principle of surgery:**
   - "Find the break and seal it" (Gonin).
   - The examiner's analogy: either bring the mountain to Muhammad (external procedures — the **scleral buckle** pushes the wall in to the retina) or Muhammad to the mountain (internal procedures — **gas or silicone oil** push the retina out to the wall).
5. **A vitrectomised eye behaves differently**: a buckle alone may suit a fresh detachment, but the choice depends on the surgeon (Dr Gopal).

## Viva: questions and expected answers
Q: How do you classify retinal detachment? (3:23:48)
A: Rhegmatogenous, tractional, exudative (serous) and combined. This is rhegmatogenous.
Q: What slit-lamp signs suggest a rhegmatogenous RD? (3:24:57)
A: **Pigment in the anterior vitreous (Shafer's sign, "tobacco dust")**, and a **lower IOP** than the other eye — (added) usually about 4–5 mm Hg lower. Shafer's sign is pathognomonic only in an unoperated eye.
Q: Which risk factors does this eye have? (3:25:40)
A: Previous trauma, vitrectomy, cataract surgery and YAG capsulotomy. In general also high myopia and predisposing peripheral degenerations.
Q: What are Lincoff's rules? (3:27:13)
A: They predict the primary break from the shape of the RD. (added) The main ones:
- A shallow inferior RD that is slightly higher on one side has its break on that side.
- An inferior RD with equal fluid on both sides has its break near 6 o'clock.
- A superior RD that stays on one side has its break within 1–1.5 clock hours of its highest border.
- A superior RD crossing the 12 o'clock meridian has its break near 12 o'clock, on the side of the lower border.
- A bullous inferior RD usually comes from a break above the horizontal meridian.
Q: Which peripheral degenerations predispose to RD, and which do not? (3:28:19)
A: **Predispose:** lattice degeneration (especially with holes or tears), snail-track degeneration, cystic retinal tufts, retinal tears, (added) degenerative retinoschisis with outer-layer holes. **Do not (no treatment):** paving-stone (cobblestone) degeneration, microcystoid degeneration, (added) honeycomb (reticular) degeneration and peripheral drusen. White without pressure is usually only observed.
Q: A high myope has lattice with holes. What do you do? (3:29:42)
A: Prophylactic **barrage laser** around the lesion (in suitable cases — fellow eye of an RD, symptomatic tears).
Q: What are the principles of managing this RD? (3:30:20)
A:
- Identify the type and find every break.
- For a rhegmatogenous RD the options are a **scleral buckle** or **pars plana vitrectomy with tamponade** (SF6 or C3F8 gas, or silicone oil).
- In this already vitrectomised eye: check for residual vitreous (stain with triamcinolone), complete the vitrectomy, drain the subretinal fluid, treat the breaks with laser or cryotherapy, then tamponade.
Q: When is an encircling band indicated? (3:31:46)
A: Breaks in **several quadrants**, extensive detachment or degeneration over more than two quadrants, large breaks; (added) also to support the vitreous base when proliferative vitreoretinopathy is present.
Q: What is pathological myopia? (3:32:19)
A: (added) High myopia (more than −6 D, axial length more than 26 mm) **with degenerative changes**: peripapillary atrophy and temporal crescent, tilted disc, **lacquer cracks**, Foster–Fuchs spot, choroidal neovascularisation, posterior staphyloma, chorioretinal atrophy.
Q: What refractive surgery would you offer a 45-year-old high myope with early posterior subcapsular cataract who wants to stop wearing glasses? (3:33:16)
A: **Refractive lens exchange**, usually done only after about 45 years of age because the **risk of RD** is high in younger high myopes. Other options for high myopia: **phakic IOL (ICL)** and bioptics.
Q: Who is the father of modern retinal surgery? (3:35:44)
A: **Jules Gonin** — "find the break and seal it".
