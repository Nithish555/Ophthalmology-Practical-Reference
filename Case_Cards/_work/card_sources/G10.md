## What it is
- The **Humphrey field analyser** does **static automated threshold perimetry**. A dim, still light is brightened at each point until seen. Automated perimetry is the **gold standard** for diagnosing and following glaucoma.
- **Threshold**: the intensity seen **50%** of the time. A higher threshold means lower sensitivity.
- Stimulus **Goldmann size III, white**, on a **31.5 apostilb (asb)** background. **Decibels (dB)**: **0 dB = 10,000 asb** (brightest), **40 dB = 1 asb**.
- **24-2** (54 points) and **30-2** (76 points) use a **6°** grid. The 24-2 suits suspects and established glaucoma, the 30-2 suspects. **10-2** (68 points, **2°** grid) is for advanced glaucoma.
- **Swedish interactive threshold algorithm (SITA)**: half the time, with little or no loss of accuracy.

**Keywords the examiner listens for:** reliability indices · pattern deviation · glaucoma hemifield test · mean deviation · pattern standard deviation · visual field index · Bjerrum's area · arcuate scotoma · nasal step · Anderson and Patella criteria

## Reading order
Read zone by zone, then conclude. Judge reliability before naming any defect.
1. **Zone 1, patient data**: eye and age. A wrong age compares him with the wrong normals.
2. **Zone 2, test conditions**: programme, strategy, pupil (dilate if under 2 mm), acuity, correcting lens.
3. **Zone 3, reliability indices**: fixation losses, false positives, false negatives, foveal threshold.
4. **Zone 4, grey scale**: a picture of the raw values, without statistics.
5. **Zone 5, raw data**: threshold in dB at each point; 0 dB is an absolute scotoma.
6. **Zone 6, deviation plots** (lower half): numbers above, probability symbols below. **Total deviation** = patient minus age-matched normal. **Pattern deviation** removes overall depression: name the defect here.
7. **Zone 7, glaucoma hemifield test**: glaucomatous loss is asymmetrical. So five sectors above the horizontal raphe are compared with their mirror images below.
8. **Zone 8, global indices**: mean deviation (MD), pattern standard deviation (PSD), visual field index (VFI).
9. **Conclude**: defect, criteria, stage, and the matching disc damage. A superior defect matches the inferior pole.

## Findings to name
@widths 36 64
| Finding | What it means |
|---|---|
| Fixation losses 20% or more | Unreliable. A light flashed into the blind spot was seen: the eye had moved |
| False positives over 15% | Unreliable. He pressed when no light was shown ("trigger-happy"); white scotomas on the grey scale |
| False negatives over 33% | Unreliable. He missed a light brighter than one seen there before: fatigue, inattention or advanced glaucoma. Clover-leaf grey scale |
| Low foveal threshold, good acuity | Wrong optical correction |
| Total deviation depressed, pattern deviation normal | Uniform depression: cataract, media opacity, small pupil, uncorrected refraction |
| Hemifield test outside normal limits (p<1%) or borderline (p<3%) | Upper and lower halves differ, as in glaucoma. Other results: within normal limits, general reduction of sensitivity, abnormally high sensitivity |
| MD more negative | Overall loss; normal is typically within 2 dB of 0 |
| PSD high | Focal loss; low PSD means no loss or diffuse loss |
| VFI | Per cent of a normal field for age (100 normal, 0 blind); unaffected by cataract |

### Glaucomatous defects
They begin in **Bjerrum's area**, the central 25° (first defects 10–25° from fixation). They respect the **horizontal meridian** and appear in this order.
@widths 36 64
| Defect | What it is |
|---|---|
| Generalised depression | Earliest; not diagnostic |
| Baring of the blind spot | Early, very non-specific |
| Paracentral scotoma | Earliest clinically significant; usually superonasal, ending at the horizontal midline |
| Seidel's scotoma | Paracentral scotoma joined to the blind spot: sickle-shaped |
| Arcuate (Bjerrum's) scotoma | From the blind spot, arching above or below fixation to the horizontal raphe |
| Ring (double arcuate) scotoma | Superior and inferior arcuates joined |
| Nasal (Roenne's) step | A right-angled step at the horizontal meridian, nasally |
| Tubular vision, temporal island | End stage: only a central and a temporal island remain |
| Dense hemifield (altitudinal) defect | Not a stage: goes with deep focal notching of the rim. Commonest defect in non-arteritic ischaemic optic neuropathy |

## Grading or classification
### Is it glaucomatous? Anderson and Patella criteria
Shown on at least **2 consecutive reliable fields**:
- **3 or more contiguous non-edge points** on the 30-2 pattern deviation plot at **p<5%**, one at **p<1%**.
- **PSD** at **p<5%**.
- **Glaucoma hemifield test outside normal limits**.

### How severe?

| Parameter | Early | Moderate | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|
| MD | **−6 dB or better** | **−6 to −12 dB** | **−12 dB or worse** |
| Central 5° points under 15 dB | None | One hemifield | Both hemifields |
| Central 10° | Spared | Spared | **Involved** |


## What each finding leads to (management)
- **Unreliable**: do not diagnose; re-instruct and repeat. The criteria need 2 reliable fields.
- **Uniform depression**: correct the refraction, dilate a small pupil, look for cataract; repeat.
- **Criteria met, matching the disc**: glaucomatous defect. Stage it and set the target pressure (card G1).
- **Central 10° involved**: serial **10-2** fields; a **size V** stimulus shows the macular split.
- **Progression** (worsening MD or VFI, deeper or wider scotoma, new defect): seen on trend and progression analyses. Despite maximal medical therapy, it calls for trabeculectomy.
- **Repeat fields** 6-monthly in mild to moderate, 3-monthly in advanced glaucoma.
- **Does not fit** (vertical meridian, no matching disc, pallor more than cupping, rapid progression): order neuroimaging.

## Viva questions
Q: What is a visual field, and what is a scotoma?
A: **Visual field**: Traquair's "island of vision in a sea of darkness". It spans about 60° up, 75° down, 60° nasally and 100° temporally. A **scotoma** is an area of lower sensitivity within greater sensitivity.
Q: What is an absolute and a relative scotoma?
A: In an **absolute scotoma** even the largest, brightest target is not seen. In a **relative scotoma** a brighter light is seen there, but a dimmer one is not.
Q: Why name the defect on the pattern deviation plot?
A: **Pattern deviation** shifts total deviation so its 7th best point reads 0. This removes generalised depression and unmasks localised defects.
Q: How does a cataract change the printout?
A: Total deviation and **mean deviation** fall; pattern deviation stays near normal. **Pattern standard deviation** stays low; the **visual field index** is unaffected.
Q: What does p<2% mean?
A: Under 2% of normal people have such low sensitivity there. It shows significance, not depth.
Q: Is the defect glaucomatous or neurological?
A: Glaucomatous: horizontal meridian (a **nasal step**), relative, incongruous. Neurological: vertical meridian, absolute, mostly congruous.
Q: What else causes an arcuate scotoma?
A: An **arcuate scotoma** also follows myopic peripapillary atrophy, disc drusen, optic pit, ischaemic optic neuropathy and pituitary adenoma.
Q: Why is the superior field hit first?
A: Inferior arcuate fibres are more susceptible. Central fibres arise closest to the disc and exit its centre, so central vision goes last.
Q: Which changes mean progression?
A: A reproducible fall of over 7 dB in a scotoma means deepening. Over 9 dB beside it means enlarging; over 11 dB at a normal point, a new scotoma.
Q: What is a decibel in perimetry?
A: A **decibel** is 0.1 log unit of attenuation of the brightest stimulus. A higher dB means a dimmer light was seen: more sensitive.
