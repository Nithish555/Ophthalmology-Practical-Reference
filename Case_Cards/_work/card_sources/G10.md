## What it is
- **Static automated threshold perimetry**: a still light, brightened until seen; **threshold** = the intensity seen **50%** of the time. **Gold standard** for glaucoma diagnosis and management.
- **Goldmann size III white** stimulus, **31.5 apostilb (asb)** background. **Decibels (dB)**: **0 dB = 10,000 asb**, **40 dB = 1 asb**; higher = more sensitive.
- **24-2** (54 points) and **30-2** (76): **6°** grid; **10-2** (68): **2°** grid, advanced glaucoma.
- **Swedish interactive threshold algorithm (SITA)**: half the time of full threshold, little or no accuracy lost.

## Reading order
Reliability first; name the defect on the **pattern deviation** plot.
1. **Zone 1, patient data** (top strip): **eye**, **age** (age-matched normals).
2. **Zone 2, test conditions** (top centre, right): programme, strategy, **pupil** (**<2 mm: dilate**), acuity, **correcting lens**.
3. **Zone 3, reliability** (top left): **fixation losses <20%** (fraction: 8/19 = 42%, unreliable); unreliable if **false positives >15%** (**white scotomas**) or **false negatives >33%** (**clover-leaf**; may mean advanced glaucoma); **fovea** should fit acuity.
4. **Zone 4, grey scale** (top right): picture of raw values; no statistics.
5. **Zone 5, raw data** (top left): threshold in dB; **0 = absolute scotoma**.
6. **Zone 6, deviation plots** (lower half: numbers, then probability (p) symbols): **total deviation (TD)** (left), patient minus age-matched normal: overall and focal loss; **pattern deviation (PD)** (right), TD shifted so its **7th best point** reads 0: overall depression removed, **localised defects** left.
7. **Zone 7, glaucoma hemifield test (GHT)** (right): **5 sectors above versus mirror images below**: **outside normal limits (p<1%)**, **borderline (p<3%)**, within normal limits, general reduction, abnormally high sensitivity.
8. **Zone 8, global indices** (below GHT): **mean deviation (MD)**, overall loss (normal typically within 2 dB of 0; more negative = worse); **pattern standard deviation (PSD)**, focal loss; **visual field index (VFI)**, % of normal, from PD (unaffected by cataract).
9. **Conclude**: defect, Anderson criteria, stage, **matching disc and retinal nerve fibre layer (RNFL) damage** (superior defect: inferior pole).

## Findings to name
From **Bjerrum's area (10–25° from fixation)**, respecting the **horizontal meridian**, in order:
1. **Generalised depression**: earliest, not diagnostic.
2. **Baring of the blind spot**: early, non-specific.
3. **Paracentral scotoma**: **earliest clinically significant**; mostly **superonasal**.
4. **Seidel's scotoma**: joins the blind spot; **sickle-shaped**.
5. **Arcuate (Bjerrum's) scotoma**: blind spot to **horizontal raphe**.
6. **Ring (double arcuate) scotoma**.
7. **Nasal (Roenne's) step**: right-angled, at the horizontal midline.
8. **Tubular vision** and **temporal island**.
### Anderson and Patella criteria (at least 2 consecutive reliable fields)
- **3 or more contiguous non-edge points** on the 30-2 PD plot, all **p<5%**, one **p<1%**
- **PSD or corrected PSD (CPSD) p<5%**
- **GHT outside normal limits**

## Grading or classification
All stages: **CPSD p<5%**, **GHT outside normal limits**.

## What each finding leads to (management)
- **Unreliable**: do not diagnose; re-instruct, **repeat** (criteria need **2 reliable fields**).
- **Generalised depression, PD normal**: cataract, miosis or uncorrected refraction; correct it, **dilate if pupil <2 mm**, repeat.
- **Criteria met, matching the disc**: **glaucomatous field defect**; stage it.
- **Central 10° involved**: serial **10-2**; **size V** for the macular split.
- **Worse on serial fields** (falling MD or VFI; deeper, wider or new scotoma; **trend** or **glaucoma progression analysis**): **progression**; review treatment.
- **Does not fit** (vertical meridian, no disc match): artefact, retinal, neurological or other optic nerve disease.

## Viva questions
Q: Why PD, not TD?
A: Removes generalised depression (cataract, small pupil), unmasking localised defects.
Q: Effect of cataract?
A: TD and MD depressed; PD near normal; PSD low; VFI unaffected.
Q: What does p<2% mean?
A: Under 2% of normals are this low there; significance, not depth.
Q: Glaucomatous versus neurological defects?
A: Glaucomatous: horizontal meridian, relative, incongruous. Neurological: vertical, absolute, mostly congruous.
Q: Other causes of an arcuate scotoma?
A: Myopic peripapillary atrophy, disc drusen, ischaemic optic neuropathy, pituitary adenoma.
Q: Point criteria for progression?
A: Reproducible loss >7 dB within a scotoma, >9 dB beside it, >11 dB at a normal point.
Q: False positives 20%?
A: Unreliable (over 15%): trigger-happy, white scotomas. Repeat.
Q: Perimetry for earlier detection?
A: Short-wavelength automated (blue-on-yellow) and frequency-doubling technology.
