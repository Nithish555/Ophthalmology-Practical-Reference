## Indications
- Intraocular pressure (IOP) in **every glaucoma case**, both eyes.
- Goldmann applanation tonometry (GAT): **reliable, reproducible**, **not influenced by scleral rigidity**.
- Other tonometers:
  - cornea scarred, irregular or oedematous, bandage contact lens, nystagmus → **Tonopen** (scarring: also **non-contact**, **Mackay–Marg**, **pneumatic**)
  - bed-bound or anaesthetised → **Perkins** (hand-held)
  - community screening → **non-contact tonometer** (no anaesthetic)

## Instrument check
- **Calibration** checked each session (manufacturer's **calibration arm**).
- **Dial at 1** (10 mm Hg), never 0: **microvibrations** can cause **corneal erosions**.
- **Astigmatism over 3 dioptres**: rotate the prism to align the **minus-cylinder axis** with the **red line** on the housing, or average two readings, prism turned **90°** for the second.

## Steps
Observed-station checklist: consent, patient instruction, tonometer checked, topical drops applied, handling, (Schiotz) conversion chart referred to.

1. **Introduce yourself**, explain the test, take **consent**.
2. **Instruct**: "Chin on the rest, forehead against the headband. Look straight ahead. Keep both eyes open. Do not squeeze. Breathe normally."
3. **Check the tonometer**: calibration, prism clean, **dial at 1**, prism rotation.
4. **Disinfect** the prism tip: **70% isopropyl alcohol**, **3% hydrogen peroxide** or **diluted sodium hypochlorite (1:10)**.
5. Instil a **topical anaesthetic drop** and **fluorescein** (strip or **0.25%** dye).
6. Slit lamp: **cobalt blue filter**, **maximal** illumination, light and microscope **60°** apart.
7. Separate the lids gently if needed: **no pressure on the globe**.
8. Advance until the prism **just touches the corneal apex**: two **green semicircular mires**, above and below the horizontal midline.
9. Turn the dial until the mires are **equal** and their **inner edges just touch**.
10. **Dial reading × 10 = IOP in mm Hg** (1.8 → 18 mm Hg). Repeat for the other eye; avoid quick repeats.
11. After use, **disinfect the prism again**.

## How to record the result
IOP (GAT) right eye (RE) 18 mm Hg, left eye (LE) 16 mm Hg at 10:30 am: **eye, method, value, time**. Case sheet: **Tn (tension) by GAT** row; central corneal thickness (CCT) goes under investigations.

- **Time** (diurnal variation): usually highest in the morning, lowest in the evening; swings **3–6 mm Hg** in normal eyes, **10 mm Hg or more** in glaucomatous eyes; peaks **3–4 hours after** the **cortisol** peak.

## Normal values and interpretation
- Normal IOP **10–21 mm Hg**. **Why 21**: mean **15.5 ± 2.57**; mean + 2 standard deviations ≈ **20.5** (**95%** of a Gaussian curve lies within ± 2). The curve is **skewed to the right**, so 21 is only approximate.
- Basis: **Imbert–Fick law** (**P = F/A**). At **3.06 mm**, corneal rigidity and the tear meniscus's **capillary attraction** cancel out.
- **CCT** (mean **520 µm**): thick **over**-reads, thin **under**-reads, about **0.7 mm Hg per 10 µm**. **Oedema** reads falsely **low**.

## Common errors
- **Mires too thick / too thin** (inappropriate fluorescein pattern) → falsely **high** / **low**.
- **Unequal semicircles** (improper vertical alignment) → falsely **high**.
- **Squeezing**, or pressure on lids or globe → IOP **raised** (orbicularis tone).
- **Breath-holding or straining** (Valsalva manoeuvre) → IOP **raised** (episcleral venous pressure).
- **Astigmatism** → with-the-rule **under**-reads, against-the-rule **over**-reads, about **1 mm Hg per 4 dioptres**.
- **Thick, thin, oedematous, scarred or irregular cornea**, or after corneal refractive surgery → **inaccurate** (see CCT).
- **Repeated readings** in quick succession → slight **fall** (massaging effect).

## Viva questions
Q: State the Imbert–Fick law.
A: Pressure in an ideal dry, thin-walled sphere = force to flatten its surface ÷ area flattened (P = F/A).
Q: Why 3.06 mm?
A: Corneal rigidity cancels tear-meniscus capillary attraction, and 0.1 g = 1 mm Hg (hence dial × 10).
Q: Why fluorescein and cobalt blue light?
A: Stained tears fluoresce, showing the **tear meniscus** at the prism–cornea edge as the two semicircles.
Q: CCT 600 µm, GAT 26 mm Hg. Comment.
A: Over-reads by about 80/10 × 0.7 ≈ 5.6 mm Hg: corrected IOP ≈ 20 mm Hg.
Q: Why is applanation better than Schiotz?
A: **Not influenced by scleral rigidity**; Schiotz under-reads in high myopia and after ocular surgery, over-reads in high hypermetropia.
Q: Which infections can a tonometer spread?
A: **Adenovirus** (epidemic keratoconjunctivitis), **herpes simplex virus type 1**, **human immunodeficiency virus**, **hepatitis B**.
Q: How is Schiotz tonometry done?
A: Supine, cornea anaesthetised; zero checked on the metal plate; footplate on cornea without pressing the globe; scale reading → **conversion chart**. Weights 5.5, 7.5, 10, 15 g.
Q: Which tonometer ignores corneal thickness?
A: **Dynamic contour tonometer (PASCAL)**: contour-matching surface, digital display, no fluorescein.
