# 01 Glaucoma — working notes: book disagreements and points to confirm with your seniors

The cards follow the newer book when the books disagree (Baidya 2024 > Namrata Sharma > Aravind 2013). The disagreements live here, not on the cards.
Each card also has a full writer's claims ledger (`checks/<ID>_notes.md`) and an independent fact-check report with every change and its book page (`checks/<ID>_check.md`).

## G1 — POAG

1. **Gonioscopy notation.** The department heads the cross "By modified Shaffer's grading" and writes III in every
   quadrant. The card now writes the deepest structure seen (SS), as last year's exam sheet did. No book says which
   structure each "modified Shaffer" grade stands for, and in Aravind's Shaffer table grade 3 shows structures up to
   the ciliary body. Ask whether to write the grade, the structure, or both.
2. **Family-history figures.** The card gives the newer book's "4-fold in siblings, 2-fold in offspring". Aravind
   gives "siblings 10%, offspring 4%". Know both in case an examiner quotes percentages.
3. **Staging and mean-deviation cut-offs.** Both books print "MD < −6 dB" for early and "MD > −12 dB" for severe. The
   card reads these as "better than −6 dB" and "worse than −12 dB", the only reading that fits Aravind's middle band of
   −6 to −12. The books also define "mild" differently: Baidya says nasal step or paracentral scotoma, Namrata says a
   normal standard field. That choice changes the Z value and the 30% target. Ask which staging the department uses.
4. **Upper limit of normal pressure.** Baidya gives normal IOP as 10–21 mm Hg on its POAG page but 12–22 on its NTG
   page. For NTG it says "always below 22", while Aravind says "less than 21". The card uses 10–21 and < 22.
5. **Cup–disc asymmetry: "> 0.2" or "≥ 0.2"?** The card says "> 0.2", the majority wording. Baidya's own case format
   says "≥ 0.2". This matters for the department's own example patient: right eye 0.8 and left eye 0.6 is exactly 0.2.

## G4 — Primary angle-closure disease

1. **Nd:YAG iridotomy settings** — the three books differ: Baidya 2024 (now on the card) 2–5 mJ, 1–3 pulses, optimal
   size 150–200 µm; Namrata 4–8 mJ, at least 200 µm (preferably 500 µm); Aravind 3–8 mJ, 300–500 µm. Ask what your
   department's laser is set at, and be ready to say "2 to 5 millijoules per Baidya; some texts use up to 8".
2. **ISGEO extent of iridotrabecular contact** — Baidya (card): three or more quadrants, almost 270°; Aravind: more than
   270°; Namrata: more than 180°. As this is your thesis area, check which definition your thesis and your guide use,
   and know that the other one exists.
3. **Writing the gonioscopy cross in the exam** — the card writes the deepest structure seen in each quadrant
   (as on last year's exam sheet). Your department's POAG sheet writes a Roman numeral "by modified Shaffer's grading";
   ask which the examiners expect, and write the structure too.
4. **Vogt's triad** — Baidya and Namrata (card): glaukomflecken, sectoral iris atrophy, Krukenberg spindle. Aravind:
   glaukomflecken, patches of iris atrophy, slightly dilated non-reacting pupil. Know both.
5. **Proof of LPI patency** — the department slide calls a light reflex on retro-illumination "patent"; Namrata says
   retro-illumination is not a sure sign and seeing the anterior lens capsule confirms it (Aravind: lens capsule must be
   seen on gonioscopy). The card presents with the department wording and keeps the caveat as a trap; confirm which
   your examiners want said first.

## G5 — Neovascular glaucoma

1. **PRP settings in NVG.** The card follows the newest book: 500 µm, 0.1 s, mild white burns one burn-width apart, 1200–1600 burns in 3–4 sittings. The older FAQ book gives a heavier set for NVG (1800–2200 burns, moderately intense, half a burn-width apart, in 2–3 sittings over 3–6 weeks). The AIIMS book says about 2000–2500 spots in 2–3 sittings. Ask which numbers your examiners expect for NVG.
2. **Timing of anti-VEGF before surgery.** The card says 2–3 days (newest book). The other books say usually 1 week (can be 14–48 hours), and 24–78 hours.
3. **Mannitol dose.** The card says 20%, 1–2 g/kg over 20–30 minutes. One book's NVG chapter prints "100 mL … 2 g/kg", which does not add up. Another book gives 1.5 g/kg (200 mL) over 30 minutes. Confirm the dose your department uses.
4. **Why ectropion uveae occurs.** The card follows two books: the contracting fibrovascular membrane pulls the posterior pigment layer round the pupil margin. The newest book links it to "repeated uveitic attacks". Confirm which viva answer is preferred.
5. **Antimetabolite strength for trabeculectomy.** This card gives 5-fluorouracil 50 mg/mL for 5 min and mitomycin C 0.2–0.4 mg/mL for 2 min (the book's NVG chapter). The trabeculectomy card (G2) gives 30 mg/mL and 0.2–0.5 mg/mL for 1–5 min (the same book's trabeculectomy chapter). Confirm the strengths your department uses.

## G6 — Pseudoexfoliation

1. **Laterality.** Aravind (Q8, fitz 259) says "glaucoma capsulare" is usually unilateral but "PXF glaucoma" tends to be
   bilateral. Namrata (Table 2, fitz 224) says PXG is usually unilateral, and the card follows Namrata. The card's
   "syndrome mostly bilateral" comes only from Aravind (Q7). The newer books describe unilateral cupping and use
   "bilateral" to separate pigmentary glaucoma from PXF. Ask which answer your examiners expect.
2. **Age of PXF.** The card says "after 50, mostly 7th decade" (newest book). Namrata also says "commonly after 40" and
   "7th–8th decade", and its Table 3 cell prints "60 years" with no sign. Aravind says 60–70 in one place and "5th decade
   and older" in another. PXG onset "over 60" is Namrata Table 2.
3. **Age of pigment dispersion.** G6 says 30–50 years (Namrata Table 3, PXF chapter). Namrata's POAG chapter (fitz 192)
   gives 20–30 years for pigmentary glaucoma, Aravind says "3rd decade", and card G1 writes "Age 20–30". Agree one figure
   for both cards.
4. **Aravind Q15 (fitz 261)** prints the angle-pigment comparison the opposite way: PXF uniform and not reaching
   Schwalbe's line; pigmentary irregular and anterior to Schwalbe's line. The card follows Baidya, Namrata and Aravind's
   own Q10 and section 4.10. If an examiner quotes Aravind Q15, explain the newer books' position.
5. **Which quadrant is most pigmented in PXF.** Namrata (fitz 222) says "more prominent in the superior quadrant". Only one
   book says this and it is not on the card. Check what your teachers expect before quoting a quadrant.

## G7 — CRVO with POAG

1. **What to call it.** Your department writes "hemi-CRVO", which the say-it speaks as "hemi-central retinal vein occlusion". In Kanski the umbrella term is "hemiretinal vein occlusion": "hemicentral" strictly means the less common dual-trunk variant, and "hemispheric" is a branch blocked at or near the disc. Ask which wording your examiners expect in the diagnosis.
2. **Vision cut-off for ischaemic CRVO.** The card follows Baidya ("counting fingers or worse"). Namrata and Aravind say "worse than 6/60". Know both.
3. **Conversion risk.** The card says 34% of non-ischaemic eyes become ischaemic by 3 years (Baidya). Namrata says about one-third within one year.
4. **IOP method in a known POAG patient shown as a fundus case.** The department's fundus sheets record non-contact tonometry, as the say-it does. Its glaucoma sheet records "Tn by GAT". Ask which to quote.

## G8 — Applanation tonometry

1. **Mire colour.** The card says green (Baidya, and the cross-card decision); Aravind says yellow. If an examiner says yellow, both books are on record.
2. **Where to write CCT.** On the glaucoma sheet the seniors struck CCT out of the clinical table, so the card now puts it under investigations. The department's fundus sheets, however, write CCT beside NCT. Confirm for glaucoma cases.
3. **Disinfection routine.** Your books give only the agents and strengths (70% isopropyl alcohol, 3% hydrogen peroxide, 1:10 hypochlorite). They do not give contact time, rinsing or drying. Ask the department's routine before the station.
4. **Default prism setting and slit-lamp magnification for non-astigmatic eyes.** These are not in your books, which give only the over-3-dioptre red-line rule. Ask what your department sets.

## G9 — Gonioscopy

1. **What the department's "modified Shaffer" grades mean.** The POAG sheet writes "III" in each quadrant; last year's exam sheet writes "SS". In Aravind's Shaffer table, grade 3 means "up to ciliary body", not the scleral spur. Ask which structure each department grade stands for. Write the structure in every quadrant either way.
2. **Room light and beam position.** The usual teaching is a dim room and a short, narrow beam kept off the pupil, because light constricts the pupil and can make a narrow angle look open. None of your three books says this (only Kanski does), so it is not on the card. "Why do gonioscopy in a dark room?" is a likely viva question; agree the answer with your seniors.
3. **Van Herick cut-off for "occludable".** Baidya and Namrata say peripheral depth < ¼ corneal thickness (grade 1) is potentially occludable. Namrata calls grades 0–2 "suspicious of angle closure", and the department slide calls 1:¼ (grade 2) "occludable". The card follows the books (< ¼).
4. **Which Goldmann lens for routine grading.** Baidya p.150 and Namrata p.180 name the two-mirror Goldmann (Namrata: use it first, to avoid pressure artefact). Baidya p.742 and the card describe the three-mirror. Both are indirect lenses that need coupling fluid; use the one your department keeps.

## G10 — Humphrey field

1. **How to say the normal MD.**
   - Baidya prints "0 dB to 2 dB" with no sign, but its own normal example reads −2.68 dB. The handout's normal example
     reads −1.45 dB.
   - The card says "within 2 dB of 0; more negative = worse".
   - Agree the exact phrasing your examiners expect: "close to zero, usually slightly negative".
2. **Reliability cut-offs differ between sources.**
   - The card uses Baidya: fixation losses < 20%, false positives > 15%, false negatives > 33%.
   - Aravind gives > 33% for both false positives and false negatives.
   - The imaging handout says all three should stay below 20%.
   - Check which figures your department quotes.
3. **Zone numbering.**
   - The card uses Baidya's 8 zones: grey scale is zone 4, raw data 5, GHT 7, global indices 8.
   - The imaging handout uses 7 zones: raw data and grey scale together are zone 3, total deviation 4, pattern deviation 5,
     global indices 6, GHT 7.
   - If an examiner says "zone 5", ask or name the plot rather than the number.
4. **Anderson–Patella criteria: one criterion or all three?** The books list three criteria, confirmed on two consecutive
   reliable fields, but never say whether one is enough or all three are needed. Ask which answer is expected.
5. **The name of the severity table.** The early/moderate/severe table is usually called the Hodapp–Parrish–Anderson
   classification. None of your three books names it, so the card leaves it unnamed. Confirm before you name it in the viva.

## G2 — POAG with trabeculectomy

1. **Antimetabolite strength and time.** The card now says 5-FU 50 mg/ml for 5 minutes and MMC 0.2–0.5 mg/ml for 1–5
   minutes. Your books also print other values: Aravind's trabeculectomy FAQ gives 5-FU at 30 mg/ml, and its
   neovascular and pigmentary glaucoma sections give MMC 0.2–0.4 mg/ml for 2 minutes. Ask what strength and exposure
   time the department uses, and quote that number.
2. **MMC or 5-FU as the routine agent.** The card follows Baidya, which uses MMC in almost all cases. Aravind uses
   intraoperative 5-FU in most primary trabeculectomies and keeps MMC for high-risk eyes. Ask which is the department's
   routine.
3. **"Failing" versus "failed" bleb.** None of the books defines where one ends and the other begins. The card treats a
   failing bleb as one showing signs of failure with rising IOP, still treatable by digital pressure or suture lysis. A
   failed bleb no longer filters (types 3 and 4). The choice changes your diagnosis line, so check how the department
   uses the two words.
4. **Bleb needling.** Needling revision of a failing or encapsulated bleb (with 5-FU or MMC) is not in your three
   practical books, so the card leaves it out. Examiners often ask about it, so ask whether they expect it and how the
   department does it.

## G3 — POAG with drainage device

1. **Phases after surgery: timing and order.**
   - Aravind's answer (Q39) is cut short in print. The page shows "Hypertensive phase" with "Transient ↑ IOP in immediate postoperative…" beneath it, and "Hypotensive phase", and then stops.
   - Its only full definition says the hypertensive phase is poor IOP control with a properly working **Baerveldt** device.
   - The card gives the two names and that definition only. Ask your seniors which comes first and when, for valved and for tied non-valved tubes.
2. **Anti-VEGF before surgery in NVG.**
   - The card says 2–3 days (Baidya, in NVG with vitreous haemorrhage, before vitrectomy, PRP and valve).
   - Namrata says usually 1 week, though it can be 14–48 hours.
   - Ask which window your department uses.
3. **Comparing outcomes.**
   - Your books say only three things:
     - The IOP fall is smaller than after trabeculectomy (Aravind).
     - Results are comparable in NVG (Namrata).
     - Valved and non-valved devices appear equal, except in Sturge–Weber syndrome, where the Ahmed does better than the Molteno (Baidya).
   - None of your books mentions any of the comparative trials of drainage devices, so ask for the department's standard answer to "tube or trabeculectomy?" and "Ahmed or Baerveldt?".
4. **Recording the tube.**
   - The books give only 1.5–2 mm, bevel up and parallel to the iris.
   - They give no number for the tube–cornea gap and no clock-hour convention.
   - Ask how the department writes the tube on its sheet.
5. **Imaging the tube (anterior segment OCT, UBM).**
   - Your books do not cover it.
   - Examiners may link it to your thesis on anterior segment OCT, so ask your seniors what answer they expect.
