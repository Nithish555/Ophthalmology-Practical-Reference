### Verified unchanged (the checks the brief asked for)
- **Ischaemic vs non-ischaemic table**:
  - Vision: moderate to severe loss vs counting fingers or worse (BAIDYA fitz 281).
  - RAPD: absent or mild vs > 0.7 log unit (BAIDYA fitz 281; NAMRATA fitz 235).
  - Capillary non-perfusion: < 10 vs > 10 disc areas (NAMRATA fitz 238, which calls it the CVOS definition; BAIDYA fitz 284 also says "disc areas"; BAIDYA fitz 282 table and ARAVIND fitz 385 write "DD"). The number agrees in all three books. "Disc areas" kept.
  - ERG: normal or mildly depressed (BAIDYA fitz 282); b-to-a ratio < 1 (BAIDYA fitz 286; NAMRATA fitz 240 Q13).
- **NVI, NVG and "100-day glaucoma"**:
  - NVI usually at 2–4 months, "100 days glaucoma" (NAMRATA fitz 240 Q5; Kanski fitz 524 agrees).
  - Ischaemic CRVO: NVI 49%, NVG 29% within the first 6 months (BAIDYA fitz 282–283).
  - One-third of eyes with NVI develop NVG (NAMRATA fitz 240; ARAVIND fitz 381 Q7, 33%).
  - Significant = NVI over 2 clock hours, or any NVA (NAMRATA fitz 240 Q7; ARAVIND fitz 387 Q39).
  - NVA without NVI in 12% (NAMRATA fitz 236).
- **CVOS**:
  - Three types; non-ischaemic about 75%; 34% convert by 3 years (BAIDYA fitz 281–282).
  - Follow-up monthly for the first 6 months, then tapered (NAMRATA fitz 240 Q8).
  - No prophylactic PRP; regression 56% vs 22% (BAIDYA fitz 287 Q19).
  - Grid laser gives no visual benefit (NAMRATA fitz 238; ARAVIND fitz 386–387).
  - "Undilated gonioscopy each visit" is kept as a supported synthesis, not a quoted sentence. CVOS waited for 2 clock hours of NVI on undilated gonioscopy (ARAVIND fitz 387 Q38 ii). The work-up includes an undilated slit-lamp check for NVA and NVI (BAIDYA fitz 283 Q13a). Undilated gonioscopy is "essential" (NAMRATA fitz 236).
- **Glaucoma–CRVO link**:
  - Open-angle glaucoma is first among the ocular associations (BAIDYA fitz 283 Q10).
  - Venous pressure must exceed IOP; stasis (Virchow's triad); lamina stretching; nasalisation (ARAVIND fitz 382 Q13; BAIDYA fitz 287 Q17; NAMRATA fitz 239 Q2).
  - CRVO causes NVG and steroid-induced glaucoma (ARAVIND fitz 382 Q14).
  - Angle closure can be cause or effect (NAMRATA fitz 236).
  - IOP over 22 mm Hg must be explained (NAMRATA fitz 236).
  - RAPD is unreliable with glaucomatous discs or miotic pupils (ARAVIND fitz 384 Q26); ERG has 80% sensitivity and specificity (ARAVIND fitz 385 Q35).
- **Management**:
  - Anti-VEGF is first-line (NAMRATA fitz 238); ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab (BAIDYA fitz 286).
  - Triamcinolone and the dexamethasone implant raise IOP and cause cataract (NAMRATA fitz 238; BAIDYA fitz 278).
  - Prompt PRP, not prophylactic unless ischaemic with follow-up impossible (BAIDYA fitz 286–287; NAMRATA fitz 239; ARAVIND fitz 387 Q41).
  - Anti-VEGF only temporises (NAMRATA fitz 239; BAIDYA fitz 286 Q15).
  - Antiglaucoma drugs, then trabeculectomy or drainage valve (BAIDYA fitz 286 Q14c).
- **Differentials**: BAIDYA fitz 281 table (OIS, NPDR); ARAVIND fitz 387 Q43 iv (collaterals cross the horizontal raphe); NAMRATA fitz 240 Q15.
- **Other viva answers**:
  - Ischaemic index 50% ≈ 10 disc areas (NAMRATA fitz 240 Q6).
  - Macular oedema is the commonest cause of visual loss (ARAVIND fitz 383 Q17); central scotoma is the commonest field defect (Q23).
  - Shunts carry retinal blood to the choroid (BAIDYA fitz 287 Q18); NVD are finer and form a network (NAMRATA fitz 237).

  - Fellow-eye gonioscopy for an occludable angle (NAMRATA fitz 237).

## 4. Confirm with your seniors
1. **What to call it.** Your department writes "hemi-CRVO", which the say-it speaks as "hemi-central retinal vein occlusion". In Kanski the umbrella term is "hemiretinal vein occlusion": "hemicentral" strictly means the less common dual-trunk variant, and "hemispheric" is a branch blocked at or near the disc. Ask which wording your examiners expect in the diagnosis.
2. **Vision cut-off for ischaemic CRVO.** The card follows Baidya ("counting fingers or worse"). Namrata and Aravind say "worse than 6/60". Know both.
3. **Conversion risk.** The card says 34% of non-ischaemic eyes become ischaemic by 3 years (Baidya). Namrata says about one-third within one year.
4. **IOP method in a known POAG patient shown as a fundus case.** The department's fundus sheets record non-contact tonometry, as the say-it does. Its glaucoma sheet records "Tn by GAT". Ask which to quote.
