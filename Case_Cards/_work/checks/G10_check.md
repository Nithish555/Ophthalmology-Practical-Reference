## 2. The sign of the normal MD (the point the brief asked about)
- **What the page prints.** BAIDYA fitz 206 (printed p.192) reads: "Normal value remains typically within **0 dB to 2 dB**.
  The MD value becomes more negative as the overall field worsens."
  - There is **no minus sign** before the 2. I confirmed this in the text layer and on a 6× render of the line.
  - So the writer quoted the page correctly.
- **Why the card cannot quote it as it stands.** "0 to 2 dB" on its own reads as a *positive* range, but loss makes MD
  negative. Both worked examples in the sources show a normal field with a slightly *negative* MD:
  - Baidya's own normal-field figure (Fig. 5.12a, "Normal HVF", fitz 207) prints **MD −2.68 dB (P < 2%)**, with the
    glaucoma hemifield test within normal limits and VFI 100%.
  - The imaging handout's sample printout (fitz24.jpg) prints **MD −1.45 dB**, within normal limits. Its zone 6 says "a
    more negative MD generally means more field loss".
- **Why "0 to −2 dB" is not used either.** It would add a sign that the page does not print.
- **What the card now says.** "Normal typically within 2 dB of 0; more negative = worse." This keeps the book's 2 dB figure
  and the book's direction. If asked, the candidate can say: "a normal MD is close to zero, usually slightly negative; it
  becomes more negative as the field worsens."

Verified without change, with sources:
- **Basics.** Static perimetry (dim light made brighter until seen) and the gold-standard status: BAIDYA fitz 195. Threshold
  seen 50% of the time: BAIDYA fitz 196; ARAVIND Q29.
- **Stimulus.** Goldmann size III, white, background 31.5 asb: BAIDYA fitz 198–199 (page image). 40 dB = 1 asb, 0 dB =
  10,000 asb, sensitivity proportional to dB: BAIDYA fitz 197 (page image).
- **Programmes.** 24-2 = 6°, 54 points; 30-2 = 6°, 76 points; 10-2 = 2°, 68 points, for advanced glaucoma: BAIDYA fitz 199
  (page-image table); ARAVIND Q32, Q34.
- **SITA.** Takes 50% of the time; little or no loss of accuracy: ARAVIND Q31; BAIDYA fitz 199.
- **The 8 zones and their order:** BAIDYA fitz 198.
- **Age, pupil and correction.** Correct age; pupil < 2 mm → dilate; refractive correction: BAIDYA fitz 198.
- **Reliability cut-offs.** Fixation losses < 20%; false positives > 15% (white scotoma); false negatives > 33% (clover leaf;
  fatigue, inattention or advanced damage); foveal threshold should match acuity: BAIDYA fitz 200 (page image). Baidya
  overrules Aravind's false-positive cut-off of > 33%.
- **Plots.** Grey scale (no statistics) and raw data (0 = absolute scotoma): BAIDYA fitz 200. Total deviation and the p < 2%
  meaning: BAIDYA fitz 203. Pattern deviation: the 7th best point is set to 0: BAIDYA fitz 204.
- **Glaucoma hemifield test.** 5 sectors, borderline p < 3%, outside normal limits p < 1%, and the other 3 results:
  BAIDYA fitz 204–205.
- **PSD and VFI.** PSD high = focal loss, low = no loss or diffuse loss: BAIDYA fitz 206. VFI: BAIDYA fitz 204.
- **Read reliability first.** Read the reliability indices first and the pattern on the pattern deviation plot; conclude
  with the NFL/ONH defect: imaging fitz 25.
- **Defect sequence and descriptions:** BAIDYA fitz 197; ARAVIND Q11, Q14, Q16–Q19.
- **Anderson and Patella criteria.** Cluster wording, PSD/CPSD p < 5%, GHT outside normal limits, two consecutive tests:

  - Trend analysis and glaucoma progression analysis: BAIDYA fitz 206.
  - Non-glaucomatous causes: imaging fitz 25.

## 5. Confirm with your seniors
1. **How to say the normal MD.**
   - Baidya prints "0 dB to 2 dB" with no sign, but its own normal example reads −2.68 dB. The handout's normal example
     reads −1.45 dB.
   - The card says "within 2 dB of 0; more negative = worse".
   - Agree the exact phrasing your examiners expect: "close to zero, usually slightly negative".
2. **Reliability cut-offs differ between sources.**
   - The card uses Baidya: fixation losses < 20%, false positives > 15%, false negatives > 33%.
   - Aravind gives > 33% for both false positives and false negatives.
   - The imaging handout says all three should stay below 20%.
   - Check which figures your department quotes.
3. **Zone numbering.**
   - The card uses Baidya's 8 zones: grey scale is zone 4, raw data 5, GHT 7, global indices 8.
   - The imaging handout uses 7 zones: raw data and grey scale together are zone 3, total deviation 4, pattern deviation 5,
     global indices 6, GHT 7.
   - If an examiner says "zone 5", ask or name the plot rather than the number.
4. **Anderson–Patella criteria: one criterion or all three?** The books list three criteria, confirmed on two consecutive
   reliable fields, but never say whether one is enough or all three are needed. Ask which answer is expected.
5. **The name of the severity table.** The early/moderate/severe table is usually called the Hodapp–Parrish–Anderson
   classification. None of your three books names it, so the card leaves it unnamed. Confirm before you name it in the viva.
