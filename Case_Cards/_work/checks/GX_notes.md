## Claims (every number, dose, mechanism, adverse effect, contraindication, setting, step, complication)

### Management model (say box)
- Target pressure first; TP = IP (1 − IP/100) − Z ± 2 — ARAVIND fitz 24–25 (model case sheet; also on G1)
- Single drug first, PG analogue first choice; review after 2 months; if not at target substitute or add — ARAVIND fitz 25
- PG preferred to beta-blocker as first line because of the latter's systemic side effects — BAIDYA fitz 170 (Q9)
- Laser trabeculoplasty when medicines cannot be used reliably (cost, memory, instillation, intolerance) — NAMRATA fitz 194
- PAC/PACG: LPI plus medical or surgical IOP control — NAMRATA fitz 200 (Table 1)
- Trabeculectomy: not at target on 3 drugs / progression despite maximal therapy; side effects; poor compliance; baseline > 40 mm Hg; one eye lost; difficult follow-up — NAMRATA fitz 194; ARAVIND fitz 302 Q7
- MMC "in almost all cases" — BAIDYA fitz 179 Q28
- GDD: failed filtering surgery with antimetabolites; conjunctiva too scarred — NAMRATA fitz 194

### Classification and IOP fall by class
- Classification: cholinergic (pilocarpine, carbachol; echothiophate…), adrenergic agonists (nonselective epinephrine, dipivefrine; selective α2 clonidine, apraclonidine, brimonidine), β-blockers (non-selective timolol, levobunolol, metipranolol, carteolol; selective betaxolol), CAIs (oral acetazolamide, methazolamide; topical dorzolamide, brinzolamide), hyperosmotics (oral glycerol, isosorbide; IV mannitol, urea), PGs, neuroprotectives — ARAVIND fitz 275 Q4; NAMRATA fitz 193 Table 1
- IOP fall: PG 25–32%; β-blockers 20–30%; adrenergic agonists 15–20%; parasympathomimetics 15–20%; CAIs 15–20% — ARAVIND fitz 283 Q59
- Advantages of medical therapy: serious side effects rare; most controlled with one or two drugs — ARAVIND fitz 275 Q2. Disadvantages: less effective than surgery, costly, effect wanes, prolonged use may hurt surgical success, compliance — ARAVIND fitz 275 Q3

### Prostaglandin analogues

- Travoprost 0.004% — NAMRATA fitz 193; ARAVIND fitz 281. Good effect on diurnal variation — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q47
- Bimatoprost 0.01%, 0.03% — NAMRATA fitz 193; 0.03% once daily — BAIDYA fitz 172. Prostamide; related to PGF2α — ARAVIND fitz 285 Q70, Q73; NAMRATA fitz 193 ("prostaglandin analogues (prostamide)")
- Tafluprost 0.0015% — NAMRATA fitz 193; ARAVIND fitz 49

- Mechanism: ↑ uveoscleral outflow — relaxation of ciliary muscle; remodelling of extracellular matrix of ciliary muscle — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q43; NAMRATA fitz 193
- Bedtime: peak effect 10–14 h after the drop; maximises efficacy; fewer symptoms of vascular dilatation — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q46
- Side effects: eyelid skin pigmentation; hypertrichosis and pigmentation of lashes; conjunctival hyperaemia; reactivation of dendritic keratitis; ↑ iris pigmentation (tyrosinase up-regulation in melanocytes); uveitis; cystoid macular oedema; no systemic side effects — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q45. - Contraindicated in uveitis (with miotics: ↑ blood–aqueous barrier breakdown) — ARAVIND fitz 285 Q72; ARAVIND fitz 266 (4.12)
- Contraindicated in NVG — BAIDYA fitz 193 (Q2)
- SWS: PG not beneficial (raised episcleral venous pressure); anterior uveal effusion with long-term use — BAIDYA fitz 189 Q3; NAMRATA fitz 204
- Angle recession: miotics ineffective (trabecular scarring); PG drug of choice — ARAVIND fitz 243 (4.8 Q39)

### Beta-blockers
- Timolol 0.25–0.5% twice daily, 12–24 h — BAIDYA fitz 171 (image). Timolol 0.5% bd — ARAVIND fitz 49, 277
- Betaxolol 0.5% twice daily, 12 h, fewer side effects than timolol — BAIDYA fitz 171; only cardioselective (β1), minimal respiratory side effects — ARAVIND fitz 276 Q10, 278 Q23
- Levobunolol 0.5% 1–2 times daily; carteolol 1% 1–2 times daily — BAIDYA fitz 171–172 (image). Levobunolol 0.5% ≈ timolol 0.5% — ARAVIND fitz 278 Q22
- Mechanism: inhibit cAMP production in ciliary epithelium → aqueous production ↓ 20–50%, IOP ↓ 20–30% — ARAVIND fitz 277 Q13
- Morning dosing: reduces aqueous production more in the morning; physiological fall in aqueous flow at night — ARAVIND fitz 277 Q14; blunts early-morning rise and avoids systemic hypotension in sleep — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q67
- Short-term escape and long-term drift (receptor response to constant antagonist exposure) — ARAVIND fitz 277 Q16
- Ocular side effects: allergy, punctate epithelial erosions, decreased tear secretion — ARAVIND fitz 277 Q17; irritation, diplopia, ptosis — BAIDYA fitz 171; irritation and dry eyes — NAMRATA fitz 193; blepharitis with timolol — BAIDYA fitz 192
- Systemic: bradycardia, hypotension, heart failure; bronchospasm, aggravated asthma; sleep disorders, depression, forgetfulness, rarely hallucinations — ARAVIND fitz 277 Q18; headache, dizziness, bronchospasm, bradycardia, hypotension — BAIDYA fitz 171
- Contraindications: congestive cardiac failure, 2nd/3rd-degree heart block, bradycardia, asthma, COPD — ARAVIND fitz 276 Q11; NAMRATA fitz 193
- On systemic β-blockers: topical β-blockers work sub-optimally; avoid as first line — NAMRATA fitz 190
- Reduce systemic absorption: lacrimal occlusion after instillation; closing the eyes for 3 minutes — ARAVIND fitz 277 Q19
- Gel-forming solution: stays longer, better absorption, day-long control, better compliance — ARAVIND fitz 278 Q21
- NTG: IOP-lowering agents "must include betaxolol" (optic-nerve blood flow) — BAIDYA fitz 175 Q6

### Alpha-2 agonists
- Brimonidine 0.1%, 0.15%, TID sometimes BD — NAMRATA fitz 193; 0.2% tartrate, 0.15% purite — ARAVIND fitz 278 Q24; 0.2% bd — ARAVIND fitz 49; 0.2% thrice a day (acute attack) — ARAVIND fitz 238
- Apraclonidine 0.5–1% twice daily, 5–8 h — BAIDYA fitz 171 (image); 1% for short-term IOP rise after laser (minimal blood–brain barrier penetration) — ARAVIND fitz 279 Q34; 0.5% in acute attack — ARAVIND fitz 238
- Brimonidine mechanism: ↓ aqueous formation, ↑ uveoscleral outflow, neuroprotection — ARAVIND fitz 278 Q25; "initial reduction of aqueous with subsequent increase in outflow" — NAMRATA fitz 193
- Apraclonidine mechanism: ↓ aqueous production, ↑ trabecular outflow, ↓ episcleral venous pressure — ARAVIND fitz 279 Q30
- Brimonidine side effects: allergic blepharoconjunctivitis, dry mouth, somnolence — ARAVIND fitz 279 Q32; allergic dermatitis of lid margin with long use — BAIDYA fitz 163; systemic bradycardia, vasovagal attack, palpitation, postural hypotension; abdominal pain, nausea — ARAVIND fitz 279 Q35
- Apraclonidine side effects: hyperaemia, mydriasis, dryness; diarrhoea, bradycardia, insomnia — BAIDYA fitz 171
- Contraindicated in children (cross the blood–brain barrier) — ARAVIND fitz 278 Q20; < 2 years: CNS effects and respiratory arrest — NAMRATA fitz 193; respiratory depression, coma, hypotension, bradycardia — BAIDYA fitz 186 Q9; infants: somnolence, hypotension, seizures, apnoea — ARAVIND fitz 279 Q32
- Caution: cerebral or coronary insufficiency, postural hypotension, renal or hepatic failure — NAMRATA fitz 193
- Avoid (with miotics) after a drainage device: increase vascularisation of the bleb capsule — ARAVIND fitz 326 Q36
- Apraclonidine "especially useful" in Posner–Schlossman syndrome — ARAVIND fitz 266 (not on card; word budget)

### Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors
- Dorzolamide 2% three times daily — BAIDYA fitz 172 (image); ARAVIND fitz 280 Q40
- Brinzolamide 1% twice a day — ARAVIND fitz 280 Q40; "TID sometimes BD" (both topicals) — NAMRATA fitz 193; bd or tid — ARAVIND fitz 49
- Acetazolamide 250 mg tablets 2–4 times daily, 8–12 h — BAIDYA fitz 172 (image); 250 mg 6-hourly or 500 mg sustained-release capsule twice a day — ARAVIND fitz 280 Q38
- Methazolamide 50 mg 2–3 times daily — BAIDYA fitz 172 (not on card)
- Mechanism: inhibit carbonic anhydrase II in ciliary epithelium → ↓ aqueous production — ARAVIND fitz 279 Q36
- Topical side effects: burning, stinging, periorbital dermatitis, superficial punctate keratitis; systemic thrombocytopenia — ARAVIND fitz 280–281 Q41; BAIDYA fitz 172 (burning, stinging)
- Oral side effects: transient myopia; metabolic acidosis, potassium depletion; GI upset, metallic taste, nausea; nocturia, renal stones, impotence; paraesthesia, drowsiness; agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia; Stevens–Johnson syndrome — ARAVIND fitz 280 Q39; BAIDYA fitz 172; NAMRATA fitz 193 (paraesthesia, renal stones, loss of appetite and taste, lassitude)
- Avoid: renal failure, renal transplant, sulpha allergy, chronic liver disease — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q42; sulphonamide allergy — BAIDYA fitz 177

### Miotics
- Pilocarpine 0.5–4% 2–4 times daily, duration 8 h — BAIDYA fitz 170 (image); 0.5/1/2% usually QID — NAMRATA fitz 193; 2% od–qid — ARAVIND fitz 48
- Mechanism POAG: contraction of longitudinal ciliary muscle pulls scleral spur, tightens TM, ↑ outflow; PACG: miosis pulls peripheral iris from TM, relieves pupillary block — ARAVIND fitz 276 Q5
- Side effects: browache, dim vision in low light (miosis), induced myopia, lacrimation (punctal stenosis); conjunctival congestion, ↑ inflammation, cataract, iris pigment cysts, retinal holes and detachment — ARAVIND fitz 276 Q7; miosis, follicular conjunctivitis, ciliary spasm; systemic salivation, urination — BAIDYA fitz 170; headaches in young patients — NAMRATA fitz 193
- Contraindicated: NVG, uveitic, phacolytic glaucoma — ARAVIND fitz 276 Q6; NVG — BAIDYA fitz 193
- Poorly tolerated by young patients (pigmentary glaucoma); higher retinal detachment — ARAVIND fitz 257
- Not with PG: pilocarpine contracts ciliary muscle, ↓ uveoscleral outflow — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q64
- Stop pilocarpine before a drainage device (inflammation) — ARAVIND fitz 322 Q15

### Hyperosmotics
- Mannitol 20% IV 1–2 g/kg over 20–30 min — ARAVIND fitz 238; 1–2 g/kg — ARAVIND fitz 283 Q53. (1.4 table: 5 ml/kg over 30–60 min — ARAVIND fitz 50; Namrata 1.5 g/kg, 200 ml, 30 min — NAMRATA fitz 200)
- Glycerol 50% oral 1–1.5 g/kg — ARAVIND fitz 238, 283; not in diabetes — ARAVIND fitz 238
- Isosorbide 45% oral 1–2 g/kg; safe in diabetics — ARAVIND fitz 283 Q53–54
- Mechanism: raise plasma osmolarity; water leaves the eye (mainly vitreous); vitreous volume falls — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q48
- Indications: acute glaucoma, preparation for surgery, malignant glaucoma — ARAVIND fitz 281 Q49
- Contraindications: anuria, severe dehydration, severe cardiac decompensation, pulmonary oedema — ARAVIND fitz 282 Q50; rule out low blood pressure and heart disease before IV mannitol — ARAVIND fitz 253
- Side effects: rebound IOP rise, intraocular haemorrhage; nausea, vomiting; thirst, chills, confusion; diuresis, dehydration; angina, pulmonary oedema, heart failure; hyperglycaemia — ARAVIND fitz 282 Q51
- After IV mannitol, do not get up at once: hypotension, rarely coning — ARAVIND fitz 282 Q52
- Mannitol IV drug of choice: less irritating to vessels, usable in diabetes and renal failure; disadvantages large volume, dehydration, diuresis — ARAVIND fitz 283 Q55–56

### Drugs in the acute attack (all ARAVIND fitz 238 unless stated)
- Above 50 mm Hg: iris ischaemia paralyses sphincter, miotics fail; first lower IOP
- Mannitol 20% IV 1–2 g/kg over 20–30 min
- No nausea or vomiting: acetazolamide 250 mg four times a day, or glycerol 50% 1–1.5 g/kg (rule out diabetes); apraclonidine 0.5%
- 250–500 mg stat (Namrata) — NAMRATA fitz 200
- IOP below 40 mm Hg: pilocarpine 2% four times over 30 min, then 6-hourly
- Timolol 0.5% twice a day with brimonidine 0.2% three times a day, 10 min apart
- Prednisolone acetate 1% four times a day; ibuprofen 500 mg twice a day
- Hourly review; gonioscopy both eyes when cornea clear; central corneal indentation
- Quiet eye, clear cornea: Nd:YAG PI both eyes; hazy cornea, closure < 2/3: surgical iridectomy; > 2/3 or persistent inflammation: primary trabeculectomy
- Sphincter ischaemic above 40 mm Hg — BAIDYA fitz 166 Q8

### Newer drugs (extra)
- ROCK inhibitors (Y-27632, H-1152): increase outflow via cytoskeleton of TM / Schlemm's canal cells; topically active, great IOP-lowering efficacy; caution — kinases in many tissues — ARAVIND fitz 287 Q4 (also fitz 51)
- Netarsudil 0.02% and ripasudil 0.4%: Rho-kinase inhibitors tried in corneal oedema (Fuchs', post-cataract) — BAIDYA fitz 124 (cornea chapter; no glaucoma use stated)
- Cannabinoids: systemic hypotension limits use — ARAVIND fitz 286 Q2
- Neuroprotection: memantine (NMDA antagonist; blocks excess glutamate, calcium-mediated apoptosis), brimonidine, nimodipine (calcium-channel blocker; evidence does not support routine use) — ARAVIND fitz 289–290; NAMRATA fitz 194 (memantine, brimonidine, flunarizine, nimodipine, antioxidants, NOS inhibitors)

### Combinations
- Problems of 2+ separate drugs: non-adherence, poor compliance, difficult scheduling, washout effect, preservative toxicity, cost — ARAVIND fitz 283 Q60
- Parameters: need, superior IOP lowering, complementary mechanisms, similar dosing schedule — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q61
- Timolol 0.5% + dorzolamide 2%: lowers more than either alone; additive although both are suppressants; no electrolyte imbalance — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q62
- Timolol + PG: ↑ uveoscleral outflow plus ↓ inflow; 13–37% IOP reduction — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q63
- PG + brimonidine; PG + topical CAI — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q65
- Advantages: simple dosing, adherence, lower IOP, less preservative toxicity, less systemic toxicity, economical — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q66
- Disadvantages: fixed timing (timolol best in the morning, PG at bedtime), alters individual potency, components' efficacy not confirmed separately — ARAVIND fitz 284–285 Q67
- Never PG + pilocarpine — ARAVIND fitz 284 Q64
- After YAG PI: PG + timolol, dorzolamide + timolol, brimonidine + timolol, pilocarpine + timolol — ARAVIND fitz 285 Q68; uveitic: dorzolamide + timolol, brimonidine + timolol — Q69
- PXG: monotherapy usually insufficient; combination advisable — NAMRATA fitz 223

### Lasers
Laser peripheral iridotomy (consistent with G4)
- Nd:YAG: pilocarpine; apraclonidine or brimonidine and topical anaesthetic before; 11–1 o'clock (some 3 or 9), outer third, crypt; 2–5 mJ; 1–3 pulses; gush of pigment debris; optimal size 150–200 µm; retreat after a few days or another site; second apraclonidine drop at end; oral acetazolamide if advanced damage or high IOP; potent topical steroid four times daily for 1 week — BAIDYA fitz 455
- Complications: IOP rise, iritis, lens damage, corneal burn, bleeding (stop by pressing the contact lens) — BAIDYA fitz 455; uveitis, hyphaema, focal cataract, posterior synechiae, glare; rarely malignant glaucoma — ARAVIND fitz 295 Q12; NAMRATA fitz 202 Q6
- Pilocarpine 1%, proparacaine 0.5%, Abraham lens +55 D; IOP at 1 h (rise ≥ 8 mm Hg or > 30 mm Hg); prednisolone acetate 1% four times a day for 5–7 days — NAMRATA fitz 201–202
- Patency: lens capsule seen through it; retro-illumination not a sure sign — NAMRATA fitz 202; ARAVIND fitz 294
- Gonioscopy at 1 week — ARAVIND fitz 294
- Photodisruption, all iris colours, near infrared 1064 nm; preferred over argon — ARAVIND fitz 293 Q8; argon: thermal, needs pigment, more energy, more late closures — ARAVIND fitz 293 Q6

Laser trabeculoplasty
- SLT: frequency-doubled (532 nm) Q-switched Nd:YAG; SLT lens or Goldmann 3-mirror; 0.5–1.5 mJ; 400 µm; 3 nanoseconds; target intracellular melanin of pigmented TM; end point tiny bubbles — BAIDYA fitz 456 (image)
- SLT 50–100 adjacent spots over 180° — ARAVIND fitz 258 (4.10); 50–100 adjacent spots — ARAVIND fitz 297
- ALT: argon; Goldmann 3-mirror; 200–1200 mW (printed "MW"); 50 µm; 0.1 s; junction of pigmented and depigmented TM; blanching or tiny bubble; 40–50 spots over 180°, 4° apart — BAIDYA fitz 456 (image)
- Mechanism: TM shrinkage stretching adjacent areas; IL-1β, TNF-α stimulate matrix metalloproteinases — BAIDYA fitz 180 Q34, 456. SLT targets pigmented melanin cells, no thermal transfer to surrounding tissue; macrophages, cytokines, MMP ↑ porosity — ARAVIND fitz 297 Q22. ALT mechanical (collagen shrinkage) and biological (macrophages) theories — ARAVIND fitz 296 Q17
- Indications: POAG, pigmentary, exfoliation, steroid-induced; contraindications: uveitic, ICE, NVG, synechial angle closure, angle recession — BAIDYA fitz 180 Q35, 456
- SLT indications include failed ALT, poorly compliant patient — ARAVIND fitz 297 Q21
- Advantages: good IOP control for 3–5 years; half need additional drops; best results in pseudoexfoliation; pigmentary good initial response not sustained — BAIDYA fitz 456
- Complications: transient IOP rise, low-grade uveitis, pigment dispersion, hyphaema, rarely persistent rise needing filtering surgery, PAS — BAIDYA fitz 456
- ALT: average reduction 30%; 50% controlled at 5 years — ARAVIND fitz 296 Q19; encapsulated blebs up to 3 times more after ALT — ARAVIND fitz 296 Q20
- Laser trabeculoplasty ineffective in angle recession (distorted anatomy, TM scarring) — NAMRATA fitz 216–217

Cyclophotocoagulation
- Transscleral: local anaesthesia; 1.5–2 s, 1500–2000 mW (printed "MW"), fixed spot; 12–24 burns behind the limbus over 360°, avoiding 3 and 9 o'clock neurovascular bundles; fewer shots for eyes with good vision (more sessions); strong topical steroid 2 weeks, antibiotic and cycloplegic 2–3 days; oral NSAID 2 days — BAIDYA fitz 455
- Diode settings 1500 mW (dark) / 1750 mW (light), 2000 ms, 270°, temporal spared; pops not a prerequisite; continue all drugs except miotics; review 1, 3, 6 weeks — ARAVIND fitz 299 Q34 (not used for the numbers; Baidya newer)
- Indications: refractory glaucoma uncontrolled on maximal therapy with failed filtering or high failure risk (aphakic, pseudophakic, NVG); inflammation; after PKP — ARAVIND fitz 299 Q33; painful blind eye; patient too sick for incisional surgery — ARAVIND fitz 330 Q12
- Mechanism: ↓ aqueous production (destroys ciliary epithelium of pars plicata; ↓ perfusion); ↑ pars plana / transscleral outflow — ARAVIND fitz 330 Q13
- Diode over Nd:YAG: less energy, small durable portable, greater melanin absorption, quick, now commonest cyclodestructive procedure — ARAVIND fitz 330–331 Q15–16
- Complications: pain, inflammation, cystoid macular oedema, hypotony; uncommon: hypopyon, scleral thinning, graft failure, malignant glaucoma, suprachoroidal haemorrhage, hyphaema, sympathetic ophthalmia, phthisis bulbi — ARAVIND fitz 299–300 Q35; hypotony, retinal detachment — BAIDYA fitz 187
- Retreatment interval 1 month; 2/3 need no retreatment — ARAVIND fitz 331 Q21
- Endocyclophotocoagulation: pars plana (NVG with vitrectomy) or limbal (with cataract extraction) — ARAVIND fitz 298 Q26

### Surgery
Trabeculectomy (consistent with G2)
- Partial-thickness filtering operation; fistula at limbus; aqueous around scleral flap edges into subconjunctival space; leaves by transconjunctival filtration or lymphatics and vessels — ARAVIND fitz 301–302 Q3, Q6
- Misnomer: block need not include TM; peripheral posterior keratectomy — ARAVIND fitz 301 Q5
- Partial over full thickness: uniform IOP control; less hypotony and hypotony maculopathy; fewer hyphaema, iris prolapse, shallow AC; less infection — ARAVIND fitz 301 Q4
- Before surgery: stop timolol 2 weeks and CAIs 1–2 days before; pilocarpine 1% three times 1 h before; aspirin stopped 5 days; NVG: PRP first — ARAVIND fitz 303 Q11
- Steps: bridle suture; conjunctival flap (limbus or fornix based); scleral flap 3 × 4 mm, 2/3 thickness; MMC; dissect to clear cornea; paracentesis; enter AC behind hinge; Kelly punch; ostium 1.5 × 2 mm; basal PI wider than ostium; 10-0 nylon; reform AC with BSS; 8-0 vicryl; atropine 1%, subconjunctival betamethasone and gentamicin — ARAVIND fitz 303–304 Q12
- Site superotemporal or superonasal (keeps other site for repeat; anterior ciliary vessel 3–4 mm from limbus at 12 o'clock) — BAIDYA fitz 179 Q26
- Plain trabeculectomy lowers IOP by 41% — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q64
- Complications: intraoperative (buttonhole, flap problems, haemorrhage, lens damage, vitreous loss, choroidal effusion); early (hypotony and flat AC: leak, overfiltration, choroidal detachment; raised IOP and flat AC: malignant glaucoma, pupillary block, suprachoroidal haemorrhage; raised IOP and deep AC: fistula block, failing bleb); late (bleb infection and endophthalmitis, cataract, leak, Tenon's cyst, failure) — ARAVIND fitz 311–312 Q50; commonest problem shallow AC — ARAVIND fitz 307 Q34; commonest cause of failure subconjunctival fibrosis — ARAVIND fitz 308 Q41
- Relative contraindications: likely to respond to less invasive treatment; failed trabeculectomy; severely scarred conjunctiva; NVG with active neovascularisation; uveitic glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 178 Q23

Antimetabolites (consistent with G2)
- MMC: alkylating agent; all phases of cell cycle; inhibits fibroblast proliferation and vascular ingrowth; much more potent than 5-FU — ARAVIND fitz 314 Q68, 315 Q4
- MMC 0.2–0.5 mg/ml for 1–5 min; 4.5 × 4.5 mm sponge between conjunctiva/Tenon's and episclera; conjunctival edges over the sponge; rinse BSS; relatively tight flap — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q65, 315–316 Q5
- 5-FU: pyrimidine analogue; S phase; inhibits fibroblast proliferation, not attachment or migration — ARAVIND fitz 314 Q67, 317 Q7
- 5-FU intraoperative 50 mg/ml for 5 min — ARAVIND fitz 252, 253, 258 (4.9, 4.10); 30 mg/ml — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q65 (disagreement, G2 chose 50)
- 5-FU subconjunctival 5 mg; undiluted 50 mg/ml (many dilute to 10 mg/ml); 30-gauge needle; 90°–180° away; twice daily week 1, daily week 2; 21 injections (105 mg) — ARAVIND fitz 317 Q8
- Placed in sub-Tenon's space (fibrosis commonest cause of failure) — ARAVIND fitz 314 Q69
- MMC indications: almost all; essential after failed trabeculectomy, conjunctival or cataract surgery, secondary glaucoma, young or dark-skinned, drops (sympathomimetics) > 3 years — BAIDYA fitz 179 Q28
- MMC side effects: cystic overfiltering bleb (commonest), late leaking bleb, corneal decompensation, scleral and corneal necrosis, hypotony, endophthalmitis — BAIDYA fitz 179 Q29
- Complications more with MMC: hypotony, bleb leak, bleb infection; punctal occlusion more with 5-FU; endothelial toxicity MMC, epithelial 5-FU — ARAVIND fitz 309–310 Q44
- Choice: 5-FU when MMC complications before or risk of hypotony maculopathy; MMC in high risk (aphakic/pseudophakic, failed filter, neovascularisation, uveitis; failed 5-FU in fellow eye) — ARAVIND fitz 318 Q10
- Prevent toxicity: thorough irrigation, watertight closure, careful dissection — ARAVIND fitz 318 Q13
- Long-term drops cause failure: more fibroblasts and inflammatory cells, fewer goblet cells — ARAVIND fitz 314 Q70

Combined surgery
- Indications: visually significant cataract with inadequate IOP control, medication intolerance or poor compliance, advanced glaucoma, marginally functioning filter — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q61
- Advantages: earlier visual rehabilitation; fewer early IOP spikes; single intervention; compliance — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q62
- Disadvantages: long-term IOP control questionable (episcleral scarring); more hyphaema, uveitis, shallow AC, hypotony — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q63
- Two-stage: greater IOP fall (plain trab 41%); fewer complications — ARAVIND fitz 313 Q64
- Cataract alone: IOP acceptable on 1–2 drugs; no significant field loss; older; no compliance or intolerance issue; higher target. Advantages: single, easier, shorter, fewer complications, temporal clear cornea preserves conjunctiva.
