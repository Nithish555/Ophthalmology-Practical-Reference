Verified unchanged, with the points the brief asked about:
- **Acute-attack protocol** (ARAVIND fitz 238): mannitol 20% IV 1–2 g/kg over 20–30 minutes; acetazolamide 250 mg four times a day (250–500 mg stat from NAMRATA fitz 200); glycerol 50% 1–1.5 g/kg after ruling out diabetes; apraclonidine 0.5%; pilocarpine 2% four times over 30 minutes then 6-hourly once IOP < 40 mm Hg; timolol 0.5% twice daily + brimonidine 0.2% three times daily; prednisolone acetate 1% four times a day; ibuprofen 500 mg twice daily; hourly review; central corneal indentation; Nd:YAG PI both eyes; surgical iridectomy if cornea hazy and closure < 2/3; primary trabeculectomy if closure > 2/3 or persistent inflammation. Baidya gives no doses (fitz 162–166; NVG section has none either).
- **Nd:YAG technique, other steps**: pilocarpine 1% and proparacaine 0.5% (NAMRATA fitz 201; Baidya fitz 455 gives no strength); brimonidine/apraclonidine before (ARAVIND fitz 294, BAIDYA fitz 455); Abraham lens +55 D (NAMRATA fitz 201; Baidya names no power); site peripheral/outer third, crypt, 11–1 o'clock (NAMRATA fitz 201, BAIDYA fitz 455); spike ≥ 8 mm Hg or > 30 mm Hg, IOP at 1 hour (NAMRATA fitz 202); prednisolone acetate 1% four times a day for 5–7 days (NAMRATA fitz 202; compatible with Baidya's "potent topical steroid four times daily for 1 week"); gonioscopy at 1 week (ARAVIND fitz 294).
- **Provocative tests** (ARAVIND fitz 234–235, read from page images): mydriatic (0.5% tropicamide, pupil 4–6 mm) and dark room (90 minutes, awake) positive at IOP rise > 8 mm Hg or closure on gonioscopy; prone 45–60 minutes (Perkins); prone dark room; phenylephrine 10% + pilocarpine 2%; triple test (> 10 mm Hg with closed angles — not on card).
- **Plateau iris** (BAIDYA fitz 165–166, ARAVIND fitz 230–231, NAMRATA fitz 199): deep central AC, flat iris, double hump on indentation, occludable despite patent PI, younger; PI first, argon iridoplasty if confirmed; configuration = no closure.
- **UBM vs AS-OCT** (IMAGING fitz 22, ARAVIND fitz 227–228): AS-OCT non-contact, quick, 360°, lens vault, cannot see behind the pigmented iris epithelium; UBM 50–100 MHz, axial resolution 25 µm, 4–5 mm penetration, ciliary body/sulcus/zonules, works through corneal oedema; echogenic/hypoechoic/echolucent (UBM) vs hyperreflective/hyporeflective (OCT); radial and transverse scans. (Baidya fitz 729 mentions "prototype models 50 to 80 MHz" — a statement about prototypes, not a definition; 50–100 MHz kept.)

## 4. Confirm with your seniors
1. **Nd:YAG iridotomy settings** — the three books differ: Baidya 2024 (now on the card) 2–5 mJ, 1–3 pulses, optimal
   size 150–200 µm; Namrata 4–8 mJ, at least 200 µm (preferably 500 µm); Aravind 3–8 mJ, 300–500 µm. Ask what your
   department's laser is set at, and be ready to say "2 to 5 millijoules per Baidya; some texts use up to 8".
2. **ISGEO extent of iridotrabecular contact** — Baidya (card): three or more quadrants, almost 270°; Aravind: more than
   270°; Namrata: more than 180°. As this is your thesis area, check which definition your thesis and your guide use,
   and know that the other one exists.
3. **Writing the gonioscopy cross in the exam** — the card writes the deepest structure seen in each quadrant
   (as on last year's exam sheet). Your department's POAG sheet writes a Roman numeral "by modified Shaffer's grading";
   ask which the examiners expect, and write the structure too.
4. **Vogt's triad** — Baidya and Namrata (card): glaukomflecken, sectoral iris atrophy, Krukenberg spindle. Aravind:
   glaukomflecken, patches of iris atrophy, slightly dilated non-reacting pupil. Know both.
5. **Proof of LPI patency** — the department slide calls a light reflex on retro-illumination "patent"; Namrata says
   retro-illumination is not a sure sign and seeing the anterior lens capsule confirms it (Aravind: lens capsule must be
   seen on gonioscopy). The card presents with the department wording and keeps the caveat as a trap; confirm which
   your examiners want said first.
