### Verdict on the writer's flagged choices
- **Angle pigment (blotchy in PXF, uniform band in pigment dispersion): kept, correct.** Supported by BAIDYA fitz 181 and 183
  (Sampaolesi's line) and NAMRATA fitz 223–225. Aravind 4.11 Q15 (fitz 261) prints the reverse, which I confirmed on the
  rendered page. Q15 is the outlier: Aravind's own Q10 B (fitz 260, "uneven") and section 4.10 Q4 (fitz 255: pigmentary
  glaucoma has "dense, homogenous" pigment, with Sampaolesi's line anterior to Schwalbe's line) agree with the card.
- **Transillumination defects at the pupillary margin: kept, correct, with stronger support than the notes give.**
  Namrata (fitz 221) is not alone. ARAVIND fitz 255 (4.10 Q3: "in contrast to PXF glaucoma, where it is seen at the
  pupillary margins") and fitz 257 (4.10 Q11 table: "peripupillary") also say so. Only Aravind 4.11 Q10 C ii (fitz 260)
  says mid-peripheral.
- **Laterality: kept.** "PXG usually unilateral" is NAMRATA fitz 224 Table 2, which outranks ARAVIND fitz 259 Q8 ("PXF
  glaucoma tends to be bilateral"). "Glaucoma capsulare usually unilateral" (Aravind Q5) agrees. "Syndrome mostly
  bilateral" comes from Aravind Q7 only and is not directly contradicted, so it stays (see Confirm with seniors #1).
- **Sampaolesi's line wording: correct.** "Dark, dense, scalloped band on or anterior to Schwalbe's line; not exclusive to
  PXF (pigment dispersion, chronic inflammation)" matches BAIDYA fitz 183 Q6. Namrata's "along Schwalbe's line" is covered.
- **Drug classes only (strengths on G1): acceptable.** NAMRATA fitz 223 gives classes only.

## 3. Items not verified and removed
- None. Every fact on the card was found in at least one of the three books, or comes from the department's own sheets
  (house wording, last-year list). Retained with a note, not removed:
  - "Classic PXF → the three zones": the writer's link between ARAVIND fitz 261 Q14 and fitz 260 Q10 E. All three books
    describe the classic capsular picture as the three-zone pattern.
  - "Arshinoff soft-shell technique": NAMRATA fitz 224 misprints the eponym as "Ashrinoff's". The technique is also in
    BAIDYA fitz 751 (no eponym).
  - "a heart check" (counselling): advice drawn from the systemic associations in BAIDYA fitz 181, not a stated fact.
  - "Diamox": kept as printed (NAMRATA fitz 224). None of the three books links the brand to its generic name.
  - "No coloured haloes → rules out angle closure": the CARD_SPEC §4 example. The PXF angle-closure mechanism itself is in
    ARAVIND fitz 262 and NAMRATA fitz 221.

## 4. Confirm with your seniors
1. **Laterality.** Aravind (Q8, fitz 259) says "glaucoma capsulare" is usually unilateral but "PXF glaucoma" tends to be
   bilateral. Namrata (Table 2, fitz 224) says PXG is usually unilateral, and the card follows Namrata. The card's
   "syndrome mostly bilateral" comes only from Aravind (Q7). The newer books describe unilateral cupping and use
   "bilateral" to separate pigmentary glaucoma from PXF. Ask which answer your examiners expect.
2. **Age of PXF.** The card says "after 50, mostly 7th decade" (newest book). Namrata also says "commonly after 40" and
   "7th–8th decade", and its Table 3 cell prints "60 years" with no sign. Aravind says 60–70 in one place and "5th decade
   and older" in another. PXG onset "over 60" is Namrata Table 2.
3. **Age of pigment dispersion.** G6 says 30–50 years (Namrata Table 3, PXF chapter). Namrata's POAG chapter (fitz 192)
   gives 20–30 years for pigmentary glaucoma, Aravind says "3rd decade", and card G1 writes "Age 20–30". Agree one figure
   for both cards.
4. **Aravind Q15 (fitz 261)** prints the angle-pigment comparison the opposite way: PXF uniform and not reaching
   Schwalbe's line; pigmentary irregular and anterior to Schwalbe's line. The card follows Baidya, Namrata and Aravind's
   own Q10 and section 4.10. If an examiner quotes Aravind Q15, explain the newer books' position.
5. **Which quadrant is most pigmented in PXF.** Namrata (fitz 222) says "more prominent in the superior quadrant". Only one
   book says this and it is not on the card. Check what your teachers expect before quoting a quadrant.
