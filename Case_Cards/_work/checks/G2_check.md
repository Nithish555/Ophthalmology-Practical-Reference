### Verified unchanged: the points the brief singled out

**Mitomycin C (MMC)**
- Alkylating agent; acts in all phases; much more potent than 5-FU. Aravind fitz 314 Q68 and fitz 315 4.19 Q4.
- 0.2–0.5 mg/ml for 1–5 min. Aravind fitz 313 Q65 and fitz 315 4.19 Q5 (image checked).
- Method: a 4.5 × 4.5 mm sponge placed between conjunctiva/Tenon's capsule and episclera; flap edges pulled over it;
  rinse with BSS; relatively tight scleral flap. Applied after the flap is outlined and partly dissected. Aravind fitz
  315–316 4.19 Q5.
- Used "in almost all cases". Essential after failed trabeculectomy, conjunctival or cataract surgery, secondary glaucoma,
  in younger or dark-skinned patients, and after drops for over 3 years. Baidya fitz 179 Q28.
- Commonest side effect: cystic bleb with overfiltration. Baidya fitz 179 Q29.
- Complications are more common with MMC than with 5-FU; MMC gives endothelial toxicity. Aravind fitz 309–310 Q44.

**5-fluorouracil (5-FU)**
- Pyrimidine analogue; acts in the S phase. Aravind fitz 317 4.19 Q7 and fitz 314 Q67.
- Injection regime: 5 mg subconjunctival dose; 30-gauge needle; 90°–180° from the trabeculectomy site; twice daily in
  week 1, once daily in week 2; 21 injections (105 mg). Aravind fitz 317 4.19 Q8 (image checked).
- Epithelial toxicity; punctal occlusion more common than with MMC. Aravind fitz 310.
- Used after MMC complications in a previous surgery, or when there is a risk of hypotony maculopathy. Aravind fitz 318
  4.19 Q10 ii.

**Surgical steps** (Aravind fitz 303–304 Q12, images checked)
- Bridle suture.
- Scleral flap based at the limbus, 3 mm radially × 4 mm circumferentially, incisions through 2/3 of scleral thickness.
- Dissection to clear cornea, then paracentesis.
- Anterior chamber entered behind the flap hinge; block excised with the Kelly Descemet's membrane punch; internal
  ostium 1.5 × 2 mm.
- Peripheral iridectomy, basal and wider than the ostium. Aravind fitz 305 Q25; Baidya fitz 178 Q25.
- Scleral flap closed with interrupted 10-0 nylon; anterior chamber reformed with BSS.
- Conjunctiva closed with 8-0 vicryl, watertight. Aravind fitz 318 4.19 Q13.
- Atropine 1%, with subconjunctival betamethasone and gentamicin.
- Anterior ciliary vessel enters 3–4 mm from the limbus (Baidya fitz 179 Q26). Aravind fitz 305 Q19 says 2–4 mm. The
  card already uses the newer book.

## 3. Items not verified and removed

None were removed. These items were kept as close paraphrases or under the spec's exemptions; I list them for
transparency:
- **"at 10 am"** in the IOP record: the house recording example (CARD_SPEC §3–§4), not a clinical claim.
- **"Patient looks down"** before the bleb examination: a patient instruction; the department slide photo shows downgaze.
- **"opaque", "no microcysts" and "Seidel's test negative"** in the sample patient (record example and say-it):
  department slide wording for a failing bleb. Aravind's model patient has a "translucent" failed bleb (fitz 21). The
  candidate should describe what he sees.
- **"Failing bleb (salvageable)"**: a label inferred from Aravind fitz 310 Q47–Q48 (apply digital pressure before the
  bleb fails).
- **"aqueous leaking through the dye = positive"**: a paraphrase of "Seidel test for aqueous leakage" (Baidya fitz 406)
  and of fluorescein with cobalt blue light (Namrata fitz 191). No book describes the positive sign in more detail.
- **"blebitis"**: the term is in Aravind fitz 299 (4.17 Q30).

## 4. Confirm with your seniors

1. **Antimetabolite strength and time.** The card now says 5-FU 50 mg/ml for 5 minutes and MMC 0.2–0.5 mg/ml for 1–5
   minutes. Your books also print other values: Aravind's trabeculectomy FAQ gives 5-FU at 30 mg/ml, and its
   neovascular and pigmentary glaucoma sections give MMC 0.2–0.4 mg/ml for 2 minutes. Ask what strength and exposure
   time the department uses, and quote that number.
2. **MMC or 5-FU as the routine agent.** The card follows Baidya, which uses MMC in almost all cases. Aravind uses
   intraoperative 5-FU in most primary trabeculectomies and keeps MMC for high-risk eyes. Ask which is the department's
   routine.
3. **"Failing" versus "failed" bleb.** None of the books defines where one ends and the other begins. The card treats a
   failing bleb as one showing signs of failure with rising IOP, still treatable by digital pressure or suture lysis. A
   failed bleb no longer filters (types 3 and 4). The choice changes your diagnosis line, so check how the department
   uses the two words.
4. **Bleb needling.** Needling revision of a failing or encapsulated bleb (with 5-FU or MMC) is not in your three
   practical books, so the card leaves it out. Examiners often ask about it, so ask whether they expect it and how the
   department does it.
