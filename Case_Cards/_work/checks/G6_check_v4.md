### The examiner's five points
1. **"PXF mostly bilateral" — removed (newer-book rule).** Aravind 4.11 Q7 is the only source. Namrata Table 2 (fitz 224)
   gives PXG as "usually unilateral" (and the text says unilateral cupping and "usually unilateral" gonioscopy);
   Baidya (fitz 181) says nothing on laterality of the syndrome. Both occurrences cut (history row, "Who gets it"). The row now
   says only what Namrata supports: PXG usually unilateral, POAG bilateral, so one-eyed glaucoma favours PXG. The example left
   eye "pseudoexfoliation syndrome" is an invented illustration, not a claim, and stays.
2. **Prognosis "Good" — rewritten to what the books state.** No book gives a prognosis list. The line now says so and quotes
   Baidya Q9 (PXF without glaucoma: annual follow-up) and Aravind Q23 iv (trabeculoplasty has a higher success rate in PXG
   than in POAG). "Poor" is Namrata fitz 221 (faster progression, poor response to medical therapy) and Namrata fitz 223 / Baidya
   fitz 182 Q5 (weak zonules, surgical complications). The old "glaucoma found early and treated aggressively" is gone: Aravind
   Q23 says "treat aggressively", but no book calls that a good prognostic factor.
3. **RAPD answer — closing link removed, wording set to the newer book.** Baidya fitz 168: "can be seen in advanced cases" (Namrata
   fitz 191 says "indicates"; the newer book wins). "The test needs a normal fellow eye" is Aravind fitz 384 (6.6 Q26). "PXG is
   usually unilateral, so it is worth looking for" was the writer's inference; removed.
4. **"In an operated eye I check the IOL position" — removed.** No book gives it; late IOL dislocation from loss of zonular
   support in PXF (Namrata fitz 362; Namrata fitz 223 Q2) stays, and the answer is complete without the instruction.
5. **Laser trabeculoplasty in inflammatory glaucoma — card follows Baidya and CONSISTENCY 14.** I rendered Baidya fitz 456 (printed
   p.442): the "Contraindications" column is uveitic glaucoma, ICE syndrome, NV glaucoma, synechial angle closure, angle
   recession; exfoliation syndrome is an indication. The card says "Not in uveitic glaucoma" in the SLT row and gives that list
   in the viva. Aravind 4.17 Q15 (contraindication) and Q21 (indication) contradict each other on "inflammatory glaucoma"; the card
   uses neither. Nothing on the card offers trabeculoplasty for uveitic glaucoma.

**CONSISTENCY 28 (pigment dispersion 20–30 years): applied and verified.** Comparison table and viva both read "20–30 years"
(Namrata fitz 192; Aravind fitz 255 "3rd decade"). Namrata Table 3's 30–50 years is not on the card.
Other items applied and checked: 1–2 (gonioscopy cross, deepest structure SS only; caption names the conclusion), 11 (CCT 520 µm,

## 5. Confirm with your seniors
1. **Laterality.** Namrata says PXG is usually unilateral; Aravind says the syndrome is mostly bilateral and "PXF glaucoma tends
   to be bilateral". The card follows Namrata and says nothing about the syndrome itself. Ask what your teachers expect.
2. **How long laser trabeculoplasty lasts in PXG.** Aravind says higher success but shorter duration; Baidya says best results
   in exfoliation and 3–5 years of control in general. The card gives both.
3. **Angle pigment pattern.** The card follows Baidya and Namrata (blotchy in PXF, uniform dark band in pigment dispersion);
   Aravind 4.11 Q15 prints the reverse.
4. **Where the transillumination defects lie.** The card says the pupillary margin (Namrata; Aravind 4.10); Aravind 4.11 Q10 says
   mid-peripheral.
5. **Gonioscopy cross.** Your department writes a grade in each quadrant; the card writes the deepest structure (SS). Confirm
   what the grade stands for (card G9).
