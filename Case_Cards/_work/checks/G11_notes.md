## 1. Buphthalmos (primary congenital glaucoma)
- Definition: "Buphthalmos is a term that is applied to congenital glaucomas with enlargement of globe that appear during the first 3 years of life" — NAMRATA fitz 206
- "Buphthalmos" = Greek "ox-eyed"; visibly enlarged eyeballs, mostly bilateral, within the first 3 years, with raised IOP; mainly a sign of primary congenital glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 185 (Q1)
- Appears in the first three years of life; earlier onset → more severe glaucomatous changes — BAIDYA fitz 183
- Males 65% — BAIDYA fitz 183, 185 (Q2)
- Bilateral 65–80% — BAIDYA fitz 184, 185; NAMRATA fitz 206
- Prevalence 1 in 10,000 live births in the West, 1 in 3,300 in India — BAIDYA fitz 185 (Q2) (Namrata: 1:10,000–1:20,000 West; 1:3300 India — NAMRATA fitz 206)
- 90% sporadic; autosomal recessive CYP1B1 in the rest (10%); siblings must be examined — BAIDYA fitz 184, 185 (Q2)
- FOXC1 also associated *(extra)* — BAIDYA fitz 185 (Q2)
- Chance of a second affected child about 3%; up to 25% if two children have the disease — NAMRATA fitz 206
- Classic triad: watering (epiphora), photophobia, blepharospasm — BAIDYA fitz 183; NAMRATA fitz 206 (most common presentation)
- Referred from the paediatrician for corneal cloudiness; parents notice large eyes, hazy white cornea — NAMRATA fitz 206; BAIDYA fitz 184
- Birth history: forceps delivery → hazy cornea with vertical tears in Descemet's membrane — BAIDYA fitz 184
- Photophobia easily seen when the child is examined with a torch — BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 206
- Examination under general anaesthesia for detailed evaluation — BAIDYA fitz 184
- Cycloplegic refraction; enlarged eyes show a myopic shift — BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 206
- Eyeball enlarged: immature, growing collagen of cornea and sclera stretches with raised IOP — NAMRATA fitz 206
- Conjunctiva normal; bluish sclera (stretching) — BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 206
- Horizontal corneal diameter > 12 mm (high index of suspicion) — BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 206, 207 (Q1)
- Normal infant horizontal corneal diameter 10–10.5 mm, increases by 0.5–1.0 mm in the first year — NAMRATA fitz 207 (Q1)
- Corneal oedema/haze; Haab's striae = horizontal breaks in Descemet's membrane; oedema resolves with treatment but Haab's striae persist for life — BAIDYA fitz 184
- Severe cases: acute hydrops — NAMRATA fitz 206
- Anterior chamber deep; iris normal or loss of crypts (stromal hypoplasia); lens normal — BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 206
- Fundus: cupping > 0.3 — BAIDYA fitz 184; asymmetric cupping or CDR > 0.3 — NAMRATA fitz 206
- IOP by hand-held Perkins tonometer or Tonopen; > 14 mm Hg, or > 5 mm Hg difference between eyes, suggests congenital glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 207
- Normal infant IOP 11–14 mm Hg (Tonopen or hand-held Goldmann) — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Perkins = portable hand-held applanation tonometer, usable in anaesthetised patients — BAIDYA fitz 753
- General anaesthetic agents in general reduce IOP; trichloroethylene, ketamine and succinylcholine (suxamethonium) increase it — ARAVIND fitz 206 (4.1 Q17)
- Direct gonioscopy is done supine, for example in the operating room for infants under anaesthesia — ARAVIND fitz 212; infant Koeppe goniolens — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Gonioscopy: smooth, homogeneous, amorphous compacted trabecular meshwork; high anterior insertion of iris root; vascular loops from the major arterial circle (Loch Ness monster phenomenon); fine fluffy tissue on peripheral iris (Lister's morning mist) — BAIDYA fitz 185
- Peripheral iris stroma hypoplastic, unpigmented, scalloped; Barkan's membrane (incomplete resorption of mesodermal tissue; existence controversial; basis of goniotomy) — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Provisional diagnosis wording: "A () years old male/female suffering from primary congenital glaucoma" — BAIDYA fitz 185
- Pathogenesis: isolated trabeculodysgenesis — absent angle recess, flat (more common) or concave iris insertion into the thick trabeculum → impaired outflow → raised IOP enlarges axial length and corneal diameter because the infant eye is soft and elastic — BAIDYA fitz 185 (Q3)
- Classification: newborn (true congenital) — IOP rise in intrauterine life 25%; infantile — before 3 years 65%; juvenile — after 3 years to before adulthood 10% — BAIDYA fitz 185 (Q4)
- Primary (congenital, infantile, juvenile) versus secondary (systemic: chromosomal, metabolic — Lowe, Zellweger; phakomatoses — Sturge–Weber; ocular developmental — anterior segment dysgenesis, aniridia, congenital ectropion uveae, nanophthalmos; ocular disease — retinoblastoma, ROP, PHPV, trauma, uveitis) — NAMRATA fitz 208 (Q5)
- Hoskins classification: A isolated trabeculodysgenesis (flat iris insertion — anterior, posterior or mixed; concave/wrap-around; unclassified); B iridodysgenesis (aniridia, iris hypoplasia, congenital iris ectropion); C iridocorneal dysgenesis (peripheral — Axenfeld–Rieger anomaly; mid-peripheral — Rieger's anomaly; central — Peters anomaly, sclerocornea, CHED, PPMD; size — microcornea or megalocornea) — BAIDYA fitz 185–186 (Q5)
- Hoskins useful for planning treatment and prognosis — NAMRATA fitz 209 (Q7)
- Differential of corneal oedema/clouding: CHED, mucopolysaccharidoses, sclerocornea, forceps injury — BAIDYA fitz 186 (Q6); NAMRATA fitz 207 (Q2)
- CHED: cornea 2–3 times thicker, no enlargement, symmetric clouding, no Descemet breaks — NAMRATA fitz 207; CHED has no photophobia, white eye, normal-sized cornea, thick cornea — ARAVIND fitz 148 (Q7)
- Mucopolysaccharidoses: no IOP rise, no corneal enlargement — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Differential of enlarged eye: axial myopia, connective-tissue disorders (homocystinuria, Marfan, Weill–Marchesani, Ehlers–Danlos), Proteus syndrome, keratoglobus, megalocornea — BAIDYA fitz 186 (Q7)
- Epiphora/red eye: nasolacrimal duct obstruction, ophthalmia neonatorum, corneal abrasion; photophobia: conjunctivitis, uveitis; corneal enlargement: axial myopia, megalocornea (X-linked or sporadic) — NAMRATA fitz 207–208 (Q2)
- Megalocornea: non-progressive symmetric enlargement of the cornea > 12 mm without significant change in corneal thickness or contour — NAMRATA fitz 160–161
- Congenital glaucoma: raised IOP, cloudy cornea, disc changes, generalised enlargement of the eye — NAMRATA fitz 161; raised IOP, larger cornea, Haab's striae, buphthalmos (all absent in CHED) — NAMRATA fitz 172
- Birth trauma vs congenital glaucoma table (corneal diameter normal vs large; IOP normal vs high; photophobia no vs yes; oedema immediate vs weeks–months; clears spontaneously vs after IOP lowered; Descemet tears vertical/oblique vs horizontal or concentric to limbus; left eye more vs equal; soft-tissue injury may be present vs absent) — NAMRATA fitz 208 (Table 1, Q3)
- Disc in children: small physiological cup; cupping from loss of neural tissue at vertical poles and enlargement of the scleral canal; faster than in adults and reversible — BAIDYA fitz 186 (Q8)
- Cupping reversible if pressure lowered early; incomplete connective tissue of lamina cribrosa allows posterior movement with elastic return — NAMRATA fitz 208 (Q4)
- Treatment mainly surgical — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Medical: prostaglandin analogue, beta-blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors; avoid alpha agonists (apraclonidine, brimonidine) under 2 years — respiratory depression, coma, hypotension, bradycardia — BAIDYA fitz 186 (Q9)
- Goniotomy: needs a clear cornea; direct goniolens; incision in the anterior trabecular meshwork just behind Schwalbe's line so aqueous flows directly into Schlemm's canal; nasal then temporal, usually same sitting; Swan knife; about 120°; complications hyphaema, iridodialysis, AC shallowing — BAIDYA fitz 186 (Q9); NAMRATA fitz 207
- Trabeculotomy: ab externo; when cornea hazy or by surgeon preference; Schlemm's canal found where blue cornea meets white sclera, deroofed until aqueous leaks; probes passed into the canal and rotated into the AC; repeated on the other side — BAIDYA fitz 186 (Q9); procedure of choice when cornea hazy — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Trabeculectomy: if the above fail; with or without MMC or combined with trabeculotomy — BAIDYA fitz 187; filtration surgery usually combined with trabeculotomy (trab + trab) — NAMRATA fitz 207; Q6 options: goniotomy (clear cornea), trabeculotomy or trabeculotomy + trabeculectomy, valve implant — NAMRATA fitz 208
- GDD: Molteno, Ahmed, Baerveldt in neonates when commoner surgeries failed; tube usually supero-temporal then infero-nasal; complications cornea–tube touch, implant migration, cataract — BAIDYA fitz 187
- Cyclodestruction (endolaser or transscleral diode cyclophotocoagulation) for refractory cases; complications hypotony, retinal detachment — BAIDYA fitz 187; cyclocryotherapy or laser CPC — NAMRATA fitz 207
- Penetrating keratoplasty for permanent corneal scarring after IOP normalised; DSAEK recently popular *(extra)* — NAMRATA fitz 207
- UBM: abnormal iris insertion, cornea, lens, membranes over TM; PI patency and trabeculectomy function — BAIDYA fitz 187 (Q10)
- Issues in management: aetiology and inheritance, systemic problems, choice of surgery, associated ocular problems (refractive error, corneal opacity, cataract, squint, amblyopia), counselling parents — NAMRATA fitz 208 (Q6)
- Prognosis lines built from: earlier onset more severe (BAIDYA fitz 183); reversible cupping if IOP lowered early (NAMRATA fitz 208; BAIDYA fitz 186); Haab's striae and corneal scars persist (BAIDYA fitz 184; NAMRATA fitz 207); amblyopia and refractive error (NAMRATA fitz 208). No book gives a formal prognosis list.

## 2. Sturge–Weber syndrome
- Definition: "a rare congenital neuro-oculocutaneous disorder present at birth", also called encephalotrigeminal angiomatosis — NAMRATA fitz 203
- Classic triad: port-wine facial telangiectasia (naevus flammeus) in the trigeminal distribution respecting the vertical midline, ipsilateral glaucoma, intracranial angiomata — NAMRATA fitz 203
- Glaucoma in about half (30–70%) when the port-wine stain involves the ophthalmic and maxillary divisions — NAMRATA fitz 203
- Tri-symptomatic (skin, eye, CNS), bisymptomatic, monosymptomatic — NAMRATA fitz 203
- Not inherited; no gender predilection — BAIDYA fitz 187
- Glaucoma at birth 60%, later in life 40% — BAIDYA fitz 187; infants ~60% angle anomaly, late onset ~40% raised episcleral venous pressure — NAMRATA fitz 205 (Q1)
- Glaucoma usually open-angle; pain from angle closure (rarer) — BAIDYA fitz 187
- Seizures in 75% within the first year; headache, mental retardation, developmental delay in 50% — BAIDYA fitz 187
- Port-wine stain: well-delineated red macule, V1 and V2 distribution; thicker and darker with age, nodules; does not usually cross the midline; may be bilateral rarely — BAIDYA fitz 188; NAMRATA fitz 205 (Q3)
- Hemi-hypertrophy, hemiparesis on the side opposite the stain — BAIDYA fitz 188
- Upper lid more often affected than lower; mechanical ptosis — BAIDYA fitz 188; NAMRATA fitz 205
- Glaucoma incidence higher when the stain involves the eyelid — NAMRATA fitz 205 (Q1)
- V1 + V2 highest risk of glaucoma; V2 alone lowest — NAMRATA fitz 205 (Q3)
- Bilateral port-wine stain → higher likelihood of SWS — NAMRATA fitz 205 (Q3)
- Dense episcleral vascular plexus, ampulliform dilatation of conjunctival vessels — NAMRATA fitz 204; episcleral vessel dilatation, limbal vascular malformation — BAIDYA fitz 188
- Cornea oedema with high IOP; buphthalmos if congenital — BAIDYA fitz 188; NAMRATA fitz 204
- Heterochromic iris; cataract or subluxated lens — BAIDYA fitz 188
- Fundus: diffuse choroidal haemangioma with "tomato-ketchup" fundus; serous RD, CME — BAIDYA fitz 188; indirect ophthalmoscopy for choroidal haemangioma, retinal oedema, RD — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Unilateral cases: IOP > 21 mm Hg in the ipsilateral eye in about 50% — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Gonioscopy: like PCG (anterior iris insertion, CBB attached to TM) or blood in the angle — BAIDYA fitz 188; infants: developmental anomaly or neovascularisation; adults usually normal — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Diagnosis wording "A () years old male/female of Sturge–Weber's syndrome with associated UL/BL glaucoma" — BAIDYA fitz 189
- Mechanisms: angle malformation (60%); raised episcleral venous pressure (blood in Schlemm's canal); hypersecretion by ciliary body or choroidal haemangioma — BAIDYA fitz 189 (Q1)
- Skull X-ray: tramline gyriform calcification (parietal, occipital); CT; MRI pial angiomatous malformation — BAIDYA fitz 189 (Q2); "double densities or railroad tracks" — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Visual fields (SAP); OCT/GDx/HRT if borderline IOP — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Medical: beta-blockers, alpha agonists, CAIs; avoid prostaglandin analogues (EVP already high; anterior uveal effusion) — BAIDYA fitz 189 (Q3); NAMRATA fitz 204
- Late-onset glaucoma may be controlled with drugs; infantile form usually needs surgery — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Goniotomy first choice (< 2 years — Baidya); no intraoperative choroidal effusion risk — BAIDYA fitz 189; NAMRATA fitz 204
- Trabeculectomy: risk of intraoperative choroidal effusion, expulsive haemorrhage, bleb failure → combined trabeculotomy–trabeculectomy preferred — BAIDYA fitz 189 (book prints "combined trabeculectomy—trabeculectomy"; Namrata fitz 204 and its references 2–3 confirm trabeculotomy–trabeculectomy); one or more sclerotomies before trabeculectomy — NAMRATA fitz 204
- Ahmed valve better than Molteno in long-term control — BAIDYA fitz 189
- Cyclophotocoagulation for refractory cases or poor visual potential; hypotony, phthisis — BAIDYA fitz 189; NAMRATA fitz 204
- Port-wine stain: pulsed dye laser early (skin thickens later); multiple sittings 2–4 weeks apart; selective photothermolysis — BAIDYA fitz 189–190 (Q4); 100% clearance not possible — NAMRATA fitz 205
- Visual outcome variable; long-term follow-up; loss from glaucoma and its surgery, choroidal haemangioma, occipital leptomeningeal angioma — BAIDYA fitz 190 (Q5)
- Congenital glaucoma vs SWS table: bilateral vs usually unilateral; no stain vs ipsilateral stain; systemic involvement rare vs neurological common — NAMRATA fitz 205 (Table 1)

## 3. Lens-induced glaucomas
- Definition: "a group of secondary glaucoma that share the lens as a common pathogenic cause" — ARAVIND fitz 271 (4.14 Q1)
- Classification: open-angle — phacolytic, lens particle, phacoanaphylaxis; closed-angle — phacomorphic, ectopia lentis — ARAVIND fitz 271 (Q2, image checked)
- Phacolytic: elderly, long poor vision, sudden rapid pain and redness; hyperaemia; microcystic corneal oedema; deep AC, prominent cells and flare, no KPs, white flocculent material ± pseudohypopyon; open angle; mature, hypermature/morgagnian cataract with wrinkled anterior capsule; IOP > 35 mm Hg — ARAVIND fitz 271 (Q3–4)
- Phacolytic mechanism: high-molecular-weight lens protein leaks through microscopic capsular openings → inflammatory reaction, engulfed by large phagocytes → swept into trabecular spaces → block outflow (open-angle) — ARAVIND fitz 271 (Q5, image)
- Phacolytic treatment: lower IOP (beta-blockers, apraclonidine, CAI, osmotic agents), then cataract extraction (ultimate treatment) — ARAVIND fitz 272 (Q6)
- Lens particle: after cataract surgery (retained cortex/epinucleus) or Nd:YAG capsulotomy; microcystic oedema, free cortical material in AC, dense flare and cells, posterior and peripheral anterior synechiae, raised IOP; aqueous suppressants, topical steroids, cycloplegics; removal of lens debris — ARAVIND fitz 272 (Q7–9)
- Phacoanaphylactic: sensitised to own lens protein after surgery or penetrating trauma → granulomatous inflammation; criteria: polymorphonuclear leucocytes in aqueous or vitreous; circulating lens protein insufficient to explain glaucoma; moderate AC reaction, KPs on endothelium and anterior lens surface, low-grade vitritis, synechiae, residual lens material — ARAVIND fitz 272 (Q10–12)
- Phacomorphic: "secondary angle closure glaucoma due to lens intumescence" — ARAVIND fitz 272 (Q13)
- Phacomorphic vs PACG: rapid lens swelling vs normal lens growth; senile or traumatic cataract vs hypermetropic eye; asymmetric central shallowing vs both eyes shallow; unilateral mature intumescent cataract vs normal lens — ARAVIND fitz 273 (Q15, image)
- Phacomorphic pathogenesis: intumescent lens forces the iris root against the cornea → angle closure — ARAVIND fitz 273 (Q16)
- Phacomorphic treatment: definitive surgery after IOP control; beta-blockers, alpha agonist, topical or systemic CAI; topical steroids and cycloplegics; YAG PI; lens extraction — ARAVIND fitz 273 (Q17)
- Ectopia lentis: displacement of lens from its normal position — ARAVIND fitz 273 (Q18)
- Ectopia lentis → anterior displacement → pupillary block (alone or with vitreous) → iris bombé → shallow AC → secondary angle closure — ARAVIND fitz 273 (Q19, image)
- Causes: familial, ectopia lentis et pupillae, aniridia; Marfan, homocystinuria, Weill–Marchesani, hyperlysinaemia, sulphite oxidase deficiency, Stickler, Ehlers–Danlos — ARAVIND fitz 273–274 (Q20)
- Treatment: laser iridectomy (treatment of choice — pupillary block); cycloplegics and mydriatics flatten the lens and pull it back; miotics worsen block — avoid; lens removal only if in AC or lens-induced uveitis; pars plana lensectomy if displaced posteriorly — ARAVIND fitz 274 (Q21–23)
- Inverse glaucoma: in spherophakia miotics slacken zonules → lens moves forward → pupillary block worsens — ARAVIND fitz 233 (4.6 Q22)
- Lens-induced angle closure (swollen or subluxated lens) in the differential of acute PACG — BAIDYA fitz 165 (Q1a); phacolytic glaucoma masquerades as acute angle closure — ARAVIND fitz 233 (Q20 B vi)
- Lens particle and phacolytic glaucoma to be differentiated from angle recession — NAMRATA fitz 216

## 4. Uveitic glaucoma
- No formal definition in the three books. Card wording ("a secondary glaucoma in which intraocular inflammation raises the IOP, by open-angle or closed-angle mechanisms") paraphrases the headings of ARAVIND fitz 265 (4.12 Q1). Flag for the fact-check.
- Open-angle mechanisms: trabeculitis (clogging by inflammatory cells), increased aqueous viscosity (fibrinous exudate), raised EVP (perivascular cuffing), increased aqueous secretion (prostaglandins), steroid-induced — ARAVIND fitz 265 (Q1 I)
- Closed-angle: pupillary block (posterior synechiae; PAS; angle neovascularisation); non-pupillary block — ciliary body oedema rotates it forward, zonules relax, lens moves forward, angle closes — ARAVIND fitz 265 (Q1 II)
- High IOP in uveitis: trabeculitis, NVG, debris, Posner–Schlossman, TM sclerosis, PAS, iris bombé, steroid — ARAVIND fitz 173 (3.x Q39)
- Signs: KPs, flare and cells, posterior synechiae, complicated cataract, band keratopathy (chronic), acute attack from pupillary block — ARAVIND fitz 249 (4.9 Q18 ii)
- Posner–Schlossman (glaucomatocyclitic crisis): unilateral, recurrent, mild discomfort or blur, raised IOP with open angles, mild AC reaction or fine white KPs, crises hours to weeks, normal IOP and no uveitis between attacks, normal fields and discs; anti-glaucoma drugs and cycloplegics; CMV DNA found by PCR in aqueous — BAIDYA fitz 381 (Q23)
- PSS: recurrent mild anterior uveitis with marked IOP rise; GI disorders (peptic ulcer); white eye, minimal congestion; open angle, no synechiae; apraclonidine especially useful — ARAVIND fitz 265–266 (Q3); quiet eye, mimics angle closure, atropine ointment — ARAVIND fitz 173 (Q41)
- Fuchs: chronic non-granulomatous unilateral heterochromic iridocyclitis — BAIDYA fitz 397 (Q91); 15% bilateral, 3rd–4th decade, cataract 80%, glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 397–398 (Q92–93); fine angle neovascularisation, hyphaema — BAIDYA fitz 398
- Fuchs triad heterochromia, cataract, KPs — ARAVIND fitz 178; white quiet eye, stellate KPs, filiform angle vessels, NVI/NVG rare, spontaneous hyphaema, OAG from trabecular sclerosis — ARAVIND fitz 249–250 (Q18 iii, Q19)
- Management — inflammation: topical prednisolone acetate 1.0% or dexamethasone 0.1%, titrated; periocular or systemic steroid if insufficient; NSAIDs; immunosuppressives (methotrexate, azathioprine, chlorambucil) in severe cases; cycloplegics atropine 1%, homatropine 1–5%, cyclopentolate 0.5–1% — ARAVIND fitz 266 (Q4 I)
- Glaucoma: beta-blocker, alpha-2 agonist or CAI first line; oral CAI added; hyperosmotic short-term; intracameral tPA 6.25–12.5 µg for fibrinous uveitis with impending pupillary block *(extra)*; miotics and prostaglandins contraindicated in the inflamed eye — ARAVIND fitz 266 (Q4 II)
- Surgery: avoid in active inflammation; laser iridotomy safer than incisional iridectomy but often fails (inflammation, fibrin, neovascularisation); filtering surgery with heavy steroid cover, subconjunctival 5-FU improves success; laser trabeculoplasty not effective and generally contraindicated; surgical iridectomy during cataract surgery; transscleral Nd:YAG CPC in eyes with limited potential; Ahmed valve when inflammation controlled with steroid cover — ARAVIND fitz 267
- Laser trabeculoplasty contraindicated in uveitic glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 180 (Q35)
- Uveitic glaucoma in differential of acute PACG — BAIDYA fitz 165 (Q1f)

## 5. Steroid-induced glaucoma
- Definition: "a form of open angle glaucoma occurring as an adverse effect [of] exogenous corticosteroid therapy or excess endogenous production of glucocorticoids" — NAMRATA fitz 219 (Q1)
- After 4–6 weeks of topical steroid, about 5% rise > 16 mm Hg and 30% rise 6–15 mm Hg — NAMRATA fitz 218
- 25% of the general population show raised IOP after 4 weeks of QID topical steroid; 5% "super responders" rise > 10–15 mm Hg within 2 weeks; no racial or sexual predilection — ARAVIND fitz 268 (Q1)
- Becker (QID, 6 weeks, final IOP): low < 20, intermediate 20–30, high > 31 mm Hg; Armaly (TDS, 4 weeks, IOP change): low < 6, intermediate 6–15, high > 15 mm Hg — NAMRATA fitz 220 (Table 2, image checked)
- Risk factors: POAG, family history of POAG, children under 10, high myopia, diabetes, connective tissue disorders (rheumatoid arthritis) — NAMRATA fitz 218
- Others: VKC, uveitis, chronic blepharitis; SLE; intravitreal triamcinolone; sub-Tenon depot; post refractive surgery; asthmatics on inhaled steroids — ARAVIND fitz 268 (Q4)
- Endogenous: adrenal hyperplasia or adenoma — ARAVIND fitz 269 (Q5)
- Time to IOP rise: topical potent QID 2–6 weeks; IVTA 4 mg 4–8 weeks; posterior sub-Tenon triamcinolone 40 mg 5–9 weeks; inhaled 3 months; dermatological betamethasone 0.1% 3 months; pulse 140 mg 4-weekly 6 months; oral hydrocortisone 25 mg/day 1 year, prednisolone 50 mg/day 2–15 months — NAMRATA fitz 219 (Table 1)
- Presentation variable; pain and gradual loss or asymptomatic — NAMRATA fitz 218
- Signs: lid skin atrophy or ptosis; increased corneal thickness or ulcers; mydriasis; PSC cataract; open angles on 4-mirror gonioscopy; glaucomatous cupping; IOP may be normal if steroid stopped, high with implants or current use — NAMRATA fitz 218
- Adults: resembles COAG with open normal angle, no symptoms; may be acute (rise within hours); may mimic low-tension glaucoma — ARAVIND fitz 269 (Q6)
- Children: lower incidence; infants develop a condition resembling congenital glaucoma (nasal/inhaled steroids, drops after squint surgery) — ARAVIND fitz 269 (Q7)
- Diagnosis needs suspicion; ask specifically about drops, ointments, pills — ARAVIND fitz 269 (Q8)
- Mechanism: GAG accumulation (lysosomal stabilisation); extracellular matrix change; more collagen, elastin, laminin, fibronectin; less TM phagocytosis; less proteinases (stromelysin, MMPs); less outflow prostaglandins (PGF2α) — NAMRATA fitz 219–220 (Q3); hydrated GAG "biologic oedema" — ARAVIND fitz 268 (Q3)
- Myocilin (TIGR) gene most studied *(extra)* — NAMRATA fitz 220 (Q4)
- Mean IOP rise: dexamethasone 0.1% 22; prednisolone 1.0% 10; dexamethasone 0.005% 8; fluorometholone 0.1% 6; hydrocortisone 0.5% 3; tetrahydrotriamcinolone 0.25% 2 mm Hg — NAMRATA fitz 220 (Table 3)
- Pressure effect proportional to anti-inflammatory potency; NSAIDs do not raise IOP — ARAVIND fitz 270 (Q12)
- Periocular depot most dangerous; systemic least likely — ARAVIND fitz 270 (Q11)
- Management: stop the steroid → IOP normal in weeks to months; anti-glaucoma drugs; IVTA → drugs until crystals resolve (6 months); intractable after depot → PPV removal + trabeculectomy; lower-potency substitutes (prednisolone/dexamethasone phosphate, rimexolone, loteprednol, fluorometholone); NSAIDs (diclofenac, nepafenac, ketorolac); steroid sparing (tacrolimus, cyclosporine in VKC; methotrexate); ALT or SLT if drugs fail or are not tolerated; trabeculectomy ± antimetabolite — NAMRATA fitz 219
- Excision of depot steroid; filtering surgery after progression confirmed — ARAVIND fitz 270 (Q13)
- Prevention: IOP every 2 weeks on steroids; avoid topical steroid where possible; low-potency — NAMRATA fitz 219; baseline IOP before therapy — ARAVIND fitz 269–270 (Q11)
- Differential: POAG (bilateral, high IOP); NTG (bilateral, thin cornea, normal IOP); glaucomatocyclitic crisis (unilateral, ciliary congestion, KPs, AC reaction); uveitic; juvenile OAG — NAMRATA fitz 218
- Laser trabeculoplasty indicated in steroid-induced glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 180 (Q35)

## 6. Pigmentary glaucoma
- No formal definition in the three books. Card wording builds on the pathomechanism row of NAMRATA fitz 225 (posterior bowing of iris, rubbing of posterior iris on zonules, release of pigment, trabecular block) and ARAVIND fitz 256 (Q7–8). Flag for the fact-check.
- Profile: young (3rd decade), myopic, male, white — ARAVIND fitz 255 (Q1); younger age 20–30 years — NAMRATA fitz 192; usually bilateral — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Chromosome 7q *(extra)* — ARAVIND fitz 255 (Q2)
- Cornea: Krukenberg spindle; endothelial pleomorphism and polymegathism — ARAVIND fitz 255
- Iris: mid-peripheral transillumination defects (PXF: pupillary margin); pigment granules; heterochromia; anisocoria; concave iris — ARAVIND fitz 255 (Q3)
- Deep AC; posterior bowing (reverse pupillary block) — ARAVIND fitz 255, 257; NAMRATA fitz 225
- Lens: pigment on zonules and posterior lens surface (Zentmayer ring / Scheie line) — ARAVIND fitz 255; Zentmayer's line — NAMRATA fitz 225
- Gonioscopy: wide open; dense homogeneous dark brown pigment band all round the TM; pigmented Schwalbe's line; Sampaolesi's line may be seen — ARAVIND fitz 255 (Q4); prominent uniform dark band — NAMRATA fitz 223; wide open angles with dark broad pigmentation — NAMRATA fitz 192; BAIDYA fitz 169 (Q1b)
- Sampaolesi's line not exclusive to PXF; seen in pigment dispersion and chronic inflammation — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q6)
- Fundus: retinal detachment, lattice — ARAVIND fitz 256 (Q6)
- Mechanism: Campbell's mechanical theory — zonules rub mid-peripheral iris because of iris concavity → pigment release; pigment release + reduced outflow facility — ARAVIND fitz 256 (Q7–8)

## 7. Angle-recession glaucoma (covered — Namrata full short case; Aravind, Baidya lists)
- Definition: "a secondary open angle glaucoma that is associated with blunt trauma to eye. Angle recession is a tear between the longitudinal and circular muscles of the ciliary body. It is a gonioscopy diagnosis." — NAMRATA fitz 215
- Hyphaema strongly associated (60–90%) — NAMRATA fitz 215; recession in 85% (71–100%) of traumatic hyphaema — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q2)
- Recession in 20–94% of eyes after blunt trauma; masked by hyphaema (shearing of anterior ciliary arteries) — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q1)
- Glaucoma in 5.5% (7–10%) of recessed eyes; risk higher if > 180°; two peaks — < 1 year and ≥ 10 years — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q7)
- Mechanism of glaucoma: trabecular damage, not the recession itself — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q4)
- Mechanism of recession: AP compression, equatorial expansion, limbal stretch, posterior displacement of lens–iris diaphragm — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q3)
- Symptoms: deep pain, redness, gradual loss; onset immediately or months to years later; may be asymptomatic; trauma often forgotten — NAMRATA fitz 215
- Signs: scars, enophthalmos, orbital fracture; lid scar; corneal blood staining or pigment (early); deep irregular AC; hyphaema (early); iridodialysis, cyclodialysis, sphincter tears, iridoschisis; corectopia, traumatic mydriasis; IOP raised (or low early — ciliary shutdown); subluxated lens, cataract, Vossius ring; vitreous haemorrhage; retinal dialysis, RD; glaucomatous disc — NAMRATA fitz 215–216
- Phacodonesis, iridodonesis, traumatic cataract, retinal tear; usually unilateral — NAMRATA fitz 192
- Gonioscopy deferred 4–6 weeks after acute injury; asymmetry vs fellow eye or other quadrants; simultaneous bilateral Koeppe gonioscopy most useful; widened ciliary body band (retro-displaced iris root) most important; prominent scleral spur; irregular darker pigmentation; PAS — NAMRATA fitz 216
- Differential of unilateral glaucoma: PXF, NVG, lens particle and phacolytic — NAMRATA fitz 216
- Medical: aqueous suppressants — timolol 0.5% BD and/or apraclonidine or brimonidine 0.2%; atropine and steroids in acute cases — NAMRATA fitz 216
- Miotics ineffective (trabecular scarring); prostaglandin analogues drug of choice — ARAVIND fitz 243 (Q39)
- Laser trabeculoplasty ineffective in most (distorted angle, TM scarring); Nd:YAG trabeculopuncture effective in some — NAMRATA fitz 216–217; angle recession is a contraindication to laser trabeculoplasty — BAIDYA fitz 180 (Q35)
- Trabeculectomy with antimetabolites effective, success lower than POAG; drainage devices limited benefit — NAMRATA fitz 217
- Recession vs cyclodialysis: in recession the longitudinal muscle stays attached; in cyclodialysis the whole ciliary body detaches — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q8)
- Seven rings of trauma: sphincter tear, iridodialysis, angle recession, cyclodialysis, TM tear, zonular dialysis/subluxation, retinal dialysis or tear — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q6)
- Sources of traumatic hyphaema: major arterial circle and ciliary body branches (> 90%) — NAMRATA fitz 217 (Q5) *(not used on card — space)*
