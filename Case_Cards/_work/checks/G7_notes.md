### Differentials
- Branch retinal vein occlusion:
  - collaterals cross the horizontal raphe temporal to the macula; in CVO they form at the optic nerve: ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q43 iv)
  - more retinal than iris new vessels: NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.15 question; the answer is cut off at the page end)
- Ocular ischaemic syndrome: veins dilated but not tortuous; dot and blot haemorrhages in the mid-periphery; optic disc normal; carotid imaging shows stenosis. BAIDYA fitz 281 (table).
- Diabetic retinopathy (NPDR): always bilateral; veins dilated and beaded; hard exudates always present (CRVO: absent). BAIDYA fitz 281 (table).

### Must know
- **Types (CVOS)**
  - Three types: ischaemic, non-ischaemic, indeterminate: BAIDYA fitz 281 (Q3); ARAVIND fitz 381 (Q1–2).
  - Indeterminate = intraretinal haemorrhage prevents angiographic perfusion grading: BAIDYA fitz 282 (Q5); ARAVIND fitz 381 (Q2).
  - Non-ischaemic about **75%**: BAIDYA fitz 282 (Q6). See Disagreements.
  - **34%** of non-ischaemic convert to ischaemic by **3 years**: BAIDYA fitz 282 (Q8). See Disagreements.
- **Ischaemic vs non-ischaemic table**
  - Vision: non-ischaemic moderate to severe impairment; ischaemic severe, **counting fingers or worse**. BAIDYA fitz 281 (Q4 table). See Disagreements.
  - RAPD: absent or mild vs marked, **> 0.7 log unit** on a neutral density filter. BAIDYA fitz 281; NAMRATA fitz 235; ARAVIND fitz 384 (+/– vs ++).
  - FFA capillary non-perfusion **< 10** vs **> 10** disc areas: BAIDYA fitz 282 (table and Q7, written "DD"); NAMRATA fitz 235, 238 ("disc areas", the CVOS definition); ARAVIND fitz 385 (Q34, "DD"). See Disagreements.
  - ERG, non-ischaemic: normal or mildly depressed, BAIDYA fitz 282.
  - ERG, ischaemic:
    - b-to-a ratio **< 1**: BAIDYA fitz 286; NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.13, normal 2:1)
    - b-wave **< 60%** of normal: NAMRATA fitz 235, 238; ARAVIND fitz 386 (Q35 ii)
- **Central, hemi-central or branch**
  - Occlusion may involve the central, hemicentral or branch retinal vein: NAMRATA fitz 234.
  - Grid laser gives no visual benefit in CRVO (CVOS): NAMRATA fitz 238 (group M) and fitz 240 (Q.8); ARAVIND fitz 386–387 (Q38 i, Q42).
  - Grid helps BRVO, unlike CRVO, because CRVO leakage is diffuse, involving the foveal centre and all four parafoveal quadrants: ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q43 ii, v). The question itself presupposes that vision improves after grid in BRVO.
- **Glaucoma both ways**
  - Open-angle glaucoma is first in the list of ocular associations: BAIDYA fitz 283 (Q10). Also ARAVIND fitz 381 (Q9 ii a) and NAMRATA fitz 234.
  - Stasis: ARAVIND fitz 382 (Q13 i).
  - Lamina cribrosa bowing: BAIDYA fitz 283 (Q9 b) and fitz 287 (Q17); NAMRATA fitz 239 (Q.2).
  - CRVO causes NVG (open- and closed-angle types) and steroid-induced glaucoma: ARAVIND fitz 382 (Q14).
  - An angle-closure attack around the time of CRVO may be "both causative as well as the effect": NAMRATA fitz 236.
- **"100-day glaucoma"**
  - NVI in about 50% of eyes, usually at **2–4 months** ("100 days glaucoma"): NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.5).
  - Ischaemic: NVI **49%**, NVA 37%, NVG **29%**, NVE 9%, NVD 6% within the first **6 months**; risk greatest in the first 6 months: BAIDYA fitz 282–283 (table, Q8). The card gives NVI and NVG only.
  - **One-third** of eyes with NVI develop NVG: NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.5); ARAVIND fitz 381 (Q7, 33%).
  - Significant = NVI over **2 clock hours** or any NVA: NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.7); ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q39).
- **Investigations**
  - FFA once the haemorrhages clear, usually about **3 months** after onset: BAIDYA fitz 284 (the book misprints "onset" as "insert"); NAMRATA fitz 238; ARAVIND fitz 385 (Q32).
  - OCT: BAIDYA fitz 285; NAMRATA fitz 238.
  - Blood pressure, fasting glucose, lipid profile: NAMRATA fitz 237. Also BAIDYA fitz 284 (CBC, FBS, PPBS, HbA1c, ECG, lipids, urea, creatinine).
  - Hypercoagulable work-up if under 60: BAIDYA fitz 278 and 284 (under 60 is "more associated with hypercoagulable states"). NAMRATA fitz 237 adds bilateral occlusion, prior occlusion, prior thrombosis and family history.
- **Macular oedema**
  - Intravitreal anti-VEGF is first-line therapy: NAMRATA fitz 238.
  - Ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab: BAIDYA fitz 286 (Q14a); ARAVIND fitz 386 (Q37 iii).
  - Intravitreal triamcinolone and the dexamethasone implant (Ozurdex): BAIDYA fitz 286; NAMRATA fitz 238.
  - Cataract and raised IOP: NAMRATA fitz 238 (Ozurdex); BAIDYA fitz 278 (triamcinolone 4 mg, in the conclusion of a BRVO trial); ARAVIND fitz 382 (Q14 ii, steroid-induced glaucoma).
- **New vessels**
  - Prompt PRP for NVI/NVA, and for NVD/NVE with or without NVA/NVI: BAIDYA fitz 286 (Q14b, Q16); NAMRATA fitz 238–239; ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q40).
  - Not prophylactic (CVOS): BAIDYA fitz 286–287 (Q16, Q19); NAMRATA fitz 239, 240 (Q.8); ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q38 ii, Q41).
  - Exception, ischaemic CRVO where follow-up is not possible: BAIDYA fitz 287; NAMRATA fitz 239; ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q41).
  - Anti-VEGF gives rapid regression but is a "temporizing adjunctive measure", with PRP as definitive treatment: NAMRATA fitz 239. Anti-VEGF cannot eliminate new vessels permanently: BAIDYA fitz 286 (Q15).
- **IOP and follow-up**
  - Topical or systemic antiglaucoma medication; trabeculectomy or drainage valve if medication fails: BAIDYA fitz 286 (Q14c).
  - CVOS follow-up: monthly for the first **6 months**, then tapered: NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.8).
  - **"Undilated gonioscopy each visit" is my synthesis**, not a sentence in the packet. It rests on:
    - BAIDYA fitz 283 (Q13a): undilated slit-lamp examination for NVA and NVI; the fellow eye "in every follow-up"
    - ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q38 ii): CVOS waited for 2 clock hours of NVI on undilated gonioscopy before PRP
    - NAMRATA fitz 236: undilated gonioscopy is essential
  - "See cards G1 and G5": G1 = POAG (CARDS_GLAUCOMA rule); G5 = neovascular glaucoma (sibling draft header).

### Viva
1. Why glaucoma predisposes:
   - CRV pressure must stay above IOP; raised IOP gives stasis (Virchow's triad); lamina stretched and bowed; nasalisation and compression of vessels: ARAVIND fitz 382 (Q13 i–iii)
   - same mechanism: BAIDYA fitz 287 (Q17); NAMRATA fitz 239 (Q.2)
   - backward bowing of the lamina in cupping: ARAVIND fitz 241 (4.8 Q22)
2. Every follow-up:
   - vision, pupil, IOP, undilated NVA/NVI, NVD, NVE, macular oedema, fellow eye: BAIDYA fitz 283 (Q13a)
   - RAPD detects conversion to ischaemic: ARAVIND fitz 384 (Q26 iii)
   - OCT to monitor oedema: BAIDYA fitz 285
3. RAPD vs ERG:
   - RAPD limitations: ARAVIND fitz 384 (Q26)
   - ERG: b-wave reduction, **80%** sensitivity and specificity; needs no normal fellow eye; usable with optic nerve and pupil abnormalities: ARAVIND fitz 385–386 (Q35)
4. Non-ischaemic: BAIDYA fitz 281–282 (table); FFA < 10 disc areas (as above).
5. Prophylactic PRP: no significant difference in NVI/NVA development; regression **56%** in untreated vs **22%** in prophylactically treated eyes: BAIDYA fitz 287 (Q19).
6. NVI more than NVD/NVE:
   - retina too ischaemic, anterior segment capillaries viable: BAIDYA fitz 283 (Q12)
   - NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.15): the retina cannot respond to the VEGF load
7. Shunt vs NVD:
   - shunt carries retinal to choroidal blood, a sign of good compensatory circulation: BAIDYA fitz 287 (Q18)
   - clue to an old RVO; NVD are smaller in calibre and branch into a network: NAMRATA fitz 237
   - collaterals on the disc are the "footprint" of non-ischaemic CRVO: ARAVIND fitz 384 (Q28)
8. Ischaemic index = non-perfused area ÷ total retinal area; 50% ≈ 10 disc areas is the threshold for significant neovascular risk: NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.6); ARAVIND fitz 385 (Q31).
9. Commonest cause of visual loss, macular oedema: ARAVIND fitz 383 (Q17).
10. Commonest field defect, central scotoma: ARAVIND fitz 383 (Q23).
11. Worse on waking, nocturnal hypotension turning partial into complete thrombosis: ARAVIND fitz 383 (Q18).
12. Fellow-eye gonioscopy, occludable angle (occlusion may be secondary to angle-closure glaucoma): NAMRATA fitz 237.

## Disagreements
- **Proportion non-ischaemic.** BAIDYA fitz 282 says about 75%. NAMRATA fitz 235 says 80% (ischaemic 20%). Card: **75%** (Baidya).
- **Conversion to ischaemic.** BAIDYA fitz 282 says 34% at 3 years. NAMRATA fitz 240 (Q.4) says about one-third within one year. Card: **34% by 3 years** (Baidya).
- **Vision cut-off for ischaemic.**
  - BAIDYA fitz 281: "CF or worse" for ischaemic; "moderate to severe impairment" for non-ischaemic.
  - NAMRATA fitz 235: < 6/60 vs > 6/60.
  - ARAVIND fitz 384: worse than 6/60 (3/60–HM) vs better than 6/60.
  - Card: **counting fingers or worse** (Baidya). The example patient (6/36) is non-ischaemic by all three.
  - A viva answer of "6/60" will also be accepted from Namrata and Aravind. Worth knowing both.
- **Neovascular risk window.**
  - BAIDYA fitz 282: greatest in the first 6 months.
  - ARAVIND fitz 381 (Q6): first 7 months, may span up to 2 years.
  - NAMRATA fitz 240: NVI usually at 2–4 months.
  - Card: "usually 2–4 months" (Namrata; no conflict with Baidya) and "within 6 months" (Baidya).
- **Unit for the non-perfusion cut-off.** BAIDYA and ARAVIND write "10 DD" (disc diameters). NAMRATA fitz 238 says "disc areas" and calls it the CVOS definition. BAIDYA fitz 284 itself also says "10 to 30 disc areas". Card: **disc areas**. The number (10) agrees in all three.
- **Neovascularisation in non-ischaemic CRVO.** BAIDYA is inconsistent with itself. Fitz 282 (table and Q8) says 10%; fitz 283 (Q8) says "neovascular complications occurred in 1.3%". Neither figure is on the card. The row was cut for length.
- **Prophylactic PRP exceptions.**
  - BAIDYA fitz 287: ischaemic CRVO when frequent follow-up is impossible, **or** with risk factors (extensive haemorrhage or non-perfusion, male sex, short duration).
  - NAMRATA fitz 239: ischaemic CRVO where follow-up is not possible, in high-risk cases.
  - ARAVIND fitz 387: ischaemic where regular follow-up is not possible.
  - Card: the common core only ("ischaemic with follow-up impossible"). Baidya's risk-factor alternative was dropped for length.
- **"Significant" anterior-segment new vessels.** NAMRATA fitz 240: NVI over 2 clock hours **or** any NVA. ARAVIND fitz 387: "at least 2 clock hours" of NVI (Q38) and "more than 2 clock hour NVI and/or NVA" (Q39). Card: Namrata's wording.
- **Why NVI rather than NVD/NVE.** BAIDYA fitz 283: the retina is too ischaemic to *secrete* angiogenic factors. NAMRATA fitz 240: the retina cannot *respond* to the VEGF load. Card uses neutral wording ("too ischaemic to form new vessels").
- **Focal or grid laser for macular oedema.** ARAVIND fitz 387 (Q40) allows focal laser if the oedema persists 3–6 months with VA below 6/12 and perfused capillaries. ARAVIND Q42 and NAMRATA fitz 238 say grid is not useful or not routinely recommended (CVOS). Card: no visual benefit from grid in central occlusion. The focal-laser line is left off.
- **ERG criteria.** BAIDYA: b-to-a ratio < 1. NAMRATA: b-wave < 60%, reduced b:a, prolonged implicit time. ARAVIND: b-wave < 60%, or 1 SD or more below the mean. These are complementary, not conflicting. Card gives both numbers.

## Format decisions
- **Focused examination** follows the brief's order: undilated checks first (RAPD → NVI → IOP → gonioscopy → lens), then the department fundus order (red glow → media → disc → vessels → background → macula → periphery → 90 D), then the drawing line.
  - Vision with pinhole and NCT are in the say-it, which follows the department write-up order, not in the numbered steps. They are recorded, not demonstrated.
- **The say-it ends with the diagnosis**, as the brief asks. The plan is not spoken there. It sits in Must know (investigations, macular oedema, new vessels, IOP and follow-up). A separate "diagnosis, then plan" step was cut for length.
- **"Clinically non-ischaemic"** rather than "non-ischaemic", because the CVOS types are defined on angiography.
- **Haemorrhages in the say-it are described first and interpreted second** ("red flame-shaped lesions … suggestive of superficial and deep retinal haemorrhages"). This follows the department's and Aravind's "describe, then 'suggestive of'" style.
- **Drawing line** extends the brief's four colours with the department's yellow for sclerosed vessels and green for ERM (p.18 RVO sheet). Sheets differ on fundus outline colour, so it is not specified.
- **Abbreviations** are expanded at first use; there are none in the say-it. "Mr X" is the spec's patient form. "90 D" is explained once as "(dioptre)".
- **Viva is at the minimum of 12** to fit the word budget, and includes the two required questions. "How does CRVO cause glaucoma?" was taken out of the viva because Must know already covers it.
