## Claims ledger

### Definition, epidemiology, genetics, systemic
- PXF definition (age-related systemic syndrome; fibrillary white flaky material, mainly on lens capsule, ciliary body, zonules, corneal endothelium, iris, pupillary margin; secondary glaucoma) — BAIDYA fitz 182 (Q1)
- "PEX" as an alternative abbreviation — BAIDYA fitz 182 (Q1: "PXF or PEX")
- Glaucoma capsulare = glaucoma in an eye with PXF; usually unilateral — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q5)
- PXG worse than POAG: high IOP at onset, faster progression, poor response to medical therapy — NAMRATA fitz 221 (introduction)
- 40% develop glaucoma or ocular hypertension — NAMRATA fitz 222; ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q9: "glaucoma … in 40%")
- PXG in up to 50% of eyes with PXF — NAMRATA fitz 221
- Incidence 1.5–27% — BAIDYA fitz 182 (Q3); NAMRATA fitz 221
- Starts after 50, mostly in the 7th decade — BAIDYA fitz 181 (see Disagreements)
- Incidence rises with age — NAMRATA fitz 221; ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q7)
- Men and women equally — BAIDYA fitz 181; NAMRATA fitz 225 (Table 3 "Men and women") (see Disagreements)
- Commonest in Scandinavians — BAIDYA fitz 181, 183; NAMRATA fitz 221, 225; ARAVIND fitz 259
- Mostly bilateral (unilateral cases also occur) — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q7)
- Risk factors: age above 70, Scandinavian, genetic predisposition, high blood homocysteine — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q7)
- Genetic predisposition often present (family history) — BAIDYA fitz 181
- LOXL1 gene, exon 1 polymorphism — ARAVIND fitz 264 (Q27; printed "loxl 1")
- Systemic: myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular events, systemic hypertension (deposits in heart, blood vessels, lung, kidney); fibrillary deposits in skin — BAIDYA fitz 181
- Aortic aneurysm (abnormal basement membrane) — NAMRATA fitz 225 (Table 3)
- Systemic deposition sites: lungs, heart, liver, kidneys, skin, gall bladder, cerebral meninges — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q21)

### True exfoliation
- Lamellar delamination of the lens capsule; heat from glass blowing or infrared radiation — BAIDYA fitz 182 (Q2)
- Superficial layers split off as scrolls; may float in the AC as thin, clear membranes; glassblower's cataract — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q1)
- Infrared risk factor; protective goggles — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q2)
- Other causes of delamination: trauma, intraocular inflammation, idiopathic with advanced age — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q3)
- Glaucoma infrequent with true exfoliation — NAMRATA fitz 223

### History and general examination
- Mostly asymptomatic, found on routine examination, even with high IOP; a few present with sudden unilateral pain and redness from an acute IOP rise — BAIDYA fitz 181; NAMRATA fitz 221
- Gradually diminishing distance vision (chief complaint wording) — NAMRATA fitz 221
- Monotherapy usually insufficient (row "One drug is usually not enough") — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Angle closure in PXF from the lens moving forward on weak zonules — ARAVIND fitz 262 (Q18 B, Q19); NAMRATA fitz 221 (anterior displacement of the lens, intermittent pupillary block)
- Uveitic glaucoma excluded by absent AC reaction and other uveitis signs — NAMRATA fitz 223; uveitic glaucoma in the DD — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q8)
- Iris transillumination defects from other causes: trauma, post-herpes — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q8)
- PDS related to myopia — NAMRATA fitz 225 (Table 3)
- Bronchial asthma: contraindication for beta-blockers; note steroid use — TYPED FORMAT fitz 0
- Sulpha / other drug allergy, steroid use as the house checklist — CARD_SPEC §5 (department sheet, not a packet fact)
- Late IOL decentration after cataract surgery (row "Cataract, laser or glaucoma surgery") — NAMRATA fitz 223 (Q2)

### PXG vs POAG table (NAMRATA fitz 224, Table 2, unless stated)
- Age at onset 40–50 years (POAG) vs > 60 years (PXG)
- Laterality bilateral vs usually unilateral
- IOP > 21 mm Hg vs > 40 mm Hg
- Greater diurnal fluctuation; IOP higher and harder to control — ARAVIND fitz 261 (Q17 i, ii)
- Disc: focal or diffuse RNFL defect (early/late) vs usually diffuse RNFL defect; diffuse rim damage, sectoral in POAG — ARAVIND fitz 261 (Q17 iii)
- More glaucomatous optic atrophy — ARAVIND fitz 261 (Q17 iv)
- Severity less vs more; response good vs poor; surgery may be required vs often required; "faster" — NAMRATA fitz 221 (introduction)
- Gonioscopy row (open angle vs blotchy pigment with Sampaolesi's line) is in Table 2 but was cut from the card table for space; the same facts appear elsewhere on the card

### PXF vs pigment dispersion table (NAMRATA fitz 225, Table 3, unless stated)
- PDS: 30–50 years; men; related to myopia; white race
- PXF: "60 years" (the comparison sign did not print, see Disagreements; card: "over 60"); men and women; aortic aneurysm; Scandinavian countries
- PDS signs: Krukenberg's spindle; deep AC with posterior iris bowing (reverse pupillary block); iris atrophy in the periphery; pigment on the lens (Zentmayer's line)
- PXF signs: white powdery deposits on the pupillary margin and lens capsule (target sign) with weak zonules; subluxated or dislocated lens; poorly dilating pupil; iris atrophy at the edge of the pupil margin
- PDS gonioscopy: heavily pigmented angle; PXF: marked blotchy pigmentation, Sampaolesi's line characteristic, pseudoexfoliative material
- PDS: "prominent uniform dark pigmentation band"; young; usually bilateral; mid-peripheral transillumination defects — NAMRATA fitz 223 (DD text)

### Differential diagnosis table
- PDS / pigmentary glaucoma, true exfoliation, primary amyloidosis, POAG, PACG, uveitic glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q8); NAMRATA fitz 223
- Primary amyloidosis: generalised systemic disorder, bilateral, fine whitish powdery deposits throughout the eye, glaucoma — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Uveitis, capsular delamination, primary amyloidosis, PDS, melanosis/melanoma — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q22)

### Classification
- Clinical classification by anterior lens capsule morphology: preclinical (clinically invisible), suspected PXF (precapsular layer), mini PXF (focal defect starts superonasally), classic PXF — ARAVIND fitz 261 (Q14)
- "Classic PXF → the three zones" is my link between Q14 and Q10 E; all three books describe PXF on the capsule as the three-zone pattern (BAIDYA fitz 181; NAMRATA fitz 222; ARAVIND fitz 260)

### Pathogenesis
- Fibrillin, α-elastin, laminin, coated with glycosaminoglycan hyaluronic acid; abnormal extracellular matrix in basement membranes — BAIDYA fitz 182 (Q4)
- Grey-white fibrillogranular extracellular matrix — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q4)
- Produced by abnormal basement membranes of ageing epithelial cells — ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q6)
- Open-angle mechanisms: active production in meshwork, Schlemm's canal, collector channels; passive deposition; swollen juxtacanalicular meshwork; narrowed Schlemm's canal; liberated iris pigment; trabecular cell dysfunction — ARAVIND fitz 262 (Q18 A)
- Clogging of an open angle by PXF material — BAIDYA fitz 182
- Angle-closure mechanisms: zonular weakness with the lens moving forward; lens thickening from cataract; iris–lens adhesiveness — ARAVIND fitz 262 (Q18 B, Q19)
- Zonules: material invades zonules, especially at the non-pigmented ciliary epithelium and pre-equatorial lens; proteolytic enzymes — ARAVIND fitz 260 (Q11); direct damage, accumulation at the zonular origin on the pre-equatorial lens — NAMRATA fitz 224 (Q4); PXF zonules are modified fibres — NAMRATA fitz 225 (Q4, continued)
- Sphincter atrophy gives poor mydriasis and transillumination defects — NAMRATA fitz 221–222; ARAVIND fitz 260 (Q10 D)
- Pigment from iris pigment epithelium rubbing on PXF material on the capsule — NAMRATA fitz 221

### Investigations
- Diurnal variation of IOP; baseline perimetry; RNFL imaging; specular (reduced endothelial count); A-scan biometry and keratometry; B-scan for hazy media — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Specular: decreased density, altered size and shape — ARAVIND fitz 260 (Q13); polymegathism — ARAVIND fitz 260 (Q10 A)
- UBM to confirm the extent of zonular dialysis — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q25)
- Diurnal variation used when setting the target pressure — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q23 iii)

### Management
- Medical as for POAG: beta-blockers, alpha-adrenergic agonists, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, prostaglandin analogues; monotherapy usually insufficient, so combine — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Responds less well to drugs; treat aggressively with frequent follow-up — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q23 i, ii)
- ALT/SLT: successful and well established for PXG with open angles — NAMRATA fitz 223; higher success than in POAG but shorter duration — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q23 iv)
- Laser trabeculoplasty or trabeculectomy if medical treatment fails — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q9)
- Trabeculectomy when medical/laser treatment fails to reach target IOP, or on progression — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Combined trabeculectomy and cataract surgery for intractable IOP with cataract — NAMRATA fitz 223
- Trabecular aspiration removes intertrabecular and pretrabecular debris — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q24)
- Lower homocysteine if high; antioxidants — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q9)
- Follow-up yearly without glaucoma, quarterly (3-monthly) with glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 183 (Q9)
- Pre-operative check: endothelial compromise, zonular dialysis (phacodonesis, asymmetric AC depth, UBM), poor dilatation, posterior synechiae, subluxation — ARAVIND fitz 263 (Q25)
- Precautions table: atropine; stretch the pupil; iris hooks; pupil expansion rings; Healon 5 (visco-adaptive) or Viscoat (visco-dispersive); soft-shell technique; chopping, or prolapsing the nucleus out of the bag; traditional extracapsular surgery; CTR; 3-piece IOL; aggressive, longer postoperative steroids; postoperative Diamox; Nd:YAG relaxing incisions in the anterior capsule at the 4 cardinal positions — NAMRATA fitz 224 (Table 1)
- Chondroitin sulphate viscoelastic; Kuglen hooks, iris hooks, mini-sphincterotomies, sector iridectomy; heparin-surface-modified IOL; CTR to manage zonular dialysis and stabilise the bag — ARAVIND fitz 264 (Q26)

### Complications
- Open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma, acute pressure rises; phacodonesis, subluxation/dislocation, nuclear cataract, zonular dialysis; posterior synechiae, uveitis, sphincter degeneration, melanin dispersion, poor mydriasis; early endothelial decompensation; CRVO, retinal detachment — ARAVIND fitz 261 (Q16)
- Poor dilatation, zonular dialysis, phacodonesis, corneal decompensation, secondary glaucoma, postoperative glaucoma, uveitis — BAIDYA fitz 182 (Q5)
- Cataract surgery: poor dilatation; zonular dialysis and vitreous loss; IOL dislocation or decentration; corneal decompensation; heightened inflammation; postoperative IOP rise; late IOL decentration and prolapse into the posterior segment — NAMRATA fitz 223 (Q2)

## Disagreements
- **Age**: BAIDYA fitz 181 says it starts after 50 and is mostly in the 7th decade. NAMRATA fitz 221 says it is commonly seen after 40. ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q7) says it is commonest at 60–70. The card uses **after 50, mostly 7th decade** (Baidya). For the PXG and comparison tables the card uses "over 60", from NAMRATA fitz 224 (Table 2, "> 60 years").
- **Table 3 age cell**: NAMRATA fitz 225 prints " 60 years" for pseudoexfoliation. The comparison sign is missing on the printed page too (checked on the rendered page), so it is not just an extraction error. The card reads it as "over 60", which matches Table 2.
- **Sex**: BAIDYA fitz 181 says males and females are equally affected. NAMRATA fitz 221 says a few studies suggest female preponderance but it is debated; its Table 3 says "men and women". The card says **men and women equally** (Baidya).
- **Laterality of the glaucoma**: NAMRATA fitz 224 (Table 2) says PXG is usually unilateral, and ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q5) says glaucoma capsulare is usually unilateral. But ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q8) separates "glaucoma capsulare" (unilateral) from "PXF glaucoma" (tends to be bilateral). The card says PXG is **usually unilateral** (Namrata) and does not use Aravind's capsulare vs PXF-glaucoma distinction. If an examiner asks Q8 word for word, Aravind's answer is: capsulare usually unilateral, PXF glaucoma tends to be bilateral.
- **Where the iris transillumination defects lie**: NAMRATA fitz 221 places them **at the pupillary margin** (from sphincter atrophy, giving the moth-eaten margin), and puts mid-peripheral defects in pigmentary glaucoma (fitz 223). BAIDYA fitz 181 gives no location. ARAVIND fitz 260 (Q10 C ii) says "mid-peripheral iris transillumination defects (moth eaten appearance)" for PXF. The card uses the **pupillary margin** (Namrata).
- **Pattern of angle pigment**: BAIDYA fitz 181 and 183 describe a dark, dense, scalloped band on or anterior to Schwalbe's line (Sampaolesi's line). NAMRATA fitz 224 and 225 say **marked blotchy** pigmentation in PXF and a **uniform** dark band in pigmentary glaucoma (fitz 223). ARAVIND fitz 260 (Q10 B) says "uneven", but ARAVIND fitz 261 (Q15) says the reverse: PXF "uniform pigmentation … doesn't extend up to Schwalbe's line", pigmentary glaucoma "thicker … irregular … extends anterior to Schwalbe's line". I checked Q15 on the rendered page and the columns are printed that way. Aravind therefore contradicts its own Q10 and both newer books. The card uses **blotchy in PXF, uniform band in pigment dispersion, Sampaolesi's line on or anterior to Schwalbe's line** (Baidya and Namrata). This difference is also the reason for the trap bullet.
- **Position of Sampaolesi's line**: BAIDYA fitz 183 says "on Schwalbe's line or anterior to it". NAMRATA fitz 222 and 223 say "along Schwalbe's line". The card says "on or anterior to" (Baidya), which includes Namrata's wording.
- **Cause of true exfoliation**: BAIDYA fitz 182 says heat from glass blowing or infrared radiation, and ARAVIND fitz 259 says infrared (glassblowers). NAMRATA fitz 223 says anterior uveitis or exposure to UV rays. The card uses **heat or infrared** (Baidya). Aravind's "trauma, intraocular inflammation, old age" (Q3) is added as other causes of delamination, which covers Namrata's uveitis. Namrata's UV rays are left off.
- **How many get glaucoma**: NAMRATA fitz 222 says 40% of patients develop glaucoma or ocular hypertension, and ARAVIND fitz 259 (Q9) says glaucoma occurs in 40%. NAMRATA fitz 221 says PXG is seen in up to 50% of eyes. These are not truly in conflict (patients vs eyes; "up to"). The card gives **40%** as the headline and "up to 50% of eyes" in the viva answer.
- **IOP level**: NAMRATA fitz 222 (text) says GAT > 21 mm Hg, with mean IOP higher than POAG. NAMRATA fitz 224 (Table 2) says > 40 mm Hg. The card says "may exceed 40" (exam table) and "often above 40" (PXG vs POAG table and Quick recall). That covers both.
- **Which end of the zonule**: NAMRATA fitz 224 calls the pre-equatorial lens the zonular "origin". ARAVIND fitz 260 calls it the "insertion" and gives the non-pigmented ciliary epithelium as the origin. The card says "their attachments" and names neither end.
- **Laser name**: NAMRATA fitz 223 prints "Argon/selective laser trabeculectomy". This is a misprint: Baidya Q9 says laser trabeculoplasty and Aravind Q23 says ALT/SLT. The card says **trabeculoplasty**.
