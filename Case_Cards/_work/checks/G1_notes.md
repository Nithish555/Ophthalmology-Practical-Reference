### At a glance
- POAG = chronic progressive optic neuropathy, open normal-appearing angle, diminished outflow — NAMRATA fitz 189; BAIDYA fitz 169
- Usually asymptomatic, found on routine examination or follow-up — BAIDYA fitz 166–167; NAMRATA fitz 189–190
- Commonly kept as a long case — NAMRATA fitz 189
- IOP to be recorded with method (GAT), time and CCT — BAIDYA fitz 168; NAMRATA fitz 191

### Summary, diagnosis, differentials
- Department diagnosis wording — proforma PDF p.9
- "Pseudoexfoliation glaucoma / syndrome"; unilateral cupping in PXF — BAIDYA fitz 181; NAMRATA fitz 189; "severe stage" — BAIDYA fitz 178
- OHT: IOP > 21 mm Hg on 2 consecutive readings; normal disc, rim, field; open angle — BAIDYA fitz 169; NAMRATA fitz 192
- NTG: IOP < 22 mm Hg on diurnal curve; more disc haemorrhages, focal notching, PPA — BAIDYA fitz 173–174; ARAVIND fitz 240
- PXF glaucoma: exfoliation material; higher IOP, greater 24-hour fluctuation — NAMRATA fitz 192
- Pigmentary glaucoma: age 20–30; Krukenberg spindle; mid-peripheral transillumination defects; wide-open angle — NAMRATA fitz 192; BAIDYA fitz 169
- Steroid-induced: steroid intake; posterior subcapsular cataract — NAMRATA fitz 192
- Angle recession: trauma; recession on gonioscopy; usually unilateral — NAMRATA fitz 192

### Definition, risk factors, classification, pathogenesis
- Glaucoma definition (irreversible optic nerve damage, corresponding field defects, with or without high IOP) — BAIDYA fitz 169
- Adult onset > 40 years; juvenile 3–40 years — NAMRATA fitz 189
- Normal IOP 10–21 mm Hg — BAIDYA fitz 169
- Risk factors: high IOP, myopia, thin CCT, diastolic perfusion pressure < 55 mm Hg, age (prevalence roughly doubles each decade over 40), Black race, first-degree relatives, no gender predisposition — ARAVIND fitz 239; thin CCT = higher risk of POAG — BAIDYA fitz 168
- Grading of glaucomatous optic nerve head damage (mild: minimal cupping, nasal step or paracentral scotoma; moderate: NRR thinning, arcuate scotoma; severe: marked cupping, defects within central 5°; end-stage: gross cupping, small residual field) — BAIDYA fitz 178
- MD cut-offs −6 dB and −12 dB — BAIDYA fitz 178; ARAVIND fitz 225
- Z values: suspect (low and high risk) 0, early 1, moderate 3, severe 5, end-stage 7 — ARAVIND fitz 24–25 (checked on the page image)
- Maximum outflow resistance at juxtacanalicular meshwork — ARAVIND fitz 240; BAIDYA fitz 170
- Raised IOP → backward bowing of lamina cribrosa → axoplasmic transport blocked → ganglion cell death — NAMRATA fitz 195; apoptosis — ARAVIND fitz 240
- Nocturnal hypotension, vasospasm, blood loss — BAIDYA fitz 172; NAMRATA fitz 195
- Poles have larger laminar pores — ARAVIND fitz 242; BAIDYA fitz 176
- Cupping starts in lower temporal quadrant → upper arcuate scotoma — ARAVIND fitz 241
- Myocilin chromosome 1; optineurin chromosome 10 — ARAVIND fitz 239; NAMRATA fitz 195

### Investigations
- Diurnal variation every 3 hours for 24 hours; difference ≥ 8 mm Hg significant; helps timing of drugs — NAMRATA fitz 192
- Normal diurnal variation 3–6 mm Hg — BAIDYA fitz 170
- Phasing: diagnosis, progression despite good control, efficacy, NTG versus POAG — ARAVIND fitz 239
- Pachymetry corrects IOP — NAMRATA fitz 192; thin < 500 µm, thick > 570 µm — NAMRATA fitz 196; thin reads falsely low, thick falsely high — ARAVIND fitz 243
- Humphrey 24-2 / 30-2; 10-2 when fixation threatened — ARAVIND fitz 224
- Glaucomatous defects follow the horizontal meridian — ARAVIND fitz 222; paracentral, arcuate (Bjerrum), nasal step — ARAVIND fitz 221–222
- OCT, GDx, HRT, SWAP as pre-perimetric tools — NAMRATA fitz 195; ARAVIND fitz 243
- RNFL interocular difference > 9 µm — BAIDYA fitz 210
- Double hump lost; superior and inferior first; 7 o'clock RE, 5 o'clock LE — BAIDYA fitz 212
- Macular GCC; useful in early disease with unreliable fields and in advanced disease (floor effect) — BAIDYA fitz 209

### Investigations
- Diurnal phasing 3-hourly for 24 h; diagnose early cases, pre-treatment baseline, nocturnal rise, timing of drops; ≥ 8 mm Hg significant — NAMRATA fitz 192; keep fluctuation < 5 mm Hg below target — NAMRATA fitz 194
- Diurnal variation: IOP highest in the morning, lower in afternoon and evening, rises through the night; 3–6 mm Hg normal; more in glaucoma — BAIDYA fitz 170; due to cyclic cortisol — ARAVIND fitz 205
- CCT < 555 µm higher risk and > 588 µm lower risk of OHT converting — BAIDYA fitz 175
- Perimetry gold standard — BAIDYA fitz 195; 24-2 (54 points, 6°) suspect and established; 30-2 (76, 6°); 10-2 (68, 2°) advanced — BAIDYA fitz 199; Goldmann size III white stimulus; size V in advanced; background 31.5 asb; pupil > 2.5 mm (dilate if < 2 mm); refractive correction — BAIDYA fitz 198–199; SITA shorter with little loss of accuracy — BAIDYA fitz 199–200
- Reliability: fixation losses < 20%; false positives > 15% unreliable (white scotoma); false negatives > 33% unreliable (clover leaf) — BAIDYA fitz 200 (as card G10)
- Pattern deviation reveals focal defects masked by generalised depression (cataract, small pupil) — BAIDYA fitz 204; GHT 5 sectors mirrored, outside normal limits — BAIDYA fitz 204–205; MD more negative as field worsens; PSD focal loss — BAIDYA fitz 206
- Anderson–Patella: cluster of ≥ 3 contiguous non-edge points on pattern deviation, all p < 5%, one p < 1%; PSD p < 5%; GHT outside normal limits; at least 2 consecutive tests — BAIDYA fitz 206
- Field defect sequence: generalised depression, baring of blind spot, paracentral (most often superonasal), Seidel, arcuate, double arcuate, nasal (Roenne) step, tubular vision with temporal island — BAIDYA fitz 197; ARAVIND fitz 221
- Glaucoma progression analysis (GPA) — BAIDYA fitz 206 (fields), 214 (OCT)
- OCT: RNFL thickness from light reflected between outer edge of RNFL and ILM; circular scan centred on ONH; rim by minimum rim width (ILM to Bruch membrane opening); macula: GC-IPL and GCC (= RNFL + GC + IPL) — BAIDYA fitz 209
- OCT colour codes green 5th–95th percentile, yellow 1st–5th, red < 1st — BAIDYA fitz 210
- Thickness map hourglass; interocular difference > 9 µm or loss of hourglass → rule out glaucoma; thinner RNFL in myopia, old age, small discs — BAIDYA fitz 210
- TSNIT double hump; superior and inferior first; 7 o'clock RE, 5 o'clock LE first — BAIDYA fitz 212
- Machines not interchangeable; Indian average RNFL 102.75 µm — BAIDYA fitz 213 (machine point used; 102.75 not on card)
- Progression: event and trend analysis; age loss about 0.2 µm per year — BAIDYA fitz 214
- Artefacts: averaging, shadow artefacts, red disease (high myope flagged red), green disease (normal average hides asymmetry) — BAIDYA fitz 214
- Floor effect in advanced disease: use macular OCT and 10-2 — BAIDYA fitz 209
- Pre-perimetric tools GDxVCC, HRT, OCT, SWAP (FDP) — NAMRATA fitz 195; ARAVIND fitz 243
- SWAP: blue on yellow; early detection; useful in glaucoma suspects and OHT; no use in moderate–advanced; time-consuming, affected by nuclear sclerosis — BAIDYA fitz 177, 731
- Fundus photography documents ONH — NAMRATA fitz 192
- Systemic diseases (diabetes, hypertension) are considerations for surgery in a glaucoma patient — ARAVIND fitz 25

### Management
- Goal of therapy: target IOP that arrests or prevents ONH damage and field progression — ARAVIND fitz 275
- Target pressure depends on IOP at presentation, severity and extent of damage, rapidity of progression — ARAVIND fitz 240
- Glaucoma suspect: observe if no risk factors; review after 2 months; treat if progression documented — ARAVIND fitz 24
- Prostaglandin first; else beta-blocker or alpha agonist — ARAVIND fitz 25
- Medical therapy: less effective than surgery; costly; effect wanes; prolonged use affects surgical success; compliance difficult — ARAVIND fitz 275
- PG mechanism: increased uveoscleral outflow by ciliary muscle relaxation and remodelling of its extracellular matrix — ARAVIND fitz 281; NAMRATA fitz 193
- PG adverse effects: eyelid skin pigmentation, hypertrichosis, conjunctival hyperaemia, reactivation of dendritic keratitis, iris pigmentation, uveitis, CME; no systemic effect — ARAVIND fitz 281
- Miotics and PG contraindicated in uveitis (blood–aqueous barrier breakdown) — ARAVIND fitz 285
- Beta-blocker mechanism: inhibits cAMP in ciliary epithelium, aqueous production down 20–50%, IOP down 20–30% — ARAVIND fitz 277
- Beta-blocker ocular: allergy, punctate epithelial erosions, reduced tear secretion; systemic: bradycardia, hypotension, heart failure, bronchospasm, sleep disorder, depression — ARAVIND fitz 277
- Timolol more effective in the morning (aqueous production falls at night) — ARAVIND fitz 277; morning dose blunts early-morning rise and avoids hypotension in sleep — ARAVIND fitz 284
- Brimonidine mechanism: decreases aqueous, increases uveoscleral outflow, neuroprotection — ARAVIND fitz 278; 0.2% tartrate, 0.15% purite — ARAVIND fitz 278; 0.1/0.15% three times, sometimes twice — NAMRATA fitz 193; CNS effects and respiratory arrest in young children, contraindicated < 2 years — NAMRATA fitz 193; crosses blood–brain barrier — ARAVIND fitz 278
- CAI mechanism: inhibits carbonic anhydrase II in ciliary epithelium — ARAVIND fitz 279; dorzolamide 2% three times, brinzolamide 1% twice — ARAVIND fitz 280; topical: burning, stinging, SPK; oral: paraesthesia, metabolic acidosis, potassium loss, renal stones, aplastic anaemia — ARAVIND fitz 280; BAIDYA fitz 172
- Pilocarpine mechanism: contracts longitudinal ciliary muscle, pulls scleral spur, opens TM — ARAVIND fitz 276; adverse: browache, dim vision in low light, induced myopia, retinal holes and detachment — ARAVIND fitz 276
- PG + pilocarpine not combined: pilocarpine contracts ciliary muscle and reduces uveoscleral outflow — ARAVIND fitz 284
- Fixed combinations: advantages simple dosing, adherence, lower IOP, less preservative and systemic toxicity, economical; disadvantages: dosing time conflict (timolol morning, PG bedtime), altered potency, exposure to an unneeded drug — ARAVIND fitz 284–285; two separate drugs: non-adherence, washout, preservative toxicity, cost — ARAVIND fitz 283
- Timolol + dorzolamide; PG + timolol (13–37%) — ARAVIND fitz 284
- NTG: perimetry 4–6-monthly; IOP lowering including betaxolol (optic nerve blood flow); treat systemic disorders — BAIDYA fitz 175; 30% reduction — NAMRATA fitz 195
- Laser trabeculoplasty mechanism: TM shrinkage stretches adjacent areas; IL-1β and TNF-α → matrix metalloproteinases — BAIDYA fitz 180, 456; SLT targets melanin of pigmented TM cells without thermal transfer; macrophages, cytokines, MMP — ARAVIND fitz 297
- SLT: frequency-doubled Q-switched Nd:YAG 532 nm, 400 µm, 3 ns, 0.5–1.5 mJ, SLT lens or Goldmann 3-mirror; "safe and effective" — BAIDYA fitz 456; 50–100 spots over 180° — ARAVIND fitz 258
- LTP complications: transient IOP rise, low-grade uveitis, pigment dispersion, hyphaema, rarely persistent rise needing surgery — BAIDYA fitz 456
- ALT: about 30% IOP fall; about 50% controlled at 5 years; up to 3 times more encapsulated blebs at later surgery — ARAVIND fitz 296
- Trabeculectomy principle: fistula at the limbus under a partial-thickness scleral flap to the subconjunctival space — ARAVIND fitz 301–302; partial thickness gives more uniform IOP control, less hypotony, hyphaema, shallow AC and infection — ARAVIND fitz 301
- Trabeculectomy steps: bridle suture; limbus- or fornix-based conjunctival flap; scleral flap 3 mm × 4 mm, 2/3 thickness; paracentesis; enter AC behind the flap hinge; Kelly punch; ostium 1.5 × 2 mm; peripheral iridectomy; 10-0 nylon flap sutures — ARAVIND fitz 303
- Trabeculectomy complications (early: flat AC with hypotony, hyphaema, failing bleb, loss of central vision; late: bleb infection, endophthalmitis, cataract, leaking bleb, Tenon's cyst, failure) — ARAVIND fitz 311–312
- PI with trabeculectomy: prevent iris blocking the ostium; prevent pupillary block — BAIDYA fitz 178; ARAVIND fitz 305; PI bigger than the internal ostium — BAIDYA fitz 178
- MMC indications and complications — BAIDYA fitz 179 (list as in v2 ledger plus the complication list: cystic overfiltering bleb commonest, late leak, corneal decompensation, scleral or corneal melt, hypotony, endophthalmitis, cataract)
- Trabeculectomy site superotemporal or superonasal: keeps other sites for repeat surgery; anterior ciliary vessel bleeding at 12 o'clock — BAIDYA fitz 179
- GDD indications: failed filtering surgery with antimetabolites, scarred conjunctiva — NAMRATA fitz 194; BAIDYA fitz 179; refractory glaucomas — ARAVIND fitz 320
- GDD steps: plate at the equator in the superotemporal quadrant, 8–10 mm behind the limbus; tube 1.5–2 mm into AC parallel to iris through a 23-gauge needle tract; scleral patch graft over the tube; non-valved tube tied 2–3 weeks — ARAVIND fitz 322–325
- GDD complications: failure, corneal decompensation, diplopia, hypotony, hyphaema, tube erosion, endophthalmitis, plate migration — BAIDYA fitz 179
- Combined cataract and filtering surgery: indications, advantages (single procedure, early and long-term IOP control, fewer drugs, antimetabolite possible), disadvantages (more complications, slower recovery, more care, less IOP control than filtering alone, drops may still be needed) — ARAVIND fitz 25–26
- Cataract alone: advantages single procedure, easier, shorter, fewer complications, temporal clear corneal approach preserves conjunctiva — ARAVIND fitz 25
- Approach (identify risk factors, document disc, confirm field, educate, single drug with fewer side effects, ensure compliance, switch rather than add, filtering surgery if maximal tolerated therapy fails or non-compliant) — ARAVIND fitz 244
